# Groups — Exercises

> [Groups](README.md) · 38 problems · Beginner → Challenge · Solutions: [solutions.md](solutions.md)

Attempt every problem in writing before looking at [solutions.md](solutions.md). Each problem names the section of the tutorial that supplies the technique. Problems marked *(proof)* require a complete argument, with the hypotheses stated and every step justified.

Notation: $e$ is the identity of a multiplicative group and $0$ that of an additive group; $|G|$ is the order of $G$ and $\operatorname{ord}(a)$ the order of the element $a$; $H \le G$ means that $H$ is a subgroup and $H \trianglelefteq G$ that it is normal; $S_n$ is the symmetric group and $A_n$ the alternating group; $\varphi$ is Euler's totient; $D_n$ is the symmetry group of the regular $n$-gon, of order $2n$; $V_4 = \{e, (1\,2)(3\,4), (1\,3)(2\,4), (1\,4)(2\,3)\}$; $Q_8 = \{\pm1, \pm i, \pm j, \pm k\}$ with $i^2=j^2=k^2=-1$ and $ij=k$. Products of permutations are composed right to left.

## Beginner

**1.** *(§5)* Decide which of the following are groups under the stated operation. For each that is not, name every axiom that fails.
(a) $(\mathbb{Z}, +)$; (b) $(\mathbb{Z}, \cdot)$; (c) $(\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}, \cdot)$; (d) $(\{1,-1,i,-i\}, \cdot)$; (e) $(\mathbb{Z}, -)$; (f) $(M_2(\mathbb{R}), \cdot)$; (g) $((0,\infty), \cdot)$.

**2.** *(§5)* Let $G = \mathbb{R}\setminus\{-1\}$ with $a \ast b = a + b + ab$. Prove that $(G,\ast)$ is an abelian group, stating the identity and the inverse of $a$. Then observe that $a \mapsto 1+a$ is an isomorphism onto $(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\cdot)$.

**3.** *(§5, §7.1)* *(proof)* Prove that $(ab)^{-1} = b^{-1}a^{-1}$ and, more generally, that $(a_1a_2\cdots a_n)^{-1} = a_n^{-1}\cdots a_1^{-1}$ for $n \ge 1$. Then compute $\big((1\,2\,3)(4\,5)\big)^{-1}$ in $S_5$.

**4.** *(§7.1)* *(proof)* Suppose $a^2 = e$ for every $a \in G$. Prove that $G$ is abelian.

**5.** *(§7.2)* Compute the order of every element of $(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z},+)$ and of the multiplicative group $(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})^\times$. What group is $(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})^\times$ isomorphic to?

**6.** *(§7.2)* *(proof)* Prove that $\operatorname{ord}(a) = \operatorname{ord}(a^{-1})$ and that $\operatorname{ord}(ab) = \operatorname{ord}(ba)$ for all $a,b \in G$. Then give an example in which $\operatorname{ord}(ab) \ne \operatorname{ord}(a)\operatorname{ord}(b)$.

**7.** *(§7.3)* *(proof)* Prove that $S^1 = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1\}$ and $\mu_n = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : z^n = 1\}$ are subgroups of $(\mathbb{C}^\times, \cdot)$.

**8.** *(§7.3, §7.4)* List all subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$ and draw their inclusion diagram.

**9.** *(§7.4)* Show that $(\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z})^\times$ is cyclic by exhibiting a generator and listing its powers. Find every generator of $\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$.

**10.** *(§7.5)* In $G = \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$ and $H = \langle \bar 4\rangle$, list all left cosets, compute the index, and verify Lagrange's theorem numerically.

**11.** *(§7.5)* Let $|G| = 60$. Which integers can occur as the order of an element? Which can occur as the order of a subgroup? Explain what Lagrange's theorem does and does not tell you here.

**12.** *(§7.6)* Decide, with proof or a counterexample, whether each subgroup is normal: (a) $\langle(1\,2)\rangle \le S_3$; (b) $\langle(1\,2\,3)\rangle \le S_3$; (c) $\langle r\rangle \le D_4$; (d) $\langle s\rangle \le D_4$; (e) $3\mathbb{Z} \le \mathbb{Z}$.

## Intermediate

**13.** *(§5.8)* *(proof)* Prove the right-handed analogue of Theorem 5.8.1: a set with an associative operation, a right identity $e$ (so $ae = a$ for all $a$) and right inverses (for each $a$ there is $b$ with $ab = e$) is a group.

**14.** *(§7.2)* *(proof)* Let $a,b \in G$ commute, with $\operatorname{ord}(a) = m$, $\operatorname{ord}(b) = n$ and $\gcd(m,n) = 1$. Prove that $\operatorname{ord}(ab) = mn$.

**15.** *(§7.3)* *(proof)* Let $H, K \le G$. Prove that $HK$ is a subgroup of $G$ if and only if $HK = KH$.

**16.** *(§7.4)* *(proof)* Let $G = \langle a\rangle$ be cyclic of order $n$ and let $k \in \mathbb{Z}$. Prove that $\langle a^k\rangle = \langle a^{\gcd(n,k)}\rangle$, deduce that $G$ has exactly $\varphi(n)$ generators, and compute those generators for $n = 12$.

**17.** *(§7.4)* *(proof)* Prove that $(\mathbb{Q},+)$ is not cyclic and is not generated by any finite set.

**18.** *(§7.5)* *(proof)* Use Lagrange's theorem to prove Fermat's little theorem: for a prime $p$ and an integer $a$ with $p \nmid a$, $a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod p$. Then compute $3^{100} \bmod 101$.

**19.** *(§7.5)* Use Euler's theorem to compute $7^{222} \bmod 100$, showing each step.

**20.** *(§7.5, §7.6)* *(proof)* Prove that a subgroup of index $2$ is normal. Show that $g^2 \in H$ for every $g \in G$ when $[G:H] = 2$. Is it true that every normal subgroup has index $2$? Justify your answer with an example.

**21.** *(§7.6)* Prove that $V_4 \trianglelefteq S_4$ and $V_4 \trianglelefteq A_4$. Show that $S_4/V_4$ has order $6$ and has no element of order $6$, and deduce $S_4/V_4 \cong S_3$.

**22.** *(§7.7)* *(proof)* Let $\varphi : G \to H$ be a homomorphism. Prove that $\varphi(e_G) = e_H$, that $\varphi(a^{-1}) = \varphi(a)^{-1}$, that $\operatorname{im}\varphi \le H$ and that $\ker\varphi \trianglelefteq G$.

**23.** *(§7.7)* Find all group homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ and all group homomorphisms $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$. Explain why the answers differ.

**24.** *(§7.8)* $D_4$ acts on the set of six two-element subsets of the vertex set $\{1,2,3,4\}$. Determine the orbits, the stabiliser of each orbit representative, and verify the orbit–stabiliser theorem.

## Advanced

**25.** *(§7.6, §7.7)* *(proof)* Let $N \trianglelefteq G$ and let $\varphi : G \to H$ be a homomorphism with $N \le \ker\varphi$. Prove that there is a unique homomorphism $\bar\varphi : G/N \to H$ with $\bar\varphi(aN) = \varphi(a)$, that $\ker\bar\varphi = (\ker\varphi)/N$, and that $\bar\varphi$ is injective exactly when $N = \ker\varphi$.

**26.** *(§7.8)* *(proof)* Prove Cayley's theorem: every group $G$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\operatorname{Sym}(G)$, and consequently every finite group of order $n$ embeds in $S_n$. Identify the images of $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$ in $S_3$ and of $V_4$ in $S_4$.

**27.** *(§7.9)* *(proof)* Let $N(\sigma)$ be the number of inversions of $\sigma \in S_n$. Prove that $N(\sigma\tau) \equiv N(\sigma) + N(\tau) \pmod 2$, deduce $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = (-1)^{N(\sigma)}$, and derive $\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma) = (-1)^{n - c(\sigma)}$. Compute the sign of $(1\,4\,2)(3\,5)$ in $S_5$.

**28.** *(§7.9)* *(proof)* Prove that $A_n$ is generated by $3$-cycles for $n \ge 3$. For $n = 4$, list the eight $3$-cycles, the four subgroups of order $3$, and show that none of these subgroups is normal in $A_4$.

**29.** *(§7.8)* In how many ways can the four faces of a regular tetrahedron be coloured with three colours, if colourings related by a rotation of the tetrahedron are considered the same? Justify the rotation count and use Burnside's lemma.

**30.** *(§7.10)* *(proof)* Prove that $\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/mn\mathbb{Z}$ if and only if $\gcd(m,n) = 1$. Show that $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \not\cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$, and that $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \cong V_4$.

**31.** *(§7.11)* *(proof)* Classify all groups of order $10$ up to isomorphism, with complete proofs.

**32.** *(§7.11)* *(proof)* Use the class equation to prove that every group of order $p^2$ ($p$ prime) is abelian, and then classify the groups of order $9$.

## Challenge

**33.** *(§7.11)* *(proof)* Classify all groups of order $8$: show that there are exactly five, namely $\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}$, $(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^3$, $D_4$ and $Q_8$, and that no two of them are isomorphic.

**34.** *(§7.8, §7.5)* *(proof)* A necklace has $p$ beads, where $p$ is prime, each coloured with one of $k$ colours; two necklaces are the same if one is a rotation of the other. Use Burnside's lemma to prove that the number of necklaces is
$$\frac{k^p + (p-1)k}{p},$$
deduce Fermat's little theorem from the fact that this number is an integer, and evaluate the formula for $p = 5$, $k = 2$.

**35.** *(§7.8, §7.9)* *(proof)* Prove that if $|G| = 2n$ with $n$ odd, then $G$ has a normal subgroup of order $n$. (Use Cauchy's theorem, the left regular action, and the sign homomorphism.)

**36.** *(§7.7, §7.8)* *(proof)* Let $H \le G$ with $[G:H] = n$ finite. Show that $G$ acts on the cosets $G/H$, that the kernel $K$ of the action is a normal subgroup contained in $H$, and that $K = \bigcap_{g\in G} gHg^{-1}$. Deduce that if $p$ is the smallest prime dividing $|G|$ and $[G:H] = p$, then $H \trianglelefteq G$. Deduce also that a simple group with a subgroup of index $n \ge 2$ has order dividing $n!$.

**37.** *(§5.6, §7.11)* *(proof)* Realise $Q_8$ as a subgroup of $GL_2(\mathbb{C})$ using the matrices displayed in Section 5.6. Prove that $Q_8$ has exactly one element of order $2$, that every subgroup of $Q_8$ is normal, and that $Q_8 \not\cong D_4$.

**38.** *(§7.8)* *(proof)* Let $G$ be a finite group acting on its set of subgroups by conjugation. Prove that the normaliser $N_G(H) = \{g \in G : gHg^{-1} = H\}$ is a subgroup, that the number of conjugates of $H$ is $[G : N_G(H)]$, and compute this number for each subgroup of $S_3$.
