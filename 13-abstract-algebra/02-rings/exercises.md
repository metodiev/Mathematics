# Rings — Exercises

> [Rings](README.md) · 36 problems · Beginner → Challenge · Solutions: [solutions.md](solutions.md)

Attempt every problem in writing before looking at [solutions.md](solutions.md). Each problem names the section of the tutorial that supplies the technique. Problems marked *(proof)* require a complete argument, with the hypotheses stated and every step justified.

Notation: all rings have $1 \ne 0$ unless stated otherwise; $R^\times$ is the group of units, $\operatorname{char} R$ the characteristic, $(S)$ the ideal generated by $S$, and $N$ the norm on $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{d}]$ or $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. A "domain" is a commutative ring with $1 \ne 0$ and no zero divisors. For $f \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, $c(f)$ is the content and $f$ is primitive when $c(f) = 1$.

## Beginner

**1.** *(§5.1)* Decide which of the following are rings in the sense of Definition 5.1.1. For each ring, say whether it is commutative, whether it is a domain and whether it is a field; for each non-ring, name every axiom that fails.
(a) $(\mathbb{Z},+,\cdot)$; (b) $(2\mathbb{Z},+,\cdot)$; (c) $(\{0\},+,\cdot)$; (d) the set of odd integers with the usual operations; (e) $(M_2(\mathbb{Z}),+,\cdot)$; (f) $(\mathbb{Z}^{X},+,\cdot)$ with $X = \{1,2\}$ and pointwise operations; (g) $(\mathbb{R},+,\star)$ where $a \star b = \frac{ab}{2}$.

**2.** *(§5.2)* *(proof)* Prove that a ring $R$ is commutative if and only if $(a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2$ for all $a, b \in R$.

**3.** *(§5.2)* Expand $(a+b)^3$ in a commutative ring, stating which hypothesis is used at each step. Then compute $(i+j)(i-j)$ in $\mathbb{H}$ and comment on the result of Exercise 2.

**4.** *(§7.1, §7.2)* For each of $R = \mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/9\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$: list the units, list the zero divisors, decide whether $R$ is a domain and whether it is a field, and determine the isomorphism type of $R^\times$.

**5.** *(§7.1, §7.2)* *(proof)* Let $R$ be a ring, $u \in R^\times$, and let $a \in R$ be a zero divisor.
(a) Prove that $ua$ is a zero divisor. Deduce that no unit is a zero divisor.
(b) Show that the converse fails: exhibit a ring and an element that is neither a unit nor a zero divisor. Is such an example possible in $\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}$ for some $n$?

**6.** *(§7.4)* Compute the characteristic of each ring: $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$; $\mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}[x]$; $M_3(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})$; $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$; $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Q}$; $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(3)$.

**7.** *(§7.5)* Decide, with justification, whether each subset is an ideal of the stated ring.
(a) $6\mathbb{Z} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$;
(b) $\{f \in \mathbb{Z}[x] : f(0) \text{ is even}\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}[x]$;
(c) $\{f \in \mathbb{R}[x] : f(1) = 0\} \subseteq \mathbb{R}[x]$;
(d) the upper triangular matrices in $M_2(\mathbb{R})$;
(e) $\mathbb{Z}[\tfrac12] = \{a/2^k : a \in \mathbb{Z},\ k \ge 0\} \subseteq \mathbb{Q}$;
(f) $\{0, 6, 12\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$.

**8.** *(§7.5)* In $\mathbb{Z}$, compute the ideal sum $(6) + (8)$, the intersection $(6) \cap (8)$ and the product $(6)(8)$, exhibiting an element of $(6) \cap (8)$ that is not in $(6)(8)$. Which general relation do these computations illustrate?

**9.** *(§7.6)* Use the third isomorphism theorem to identify $(\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})\big/(4\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z})$, and verify the result by counting the cosets directly.

**10.** *(§7.11)* Divide $f = 2x^4 - 3x^3 + 4x - 5$ by $g = x^2 - 2$ in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$: find the quotient $q$ and the remainder $r$, and verify by expanding $qg + r$.

**11.** *(§7.12)* Find all rational roots of $2x^3 + 3x^2 - 8x + 3$ using the rational root test, and factor the polynomial completely over $\mathbb{Q}$.

**12.** *(§7.12)* Decide, with proof, whether each polynomial is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$: (a) $x^3+x+1$; (b) $x^2-3x+5$; (c) $3x^2+6x+9$; (d) $x^4+x^2+1$.

## Intermediate

**13.** *(§7.1)* *(proof)* Prove that $R^\times$ is a group under multiplication, and that $(R \times S)^\times \cong R^\times \times S^\times$ for rings $R, S$.

**14.** *(§7.5)* *(proof)* Let $R$ be a ring and $(I_j)_{j \in J}$ a family of ideals.
(a) Prove that $\bigcap_{j \in J} I_j$ is an ideal.
(b) Prove that the sum $I + J = \{i + j : i \in I,\ j \in J\}$ of two ideals is an ideal.
(c) Show that the union $I \cup J$ need not be an ideal, with the example $I = (2)$, $J = (3)$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.

**15.** *(§7.5, §7.8)* *(proof)* Let $\varphi : R \to S$ be a ring homomorphism and let $F$ be a field.
(a) Prove that $\varphi$ is injective if and only if $\ker\varphi = (0)$.
(b) Prove that every unital ring homomorphism $F \to S$ with $S \ne \{0\}$ is injective.
(c) Deduce that the map $\mathbb{Z} \to R$, $n \mapsto n\cdot 1$, is injective if and only if $\operatorname{char} R = 0$.

**16.** *(§7.6)* *(proof)* Prove that a subset $I \subseteq R$ is a two-sided ideal if and only if there exist a ring $S$ and a ring homomorphism $\varphi : R \to S$ with $I = \ker\varphi$.

**17.** *(§7.7)* Use the first isomorphism theorem to exhibit isomorphisms, in each case describing the homomorphism and its kernel:
(a) $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^2+1) \cong \mathbb{Z}[i]$; (b) $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \times \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$; (c) $\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x-3) \cong \mathbb{Z}$.

**18.** *(§7.7)* State the Chinese remainder theorem for ideals $(m), (n)$ of $\mathbb{Z}$ with $\gcd(m,n)=1$, then solve the system
$$x \equiv 2 \pmod 7, \qquad x \equiv 5 \pmod{12},$$
giving the general solution modulo $\operatorname{lcm}(7,12)$, and verify both congruences.

**19.** *(§7.8)* Determine all prime ideals and all maximal ideals of $\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$, and draw the inclusion diagram of all its ideals.

**20.** *(§7.8)* *(proof)* Prove that $(x^2+1)$ is a prime ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ that is not maximal, and exhibit a maximal ideal of $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ containing it.

**21.** *(§7.9)* *(proof)* Prove that $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}] = \{a+b\sqrt{-2} : a,b \in \mathbb{Z}\}$ is a Euclidean domain with $N(a+b\sqrt{-2}) = a^2+2b^2$. (Model the proof on Proposition 7.9.4.)

**22.** *(§7.11)* *(proof)* Let $F$ be a field and let $f \in F[x]$ be nonzero of degree $n$. Prove that $f$ has at most $n$ roots in $F$, counted without multiplicity. Show by example that the statement fails for $f \in \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}[x]$.

## Advanced

**23.** *(§7.9)* *(proof)* In $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ define $N(z) = z\bar z$.
(a) Prove that $N$ is multiplicative and that $N(z) = 1$ if and only if $z$ is a unit.
(b) Deduce $\mathbb{Z}[i]^\times = \{\pm1, \pm i\}$.
(c) Prove that if $z \mid w$ with $w \ne 0$ then $N(z) \le N(w)$, and explain why this guarantees termination of the Euclidean algorithm.

**24.** *(§7.10)* *(proof)* Prove that in a unique factorisation domain every irreducible element is prime.

**25.** *(§7.9, §7.10)* *(proof)* Let $R$ be a PID and $a, b \in R$, not both zero. Prove that the ideal $(a) + (b)$ is generated by a greatest common divisor $d$ of $a$ and $b$, that $d = ax + by$ for some $x, y \in R$, and that $(a) \cap (b)$ is generated by a least common multiple of $a$ and $b$.

**26.** *(§7.11)* *(proof)* Let $k$ be a field. Prove that $(x,y) \subseteq k[x,y]$ is maximal but not principal, and deduce that $k[x,y]$ is not a PID. Show also that $(x)$ is prime but not maximal in $k[x,y]$.

**27.** *(§7.13)* *(proof)* Let $R$ be an integral domain such that $R[x]$ is a unique factorisation domain. Prove that $R$ is a unique factorisation domain.

**28.** *(§7.12)* *(proof)* For a prime $p$, let $\Phi_p(x) = x^{p-1} + x^{p-2} + \cdots + x + 1$. Prove that $\Phi_p$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ by applying Eisenstein's criterion to $\Phi_p(x+1)$ and identifying its coefficients.

**29.** *(§7.6, §7.9, §7.8)* *(proof)* Prove that $\mathbb{Z}[i]/(2+i) \cong \mathbb{F}_5$, by showing that $(2+i)$ is maximal and computing the number of elements of the quotient. Then find an explicit isomorphism.

**30.** *(§7.6, §7.9)* *(proof)* (a) Prove that if $R$ is a PID then every quotient $R/I$ is a ring in which every ideal is principal. (b) Deduce that every ideal of $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is principal, list them, and explain why $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ is neither a domain nor a field.

**31.** *(§7.14)* *(proof)* Verify that $\mathbb{H}$ is a division ring: using $i^2 = j^2 = k^2 = ijk = -1$, derive $ij = k$, $ji = -k$, $jk = i$, $kj = -i$, $ki = j$, $ik = -j$, and $ijk = -1$; then prove that for $z = a+bi+cj+dk \ne 0$,
$$z^{-1} = \frac{\bar z}{z\bar z}, \qquad \bar z = a - bi - cj - dk, \qquad z\bar z = a^2+b^2+c^2+d^2.$$

## Challenge

**32.** *(§7.10, §7.12)* Prove that $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-3}]$ is not a unique factorisation domain, by analysing
$$4 = 2\cdot 2 = (1+\sqrt{-3})(1-\sqrt{-3}).$$
You must prove that all four factors are irreducible, and that the two factorisations are not related by order and units. Explain why the norm argument alone does not settle the question, unlike the case of $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$.

**33.** *(§7.9)* Let $R$ be a Euclidean domain with Euclidean function $N$. Define, for $a \ne 0$,
$$M(a) = \min\{N(ax) : x \in R,\ x \ne 0\}.$$
Prove that $M$ is a Euclidean function on $R$ and that $M(a) \le M(ab)$ for all nonzero $a, b$. (Such a function is called *submultiplicative*.)

**34.** *(§7.12)* Prove that $x^4+1$ is reducible in $\mathbb{F}_p[x]$ for every prime $p$, even though it is irreducible in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ (Example 9.6). You may use the fact that for an odd prime $p$ at least one of $-1$, $2$, $-2$ is a quadratic residue modulo $p$.

**35.** *(§7.8)* *(proof)* Prove Krull's theorem: every proper ideal of a commutative ring $R$ with $1 \ne 0$ is contained in a maximal ideal. (Use Zorn's lemma on the set of proper ideals containing the given one, ordered by inclusion, and show that the union of a chain of proper ideals is proper.)

**36.** *(§7.1, §7.9, §7.11)* *(proof)* Let $R$ be a commutative ring with $1 \ne 0$. Prove that the following are equivalent:
(i) $R$ is a field;
(ii) every nonzero ring homomorphism from $R$ to a nonzero ring is injective;
(iii) $R[x]$ is a Euclidean domain.
