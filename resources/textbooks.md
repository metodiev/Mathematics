# Recommended Textbooks

Part of the [Mathematics](../README.md) repository. The [Roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) lists the topics
each discipline covers; this page is a reading guide for books that can be used alongside those
topics. It is not a syllabus, and inclusion is not a ranking.

The tutorials in this repository are self-contained. A textbook is useful when you want a second
explanation, a larger exercise set, or a published statement of a theorem you can cite. One main
text per subject is usually enough: read it alongside the matching tutorial rather than collecting
several books at once.

Editions matter. Theorem numbering, exercise numbering and even definitions change between
editions, so record the edition and year you used. The citation conventions are in
[References and How to Cite](references.md).

## Level Key

| Level | Assumes | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Introductory** | School algebra and trigonometry; little or no proof experience | A first pass, or self-study with guidance |
| **Standard** | Roughly one semester of university mathematics, or equivalent maturity | A standard undergraduate course |
| **Advanced** | Comfort with proofs, abstraction and careful notation | Honours or graduate study; reference use |

**Free** and **open access** in the publisher column mean that the full text is legally available
without charge from the author or publisher. **Free online viewing** means the text can be read
online but is not necessarily downloadable. Print copies may still be sold, and a free edition is
not always the same as the current print edition.

## Pre-University Mathematics

Books for the transition from school arithmetic and algebra to university-level abstraction.

| Author(s) | Title | Edition, year | Publisher / access | Suits and strengths | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Serge Lang | *Basic Mathematics* | 1988 | Springer | Starts from arithmetic and school algebra and builds toward functions, geometry and trigonometry, with attention to why the rules hold. It suits a reader who wants one bridge from school mathematics to a proof-based course. | Standard |
| I. M. Gelfand and Alexander Shen | *Algebra* | 1993 | Birkhäuser | Revisits school algebra through structure, symmetry and proof, with many problems in the Russian tradition. It suits a reader who can manipulate expressions but wants to understand the ideas underneath. | Standard |
| Richard Courant and Herbert Robbins; revised by Ian Stewart | *What Is Mathematics?* | 2nd ed., 1996 | Oxford University Press | A guided tour of number theory, geometry, topology, calculus and analysis that explains ideas rather than drilling techniques. It suits a reader who wants an overview before choosing a specialism. | Introductory |
| Timothy Gowers | *Mathematics: A Very Short Introduction* | 2002 | Oxford University Press | A short account of abstraction, modelling and proof, written for a general reader. It is orientation rather than a course and does not replace practice. | Introductory |
| Jay Abramson et al. | *Precalculus* | 2nd ed., 2021 | OpenStax (**free**) | Covers functions, trigonometry, sequences, series and analytic geometry in the order of a US precalculus course, with exercises and an open licence. It suits a reader who wants a complete, low-cost course text. | Introductory |
| Carl Stitz and Jeff Zeager | *Precalculus* | 3rd corrected ed., 2013 | Stitz-Zeager (**free**) | A full precalculus text with direct explanations, worked examples and a large exercise set. It suits a reader who wants more practice than a short review provides. | Introductory |

## Calculus and Analysis

The calculus books are ordered roughly from computation to proof; the analysis books assume that
calculus has already been seen once.

| Author(s) | Title | Edition, year | Publisher / access | Suits and strengths | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Michael Spivak | *Calculus* | 4th ed., 2008 | Publish or Perish | A proof-based treatment of single-variable calculus whose exercises often introduce substantial results. It suits a reader who has seen calculus once and now wants rigour. | Advanced |
| Tom M. Apostol | *Calculus, Vol. I* | 2nd ed., 1967 | Blaisdell; Wiley reprint | A careful, theorem-proof development of one-variable calculus with linear algebra and some differential equations. It is more demanding than a service course and rewards slow reading. | Advanced |
| James Stewart | *Calculus: Early Transcendentals* | 8th ed., 2015 | Cengage | A widely used service-course text with clear worked examples, graded exercises and strong graphics. It suits a reader who wants a dependable computational first course. | Standard |
| Gilbert Strang et al. | *Calculus, Volumes 1–3* | 2016 | OpenStax (**free**) | An open textbook covering single and multivariable calculus with applications, concise explanations and a large problem set. It suits a reader who wants a complete course text at no cost. | Standard |
| Stephen Abbott | *Understanding Analysis* | 2nd ed., 2015 | Springer | Introduces real analysis through questions and examples rather than an immediate wall of definitions and theorems. It suits a first proof-based analysis course. | Standard |
| Kenneth A. Ross | *Elementary Analysis: The Theory of Calculus* | 2nd ed., 2013 | Springer | A careful first course in analysis that stays close to calculus, with clear proofs and many exercises. It suits a reader who wants a gentler route to Rudin. | Standard |
| Walter Rudin | *Principles of Mathematical Analysis* | 3rd ed., 1976 | McGraw-Hill | Compact, precise and demanding, covering sequences, series, continuity, differentiation, integration and multivariable analysis. It is a standard reference for theorem statements and proofs. | Advanced |
| Tom M. Apostol | *Mathematical Analysis* | 2nd ed., 1974 | Addison-Wesley | A broader and more leisurely treatment than Rudin, with motivation and coverage of topology and measure. It suits a reader who wants a full account rather than a summary. | Advanced |
| Robert G. Bartle and Donald R. Sherbert | *Introduction to Real Analysis* | 4th ed., 2011 | Wiley | A patient undergraduate analysis text with detailed proofs and a good balance of theory and computation. It is useful when Rudin is too terse. | Standard |
| Walter Rudin | *Real and Complex Analysis* | 3rd ed., 1987 | McGraw-Hill | Graduate-level integration, measure theory and complex analysis, written tersely but precisely. It is usually read after a first analysis course. | Advanced |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
