# Compactness — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 36 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely: state the definition or theorem you use, check its hypotheses, and verify the
conclusion against an example. Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md); do not open that file until
you have attempted the problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Define an open cover, a subcover, and a compact space. Prove that every finite topological space is
compact.

**2.** Prove that a discrete space $X$ is compact if and only if $X$ is finite.

**3.** Prove that every set with the indiscrete topology is compact.

**4.** Prove that every set with the cofinite topology is compact.

**5.** Show that $(0,1)$ is not compact by exhibiting an open cover with no finite subcover.

**6.** Show that $[0,1)$ is not compact by exhibiting an open cover with no finite subcover.

**7.** Show that $\mathbb{R}$ is not compact.

**8.** Show that $\mathbb{Z}$ with the discrete topology is not compact.

**9.** Give an open cover of $[0,1]$ and extract an explicit finite subcover.

**10.** Prove that every closed subset of a compact space is compact.

**11.** Prove that the continuous image of a compact space is compact. Apply the result to $f(x)=x^2$ on
$[-1,1]$.

**12.** Prove that every compact subset of a Hausdorff space is closed. Apply the result to
$[0,1]\subseteq\mathbb{R}$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Prove that a finite union of compact subsets of a space is compact.

**14.** Show that an arbitrary union of compact subsets need not be compact, using
$\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}[-n,n]\subseteq\mathbb{R}$.

**15.** Prove that the intersection of two compact subsets of a Hausdorff space is compact.

**16.** Prove that every compact metric space is complete.

**17.** Prove that every compact metric space is totally bounded.

**18.** Prove that every compact metric space is separable (has a countable dense subset).

**19.** Write out the bisection proof that $[0,1]$ is compact.

**20.** Prove that the unit circle $S^1$ and the closed unit disk $\overline{B_1(0)}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^2$ are
compact.

**21.** Let $f(x)=x^3-3x$ on $[0,2]$. Use the extreme value theorem to justify that $f$ attains a maximum and a
minimum, and find both.

**22.** Show that $f(x)=1/x$ is continuous but not uniformly continuous on $(0,1)$. Which compactness
hypothesis of Theorem 7.26 fails?

**23.** Show that $\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1]$ is not compact, using either an explicit open cover or a sequence.

## Advanced

**24.** Prove that every compact metric space is sequentially compact.

**25.** Prove that every sequentially compact metric space is compact, using total boundedness and the
Lebesgue number lemma.

**26.** Prove the Heine–Borel theorem in $\mathbb{R}^n$: a subset is compact if and only if it is closed and
bounded.

**27.** Prove that every continuous function from a compact metric space to a metric space is uniformly
continuous.

**28.** Prove the Lebesgue number lemma for a compact metric space.

**29.** Let $X$ be a locally compact Hausdorff space. Prove that its one-point compactification $X^*$ is compact
and Hausdorff.

**30.** Prove that $\mathbb{Q}$ with the usual topology is not locally compact.

**31.** (Dini's theorem.) Let $X$ be compact, $(f_n)$ a sequence of continuous real-valued functions converging
pointwise monotonically to a continuous $f$. Prove that the convergence is uniform.

## Challenge

**32.** Prove that a metric space $X$ is compact if and only if every continuous function $f:X\to\mathbb{R}$ is
bounded.

**33.** Prove that the one-point compactification of a locally compact Hausdorff space is unique up to a
homeomorphism fixing $X$ pointwise.

**34.** Prove that the closed unit ball of $\ell^2$ is not compact, even though it is closed and bounded.

**35.** Show that the open cover $\{(1/n,1):n\ge2\}$ of $(0,1)$ has no Lebesgue number. Why does this show that
compactness is needed in the Lebesgue number lemma?

**36.** State and prove the Cantor intersection theorem for a nested sequence of nonempty closed sets with
diameters tending to $0$ in a complete metric space. Show by example that completeness is necessary.
