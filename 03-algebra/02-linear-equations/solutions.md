# Linear Equations — Solutions

> Exercises: [exercises.md](exercises.md) · Tutorial: [README.md](README.md)

Each solution states the strategy, shows the equivalence steps, and checks the result. Restrictions and classifications (conditional, identity, contradiction) are part of the answer.

## Beginner

**1.** Subtract $7$ from both sides:

$$
x+7=12 \iff x=5.
$$

Check: $5+7=12$. The solution set is $\{5\}$; the equation is conditional.

**2.** Multiply both sides by $\frac13$:

$$
3x=-15 \iff x=-5.
$$

Check: $3(-5)=-15$. The solution set is $\{-5\}$.

**3.** Add $5$ to both sides, then divide by $2$:

$$
2x-5=9 \iff 2x=14 \iff x=7.
$$

Check: $2(7)-5=9$. The solution set is $\{7\}$.

**4.** Subtract $3$, then multiply by $4$:

$$
\frac{x}{4}+3=7 \iff \frac{x}{4}=4 \iff x=16.
$$

Check: $\frac{16}{4}+3=4+3=7$. The solution set is $\{16\}$.

**5.** Subtract $5$ from both sides:

$$
5-x=2 \iff -x=-3.
$$

Multiply by $-1$:

$$
x=3.
$$

Check: $5-3=2$. The solution set is $\{3\}$.

**6.** Subtract $2x$ from both sides, then subtract $3$:

$$
4x+3=2x+11 \iff 2x+3=11 \iff 2x=8 \iff x=4.
$$

Check: $4(4)+3=19$ and $2(4)+11=19$. The solution set is $\{4\}$.

**7.** Divide both sides by $3$ (or distribute first):

$$
3(x-2)=12 \iff x-2=4 \iff x=6.
$$

Check: $3(6-2)=3\cdot4=12$. The solution set is $\{6\}$.

**8.** Distribute the left side:

$$
2(x+1)=2x+2 \iff 2x+2=2x+2.
$$

Subtracting $2x$ gives $2=2$, which is true for every $x$. The equation is an identity, and its solution set is $\mathbb{R}$.

**9.** Subtract $3x$ from both sides:

$$
3x-4=3x+7 \iff -4=7.
$$

The resulting statement is false, independent of $x$. The equation is a contradiction, and its solution set is $\varnothing$.

**10.** By Theorem 8.4, $|x|=6$ gives $x=6$ or $x=-6$. Check: $|6|=6$ and $|-6|=6$. The solution set is $\{-6,6\}$.

**11.** The equation $|x-4|=3$ says the distance from $x$ to $4$ is $3$. Split:

$$
x-4=3 \quad\text{or}\quad x-4=-3.
$$

The solutions are $x=7$ and $x=1$. Check: $|7-4|=3$ and $|1-4|=3$. The solution set is $\{1,7\}$.

**12.** Subtract $3x$ from both sides:

$$
3x+y=10 \iff y=10-3x.
$$

For each real $x$ there is exactly one $y$, so the solution set is the line of pairs $(x,10-3x)$. Check with $x=1$: $y=7$, and $3(1)+7=10$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Subtract $2x$, then add $3$:

$$
5x-3=2x+12 \iff 3x-3=12 \iff 3x=15 \iff x=5.
$$

Check: $5(5)-3=22$ and $2(5)+12=22$. The solution set is $\{5\}$.

**14.** Distribute and combine constants:

$$
7-2(x-3)=4x+1 \iff 7-2x+6=4x+1 \iff 13-2x=4x+1.
$$

Add $2x$ and subtract $1$:

$$
12=6x \iff x=2.
$$

Check: $7-2(-1)=9$ and $4(2)+1=9$. The solution set is $\{2\}$.

**15.** Simplify both sides:

$$
3(2x+1)-4=6x+3-4=6x-1,
$$

$$
2(3x-5)+9=6x-10+9=6x-1.
$$

The equation is $6x-1=6x-1$; subtracting $6x$ gives $-1=-1$, true for every $x$. It is an identity, and the solution set is $\mathbb{R}$.

**16.** The LCD of $2,3,4$ is $12$. Multiply every term by $12$:

$$
6x+4=9x-12.
$$

Subtract $6x$ and add $12$:

$$
16=3x \iff x=\frac{16}{3}.
$$

Check: $\frac12\cdot\frac{16}{3}+\frac13=\frac83+\frac13=3$, and $\frac34\cdot\frac{16}{3}-1=4-1=3$. The solution set is $\left\{\frac{16}{3}\right\}$.

**17.** Multiply both sides by $6$:

$$
2(2x-1)=3(x+4).
$$

Distribute:

$$
4x-2=3x+12 \iff x=14.
$$

Check: $\frac{2(14)-1}{3}=\frac{27}{3}=9$ and $\frac{14+4}{2}=\frac{18}{2}=9$. The solution set is $\{14\}$.

**18.** Multiply every term by $10$:

$$
5x-12=2x+9.
$$

Subtract $2x$ and add $12$:

$$
3x=21 \iff x=7.
$$

Check: $0.5(7)-1.2=3.5-1.2=2.3$ and $0.2(7)+0.9=1.4+0.9=2.3$. The solution set is $\{7\}$.

**19.** Multiply every term by $100$:

$$
3x+2=5x-4.
$$

Subtract $3x$ and add $4$:

$$
6=2x \iff x=3.
$$

Check: $0.03(3)+0.02=0.09+0.02=0.11$ and $0.05(3)-0.04=0.15-0.04=0.11$. The solution set is $\{3\}$.

**20.** Since $11>0$, split:

$$
2x+1=11 \quad\text{or}\quad 2x+1=-11.
$$

The first gives $x=5$; the second gives $2x=-12$, so $x=-6$. Check: $|2(5)+1|=11$ and $|2(-6)+1|=|-11|=11$. The solution set is $\{-6,5\}$.

**21.** Since $4>0$, split:

$$
5-3x=4 \quad\text{or}\quad 5-3x=-4.
$$

The first gives $-3x=-1$, so $x=\frac13$. The second gives $-3x=-9$, so $x=3$. Check: $|5-1|=4$ and $|5-9|=4$. The solution set is $\left\{\frac13,3\right\}$.

**22.** Subtract $3$ from both sides:

$$
|x|+3=1 \iff |x|=-2.
$$

Absolute value is never negative, so there is no real solution. The solution set is $\varnothing$.

**23.** Multiply both sides by $2$:

$$
a+b=2c \iff b=2c-a.
$$

The multiplier $2$ is nonzero, so no restriction is needed. Check with $a=1$, $c=3$: $b=5$, and $\frac{1+5}{2}=3$.

**24.** Divide by $P$ (requires $P\ne0$):

$$
\frac{S}{P}=1+rt.
$$

Subtract $1$:

$$
\frac{S}{P}-1=rt.
$$

Divide by $t$ (requires $t\ne0$):

$$
r=\frac{1}{t}\left(\frac{S}{P}-1\right)=\frac{S-P}{Pt}.
$$

Thus $r=\frac{S-P}{Pt}$ provided $P\ne0$ and $t\ne0$. If $t=0$, the original equation reads $S=P$ and places no condition on $r$. If $P=0$ and $t\ne0$, then $S=0$ and again $r$ is not determined. Check with $P=1000$, $S=1100$, $t=2$: $r=\frac{100}{2000}=0.05$.

## Advanced

**25.** The LCD of $4,2,6$ is $12$. Multiply every term by $12$:

$$
9-6(x-1)=2(2x+1).
$$

Distribute:

$$
9-6x+6=4x+2 \iff 15-6x=4x+2.
$$

Add $6x$ and subtract $2$:

$$
13=10x \iff x=\frac{13}{10}.
$$

Check numerically: $\frac34-\frac{0.3}{2}=0.75-0.15=0.6$, and $\frac{2(1.3)+1}{6}=\frac{3.6}{6}=0.6$. The solution set is $\left\{\frac{13}{10}\right\}$.

**26.** Multiply every term by $12$:

$$
3(x-3)-2(2x+1)=6.
$$

Distribute:

$$
3x-9-4x-2=6 \iff -x-11=6.
$$

Add $11$:

$$
-x=17 \iff x=-17.
$$

Check: $\frac{-20}{4}-\frac{-33}{6}=-5-(-5.5)=0.5=\frac12$. The solution set is $\{-17\}$.

**27.** Multiply every term by $100$:

$$
2(3x-1)=5(x+2).
$$

Distribute:

$$
6x-2=5x+10 \iff x=12.
$$

Check: $0.02(35)=0.7$ and $0.05(14)=0.7$. The solution set is $\{12\}$.

**28.** Multiply every term by $15$:

$$
30-3(x+3)=5(2x-1).
$$

Distribute:

$$
30-3x-9=10x-5 \iff 21-3x=10x-5.
$$

Add $3x$ and add $5$:

$$
26=13x \iff x=2.
$$

Check: $2-\frac55=1$ and $\frac{4-1}{3}=1$. The solution set is $\{2\}$.

**29.** Since the right side is $0$, there is one case:

$$
3x-6=0 \iff 3x=6 \iff x=2.
$$

Check: $|3(2)-6|=0$. The solution set is $\{2\}$.

**30.** Use $|A|=|B|\iff A=B$ or $A=-B$:

$$
2x-3=x+1 \quad\text{or}\quad 2x-3=-(x+1).
$$

The first gives $x=4$. The second gives $2x-3=-x-1$, hence $3x=2$ and $x=\frac23$. Check: $|2(4)-3|=5=|4+1|$, and $\left|\frac43-3\right|=\frac53=\left|\frac23+1\right|$. The solution set is $\left\{\frac23,4\right\}$.

**31.** Expand both sides:

$$
ax+ab=cx-cd.
$$

Subtract $cx$ and $ab$:

$$
(a-c)x=-cd-ab=-(ab+cd).
$$

Since $a\ne c$, divide by $a-c$:

$$
x=-\frac{ab+cd}{a-c}.
$$

Check with $a=2$, $b=3$, $c=1$, $d=4$: $x=-\frac{6+4}{1}=-10$; the left side is $2(-10+3)=-14$ and the right side is $1(-10-4)=-14$. Correct.

**32.** Let $d$ be the daughter's present age. The mother is $3d$. In $12$ years their ages are $d+12$ and $3d+12$, and the condition is

$$
3d+12=2(d+12).
$$

Solve:

$$
3d+12=2d+24 \iff d=12.
$$

The daughter is $12$ and the mother is $36$. Check: in $12$ years they are $24$ and $48$, and $48=2\cdot24$. Correct.

**33.** Let $t$ be the time in hours working together. Their rates are $\frac19$ and $\frac1{18}$ of the tank per hour, so

$$
\frac19+\frac1{18}=\frac1t.
$$

The left side is $\frac{2}{18}+\frac{1}{18}=\frac{3}{18}=\frac16$, so $t=6$. Check: in $6$ hours A fills $\frac69=\frac23$ and B fills $\frac6{18}=\frac13$; together $1$ tank. The answer is $6$ hours.

**34.** Let $t$ be the number of hours. The trains move in opposite directions, so their distances add:

$$
60t+80t=350.
$$

Thus $140t=350$ and $t=\frac{350}{140}=\frac52$ hours, that is, $2.5$ hours. Check: the first travels $60(2.5)=150$ km and the second $80(2.5)=200$ km; $150+200=350$. Correct.

**35.** The amount of acid is $0.30(10)=3$ L from the first solution and $0.60(20)=12$ L from the second, for a total of $15$ L. The total volume is $10+20=30$ L, so the concentration is

$$
c=\frac{15}{30}=0.5,
$$

that is, $50\%$. Check: $3+12=15$ and $15/30=0.5$. Correct.

**36.** Let $x$ be the litres of pure water added. The salt amount is $0.40(12)=4.8$ L and is unchanged by adding water. The final volume is $12+x$, and the final concentration is $0.30$:

$$
0.40(12)=0.30(12+x).
$$

Solve:

$$
4.8=3.6+0.30x \iff 1.2=0.30x \iff x=4.
$$

Check: the final volume is $16$ L and the salt is $4.8$ L; $4.8/16=0.30$. The answer is $4$ litres.

## Challenge

**37.** Distribute both sides:

$$
2x-2=3x-3.
$$

Subtract $2x$ and add $3$:

$$
1=x.
$$

Check: $2(1-1)=0$ and $3(1-1)=0$, so $x=1$ is the solution. The solution set is $\{1\}$.

If one divides both sides by $x-1$ at the start, one obtains $2=3$, a false statement. The division is invalid when $x-1=0$, which is exactly at the solution $x=1$; division by zero is undefined, and the operation also discards the only solution. The correct move is to subtract $3(x-1)$ and factor:

$$
2(x-1)-3(x-1)=0 \iff (2-3)(x-1)=0 \iff -(x-1)=0 \iff x=1.
$$

**38.** The right side $3x+2$ can be negative, so every candidate must be checked.

**Case 1.** $2x-1=3x+2$. Then $-x=3$, so $x=-3$. The right side is $3(-3)+2=-7<0$, which cannot equal an absolute value. Reject $x=-3$.

**Case 2.** $2x-1=-(3x+2)$. Then $2x-1=-3x-2$, so $5x=-1$ and $x=-\frac15$. The right side is $3\left(-\frac15\right)+2=\frac75>0$, and the left side is $\left|-\frac25-1\right|=\left|-\frac75\right|=\frac75$. Valid.

The solution set is $\left\{-\frac15\right\}$.

**39.** Assume $a>0$. Split $|x-a|=2a$:

$$
x-a=2a \quad\text{or}\quad x-a=-2a.
$$

The first gives $x=3a$; the second gives $x=-a$. Both check: $|3a-a|=|2a|=2a$ and $|-a-a|=|-2a|=2a$ because $a>0$. The solution set is $\{-a,3a\}$.

If $a=0$, the equation is $|x|=0$, whose only solution is $x=0$. If $a<0$, the right side $2a$ is negative, while the left side is nonnegative, so there is no solution.

**40.** Write the equation as $3|x-2|=k$.

* If $k>0$, then $|x-2|=\frac{k}{3}$, which has the two distinct solutions $x=2\pm\frac{k}{3}$.
* If $k=0$, then $|x-2|=0$, which has the single solution $x=2$.
* If $k<0$, there is no solution because $3|x-2|\ge0$.

Therefore exactly one solution occurs precisely when $k=0$.

**41.** Subtract $cx+b$ from both sides:

$$
ax+b=cx+d \iff (a-c)x=d-b.
$$

This step is reversible: adding $cx+b$ to both sides recovers the original equation.

If $a\ne c$, then $a-c\ne0$, so Theorem 8.1 applies and

$$
x=\frac{d-b}{a-c}
$$

is the unique solution.

If $a=c$, the reduced equation is $0=d-b$. If $d=b$, this is $0=0$, true for every real $x$; the original equation is an identity. If $d\ne b$, it is a false numerical statement, so no real $x$ satisfies it; the original equation is a contradiction. These three cases exhaust all possibilities, so the trichotomy is proved. $\square$

**42.** Squaring both sides is not a reversible operation on the real numbers. If $A=B$, then certainly $A^2=B^2$. But $A^2=B^2$ implies only $A=B$ or $A=-B$, and the second alternative may not satisfy $A=B$.

For example, start with $x=3$. Squaring gives $x^2=9$, whose solutions are $x=3$ and $x=-3$. The value $-3$ does not satisfy the original equation $x=3$; it is an extraneous solution introduced by squaring. Substituting every candidate into the original equation removes it. In general, squaring can add solutions but cannot remove them; checking is therefore mandatory whenever it is used.

**43.** Let $t$ be the number of hours after 9:00. The cyclist rides for $t$ hours at $15$ km/h, covering $15t$ km. The car starts at 10:00, so it travels for $t-1$ hours at $45$ km/h, covering $45(t-1)$ km. At the catch-up point the distances are equal:

$$
15t=45(t-1).
$$

Solve:

$$
15t=45t-45 \iff 30t=45 \iff t=\frac32.
$$

The car catches the cyclist $1.5$ hours after 9:00, that is, at 10:30. The distance is $15\left(\frac32\right)=22.5$ km.

Check: the cyclist rides $1.5$ h at $15$ km/h, covering $22.5$ km; the car rides $0.5$ h at $45$ km/h, also covering $22.5$ km. Correct.

**44.** Let $b$ be the number of days B takes alone. In one day A completes $\frac1{10}$ of the job, B completes $\frac1b$, and together they complete $\frac16$:

$$
\frac1{10}+\frac1b=\frac16.
$$

Subtract $\frac1{10}$:

$$
\frac1b=\frac16-\frac1{10}=\frac{5-3}{30}=\frac{2}{30}=\frac1{15}.
$$

Therefore $b=15$. Check: in one day A does $\frac1{10}$ and B does $\frac1{15}$; the sum is $\frac{3}{30}+\frac{2}{30}=\frac{5}{30}=\frac16$, so together they take $6$ days. The answer is $15$ days.

**45.** Let $s$ be the son's present age. The father's present age is $2s+5$. Eight years ago their ages were $s-8$ and $2s+5-8=2s-3$, and the father was three times as old:

$$
2s-3=3(s-8).
$$

Solve:

$$
2s-3=3s-24 \iff 21=s.
$$

The son is $21$ and the father is $2(21)+5=47$.

Check: eight years ago the son was $13$ and the father was $39$, and $39=3\cdot13$. Correct.
