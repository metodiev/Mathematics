# Elementary Algebra — Solutions

> Exercises: [exercises.md](exercises.md) · Tutorial: [README.md](README.md)

Every solution below states the strategy, shows the significant steps, and checks the result where a check is meaningful. Restrictions on variables are part of the answer whenever a denominator is involved.

## Beginner

**1.** Substitute $x=3$, using parentheses:

$$
2(3)^2-5(3)+1=2\cdot9-15+1=18-15+1=4.
$$

The value is $4$.

**2.** Substitute $a=-1$ and $b=4$:

$$
3(-1)-2(4)+(-1)(4)=-3-8-4=-15.
$$

The value is $-15$. The last term is negative because $(-1)(4)=-4$.

**3.** "Twice a number $n$" is $2n$; "seven more than" adds $7$. The expression is $2n+7$.

**4.** The sum is $x+3$, and the product of five with that sum requires parentheses: $5(x+3)$. Without parentheses, $5x+3$ would mean "five times $x$, plus three", which is a different expression.

**5.** (a) Expression: it has no equals sign and no truth value. (b) Equation: it states that two expressions are equal. (c) Expression: the equals sign is absent. (d) Equation: it is a statement about $y$.

**6.** Reorder and combine like terms:

$$
7x+3-2x+8=(7-2)x+(3+8)=5x+11.
$$

Check at $x=1$: the original is $7+3-2+8=16$, and $5(1)+11=16$. Correct.

**7.** Distribute and combine constants:

$$
4(3x-2)+5=12x-8+5=12x-3.
$$

Check at $x=1$: $4(1)+5=9$ and $12-3=9$. Correct.

**8.** The coefficients $15$ and $25$ have greatest common divisor $5$, and $x$ is not common to both terms:

$$
15x+25=5(3x)+5(5)=5(3x+5).
$$

Check by expanding: $5(3x+5)=15x+25$.

**9.** Group like terms:

$$
(3x^2+2x-5)+(x^2-7x+4)=(3+1)x^2+(2-7)x+(-5+4)=4x^2-5x-1.
$$

Check at $x=1$: the original is $(3+2-5)+(1-7+4)=0-2=-2$, and $4-5-1=-2$. Correct.

**10.** Subtract every term of the second polynomial:

$$
(5x^2-3x+2)-(2x^2+x-6)=5x^2-3x+2-2x^2-x+6=3x^2-4x+8.
$$

The signs of both $x$ and $-6$ change. Check at $x=1$: the original is $(5-3+2)-(2+1-6)=4-(-3)=7$, and $3-4+8=7$. Correct.

**11.** Divide coefficients and subtract exponents:

$$
\frac{18x^4}{6x^2}=\frac{18}{6}x^{4-2}=3x^2.
$$

The original expression is undefined when $6x^2=0$, that is, when $x=0$. Therefore the simplification is valid for $x\ne0$, and the restriction must be stated even though $3x^2$ is defined at $0$. Check at $x=2$: $\frac{18\cdot16}{6\cdot4}=\frac{288}{24}=12=3\cdot4$.

**12.** Subtract $2l$ from both sides, then divide by $2$:

$$
P=2l+2w
\iff P-2l=2w
\iff w=\frac{P-2l}{2}.
$$

The divisor is the nonzero constant $2$, so no restriction is needed. Check with $P=20$ and $l=6$: $w=\frac{20-12}{2}=4$, and $2(6)+2(4)=20$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Distribute both products, taking care with the second sign:

$$
3(2x-4)-2(x-5)=6x-12-2x+10=4x-2.
$$

Check at $x=3$: the original is $3(2)-2(-2)=6+4=10$, and $4(3)-2=10$. Correct.

**14.** Work from the innermost parentheses outward:

$$
3x-4(x-1)=3x-4x+4=-x+4.
$$

Substitute this back:

$$
2x-(-x+4)+5=2x+x-4+5=3x+1.
$$

The crucial step is $-(-x+4)=+x-4$. Check at $x=2$: the original is $4-[6-4(1)]+5=4-2+5=7$, and $3(2)+1=7$. Correct.

**15.** The coefficient gcd is $\gcd(24,36,12)=12$. The smallest power of $a$ is $a^2$, and the smallest power of $b$ is $b^2$. Hence

$$
24a^3b^2-36a^2b^3+12a^2b^2=12a^2b^2(2a-3b+1).
$$

Check at $a=2$, $b=1$: the original is $192-144+48=96$, and the factored form is $12\cdot4\cdot1\cdot(4-3+1)=96$. Correct.

**16.** Multiply each term by each term:

$$
(x+4)(x-6)=x^2-6x+4x-24=x^2-2x-24.
$$

Check at $x=1$: $(5)(-5)=-25$ and $1-2-24=-25$. Correct.

**17.**

$$
(3x-2)(2x+5)=6x^2+15x-4x-10=6x^2+11x-10.
$$

Check at $x=2$: $(4)(9)=36$ and $24+22-10=36$. Correct.

**18.** Use $(a+b)^2=a^2+2ab+b^2$ with $a=2x$ and $b=3$:

$$
(2x+3)^2=(2x)^2+2(2x)(3)+3^2=4x^2+12x+9.
$$

Check at $x=1$: $5^2=25$ and $4+12+9=25$. Correct. The missing middle term $12x$ is the usual error.

**19.** Divide term by term:

$$
\frac{15x^4y^3}{5x^2y}-\frac{10x^3y^2}{5x^2y}+\frac{5x^2y}{5x^2y}
=3x^2y^2-2xy+1.
$$

The divisor $5x^2y$ is zero when $x=0$ or $y=0$, so the original expression requires $x\ne0$ and $y\ne0$. Check at $x=2$, $y=3$: the numerator is $6480-720+60=5820$, the divisor is $60$, and the quotient is $97$; the simplified form gives $3\cdot4\cdot9-2\cdot2\cdot3+1=108-12+1=97$. Correct.

**20.** Substitute with parentheses:

$$
4(-2)^2-3(-2)(-1)+(-1)^2=4\cdot4-6+1=16-6+1=11.
$$

The middle term is negative because $(-2)(-1)=2$ and $-3\cdot2=-6$. The value is $11$.

**21.** Subtract $u$ from both sides, then divide by $a$:

$$
v=u+at
\iff v-u=at
\iff t=\frac{v-u}{a}\qquad(a\ne0).
$$

The condition $a\ne0$ is required. Check with $u=2$, $a=3$, $t=4$: $v=14$, and $\frac{14-2}{3}=4$.

**22.** The constant $2\pi$ is nonzero, so

$$
C=2\pi r
\iff r=\frac{C}{2\pi}.
$$

Check with $C=10\pi$: $r=\frac{10\pi}{2\pi}=5$.

**23.** Divide both sides by $w$:

$$
A=lw
\iff l=\frac{A}{w}\qquad(w\ne0).
$$

If $w=0$, the original equation reads $A=0$ and gives no information about $l$; the rearrangement is not available. Check with $A=12$ and $w=3$: $l=4$, and $3\cdot4=12$.

**24.** The three integers are $n$, $n+1$, and $n+2$. Their sum is

$$
n+(n+1)+(n+2)=3n+3=3(n+1).
$$

Check with $n=4$: $4+5+6=15=3(5)$. The middle integer is $n+1$, and the sum of three consecutive integers is three times the middle one.

## Advanced

**25.** Work from the innermost bracket outward:

$$
3-(x-4)+2x=3-x+4+2x=7+x.
$$

Then

$$
5-2(7+x)=5-14-2x=-9-2x.
$$

Check at $x=1$: the original is $5-2[3-(-3)+2]=5-2(8)=-11$, and $-9-2=-11$. Correct.

**26.** The common monomial with the requested sign is $-3x$:

$$
-6x^3+9x^2-3x=-3x(2x^2-3x+1).
$$

Check at $x=2$: the original is $-48+36-6=-18$, and $-6(8-6+1)=-18$. Correct. The equivalent factorization $3x(-2x^2+3x-1)$ is also valid; the choice of sign is a convention, not a mathematical requirement. (The inner quadratic happens to factor further as $(2x-1)(x-1)$, but factoring quadratics is treated later.)

**27.** Distribute each term of the first factor:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x-2)(x^2+3x-1)
&=x(x^2+3x-1)-2(x^2+3x-1)\\
&=x^3+3x^2-x-2x^2-6x+2\\
&=x^3+x^2-7x+2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Check at $x=3$: $(1)(9+9-1)=17$, and $27+9-21+2=17$. Correct.

**28.** Divide term by term:

$$
\frac{8x^3y^2}{4x^2y^2}-\frac{4x^2y^3}{4x^2y^2}+\frac{12x^2y^2}{4x^2y^2}
=2x-y+3.
$$

The divisor $4x^2y^2$ is zero when $x=0$ or $y=0$, so $x\ne0$ and $y\ne0$ are required. Check at $x=2$, $y=1$: the numerator is $64-16+48=96$, the divisor is $16$, and the quotient is $6$; the simplified form gives $4-1+3=6$. Correct.

**29.** The original expression requires $r\ne1$. Multiply both sides by $1-r$, which is nonzero on the domain:

$$
S(1-r)=a.
$$

Distribute and isolate the term containing $r$:

$$
S-Sr=a
\iff -Sr=a-S.
$$

If $S\ne0$, divide by $-S$:

$$
r=\frac{a-S}{-S}=\frac{S-a}{S}.
$$

The formula is valid whenever $S\ne0$.

Now consider $S=0$. The equation $S(1-r)=a$ becomes $0=a$. If $a\ne0$, there is no solution for $r$. If $a=0$, the original equation reads $0=\frac{0}{1-r}$, which is true for every $r\ne1$; in that case $r$ is not determined.

Check the formula with $a=2$ and $r=\frac12$: then $S=\frac{2}{1-\frac12}=4$, and $\frac{S-a}{S}=\frac{4-2}{4}=\frac12$. Correct.

**30.** Subtract $cx+b$ from both sides:

$$
ax+b=cx+d
\iff ax-cx=d-b
\iff (a-c)x=d-b.
$$

If $a\ne c$, then $a-c\ne0$ and

$$
x=\frac{d-b}{a-c}.
$$

If $a=c$, the equation reduces to $0=d-b$. When $d=b$ this is true for every $x$ (an identity); when $d\ne b$ it is false for every $x$ (a contradiction). Thus the hypothesis $a\ne c$ is exactly what guarantees a unique solution.

Check with $a=3$, $b=1$, $c=1$, $d=9$: $x=\frac{9-1}{3-1}=4$, and both sides equal $3(4)+1=13$ and $1(4)+9=13$.

**31.** (a) For $m$ minutes, Plan A costs $25+0.10m$ dollars per month, and Plan B costs $40$ dollars per month.

(b) Set the costs equal:

$$
25+0.10m=40
\iff 0.10m=15
\iff m=150.
$$

At $150$ minutes both plans cost $25+15=40$ dollars. Check: Plan A is $25+0.10(150)=40$, and Plan B is $40$. The answer is $150$ minutes.

**32.** Let $w$ be the width in metres. The length is $2w+5$. The perimeter condition gives

$$
2\bigl(w+(2w+5)\bigr)=70.
$$

Simplify and solve:

$$
2(3w+5)=70
\iff 6w+10=70
\iff 6w=60
\iff w=10.
$$

The width is $10$ m and the length is $2(10)+5=25$ m. Check: $2(10)+2(25)=20+50=70$ m, and $25$ is $5$ more than twice $10$. Correct.

**33.** Expand using distributivity (or the square-of-a-sum identity):

$$
(x+3)^2=(x+3)(x+3)=x^2+3x+3x+9=x^2+6x+9.
$$

Therefore

$$
(x+3)^2-(x^2+9)=6x.
$$

The two expressions are equal only when $6x=0$, that is, when $x=0$. For a numerical counterexample, take $x=1$: $(1+3)^2=16$, while $1^2+9=10$. Since $16\ne10$, the proposed identity fails. The error is squaring each term separately and omitting the cross terms $3x+3x$.

**34.** Begin by writing each product with the variable last, using commutativity of multiplication:

$$
3x+2x=x\cdot3+x\cdot2.
$$

Now apply distributivity in the form $x(3+2)=x\cdot3+x\cdot2$, read from right to left:

$$
x\cdot3+x\cdot2=x(3+2).
$$

Finally, $3+2=5$ and $x\cdot5=5x$ by commutativity. Hence $3x+2x=5x$. Each step uses only the stated properties and arithmetic; the variable part $x$ is a common factor, which is exactly what distributivity combines.

## Challenge

**35.** We prove the sign rule in two stages.

*Stage 1: $(-a)b=-(ab)$.* By distributivity, additivity of inverses, and multiplication by zero,

$$
ab+(-a)b=\bigl(a+(-a)\bigr)b=0\cdot b=0.
$$

Thus $(-a)b$ is an additive inverse of $ab$. By uniqueness of additive inverses (Lemma 7.1), $(-a)b=-(ab)$.

*Stage 2: $a(-b)=-(ab)$.* By commutativity and Stage 1,

$$
a(-b)=(-b)a=-(ba)=-(ab).
$$

Now apply Stage 1 with $b$ replaced by $-b$:

$$
(-a)(-b)=-\bigl(a(-b)\bigr).
$$

By Stage 2, $a(-b)=-(ab)$, so

$$
-\bigl(a(-b)\bigr)=-\bigl(-(ab)\bigr).
$$

Since $ab+(-(ab))=0$, the number $ab$ is an additive inverse of $-(ab)$; uniqueness of additive inverses gives $-\bigl(-(ab)\bigr)=ab$. Therefore $(-a)(-b)=ab$. Each step used only (A2), (A3), (A5), (A6), Lemma 7.1, and Consequence 7.2.

**36.** Suppose $ab=0$. We must show $a=0$ or $b=0$. Equivalently, suppose $a\ne0$ and prove $b=0$.

Because $a\ne0$, the multiplicative inverse $a^{-1}$ exists by (A5). Then

$$
b=1\cdot b=(a^{-1}a)b=a^{-1}(ab)=a^{-1}\cdot0=0,
$$

where the equalities use (A4), (A5), (A3), the hypothesis $ab=0$, and Consequence 7.2. Hence $b=0$ whenever $a\ne0$. Therefore at least one factor is zero. $\square$

The result is used in Section 7.8 to prove that the degree of a product is the sum of the degrees: the leading coefficient of a product cannot vanish unless one of the leading coefficients does.

**37.** The invalid step is (4): dividing both sides by $a-b$. Since the proof begins with $a=b$, we have $a-b=0$, and division by zero is undefined. The equality in (3),

$$
(a-b)(a+b)=b(a-b),
$$

is true precisely because both sides are $0$; it carries no information about $a+b$ and $b$. Legitimately, (3) is equivalent to

$$
(a-b)\bigl((a+b)-b\bigr)=0,
$$

that is, $(a-b)a=0$. Since the hypothesis is $a=b$, the factor $a-b$ is zero, so this equation is automatically true and yields no information about $a$ or $b$. The "proof" fails at the division by zero, not at any of the algebraic identities before it.

**38.** Write $4x^2-9=(2x)^2-3^2$ and apply $u^2-v^2=(u-v)(u+v)$ with $u=2x$ and $v=3$:

$$
4x^2-9=(2x-3)(2x+3).
$$

Verify by expanding:

$$
(2x-3)(2x+3)=4x^2+6x-6x-9=4x^2-9.
$$

Check at $x=2$: $4(4)-9=7$ and $(4-3)(4+3)=1\cdot7=7$. Correct.

**39.** Let $n$ be the number. "Three times the number" is $3n$; "twelve less than" that is $3n-12$. The sentence becomes

$$
3n-12=5n.
$$

Subtract $3n$ from both sides:

$$
-12=2n.
$$

Divide by $2$:

$$
n=-6.
$$

Check: $3(-6)-12=-18-12=-30$, and $5(-6)=-30$. Both sides agree, so the number is $-6$.

**40.** The length is $2w+4$. The perimeter is twice the sum of width and length:

$$
P=2\bigl(w+(2w+4)\bigr)=2(3w+4)=6w+8.
$$

Check with $w=3$: the length is $10$, the perimeter is $2(3)+2(10)=26$, and $6(3)+8=26$. Correct. The simplified expression is $6w+8$.
