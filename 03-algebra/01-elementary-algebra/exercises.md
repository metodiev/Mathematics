# Elementary Algebra — Exercises

> Tutorial: [README.md](README.md) · Solutions: [solutions.md](solutions.md)

Work each problem on paper before consulting the solutions. Show the reason for every significant step, not only the answer. Where a problem asks for a simplification, state any restriction on the variables; where it asks for a factorization, verify it by expanding.

## Beginner

**1.** Evaluate $2x^2-5x+1$ at $x=3$.

**2.** Evaluate $3a-2b+ab$ at $a=-1$ and $b=4$.

**3.** Translate into an algebraic expression: "seven more than twice a number $n$."

**4.** Translate into an algebraic expression: "the product of five and the sum of $x$ and three."

**5.** Classify each item as an expression or an equation:

(a) $4x-7$  (b) $4x-7=9$  (c) $2(x+1)+3$  (d) $5y=20$

**6.** Simplify $7x+3-2x+8$.

**7.** Simplify $4(3x-2)+5$.

**8.** Factor $15x+25$.

**9.** Add: $(3x^2+2x-5)+(x^2-7x+4)$.

**10.** Subtract: $(5x^2-3x+2)-(2x^2+x-6)$.

**11.** Simplify $\dfrac{18x^4}{6x^2}$ and state any restriction on $x$.

**12.** Solve the formula $P=2l+2w$ for $w$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Simplify $3(2x-4)-2(x-5)$.

**14.** Simplify $2x-[3x-4(x-1)]+5$.

**15.** Factor $24a^3b^2-36a^2b^3+12a^2b^2$.

**16.** Multiply $(x+4)(x-6)$.

**17.** Multiply $(3x-2)(2x+5)$.

**18.** Expand $(2x+3)^2$.

**19.** Simplify $\dfrac{15x^4y^3-10x^3y^2+5x^2y}{5x^2y}$ and state all restrictions.

**20.** Evaluate $4x^2-3xy+y^2$ at $x=-2$ and $y=-1$.

**21.** Solve $v=u+at$ for $t$, stating any necessary condition.

**22.** Solve $C=2\pi r$ for $r$.

**23.** Solve $A=lw$ for $l$, stating any necessary condition.

**24.** Write a simplified expression for the sum of three consecutive integers beginning with $n$.

## Advanced

**25.** Simplify $5-2[3-(x-4)+2x]$.

**26.** Factor out the common monomial $-3x$ from $-6x^3+9x^2-3x$.

**27.** Multiply $(x-2)(x^2+3x-1)$.

**28.** Simplify $\dfrac{8x^3y^2-4x^2y^3+12x^2y^2}{4x^2y^2}$ and state all restrictions.

**29.** Solve $S=\dfrac{a}{1-r}$ for $r$. State all restrictions and discuss separately what happens when $S=0$.

**30.** Solve $ax+b=cx+d$ for $x$. State the hypothesis needed on $a$ and $c$, and explain what happens when that hypothesis fails.

**31.** A phone company offers two plans. Plan A costs \$25 per month plus \$0.10 per minute of calls. Plan B costs \$40 per month with unlimited calls. Let $m$ be the number of minutes used in a month.

(a) Write an expression for the monthly cost of each plan.
(b) Find the number of minutes for which the two plans cost the same.

**32.** A rectangular garden is $5$ m longer than twice its width. Its perimeter is $70$ m. Find its width and length.

**33.** Explain why $(x+3)^2$ is not equal to $x^2+9$ in general. Expand $(x+3)^2$ fully and give a numerical counterexample.

**34.** Justify the identity $3x+2x=5x$ using only commutativity of multiplication, distributivity, and ordinary arithmetic.

## Challenge

**35.** Prove from the field axioms that $(-a)(-b)=ab$ for all real numbers $a$ and $b$. Cite each axiom or previously proved consequence that you use.

**36.** Prove that if $ab=0$, then $a=0$ or $b=0$.

**37.** A student offers the following "proof" that $1=2$.

> Let $a=b$. Then
> (1) $a^2=ab$;
> (2) $a^2-b^2=ab-b^2$;
> (3) $(a-b)(a+b)=b(a-b)$;
> (4) dividing both sides by $a-b$ gives $a+b=b$;
> (5) since $a=b$, this gives $2b=b$, hence $2=1$.

Identify the invalid step, explain why it is invalid, and state what can legitimately be concluded from line (3).

**38.** Use the difference-of-squares identity to factor $4x^2-9$, and verify the factorization by expanding.

**39.** Translate and solve: "Twelve less than three times a number is the same as five times the number." Find the number.

**40.** A rectangle has width $w$ and length $4$ more than twice its width. Write a simplified expression for its perimeter in terms of $w$.
