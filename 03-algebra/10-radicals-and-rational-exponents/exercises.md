# Radicals and Rational Exponents — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 36 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Show every step: state the index, extract perfect powers explicitly, and for equations record the
domain and the sign condition *before* squaring. Check each solution in the original equation. Full
solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md); do not open that file until you have attempted the
problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Evaluate (a) $\sqrt{81}$, (b) $\sqrt[3]{-64}$, (c) $\sqrt[4]{81}$.

**2.** Simplify $\sqrt{98}$.

**3.** Simplify $\sqrt{108}$.

**4.** Simplify $\sqrt{20x^2}$, where $x$ is any real number.

**5.** Simplify $\sqrt{16x^2y^4}$.

**6.** Write with a rational exponent: (a) $\sqrt[3]{x^5}$, (b) $\sqrt[5]{x^3}$.

**7.** Write with radicals: (a) $x^{3/4}$, (b) $x^{-2/3}$.

**8.** Evaluate $125^{2/3}$.

**9.** Evaluate $81^{-3/4}$.

**10.** Simplify $4\sqrt7+5\sqrt7$.

**11.** Simplify $\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{27}$.

**12.** Rationalise the denominator: $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt3}$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Multiply and simplify: $(5+\sqrt3)(5-\sqrt3)$.

**14.** Expand and simplify: $(3\sqrt2-2)^2$.

**15.** Rationalise the denominator: $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{11}-\sqrt7}$.

**16.** Simplify $\sqrt[3]{24}+\sqrt[3]{81}$.

**17.** Simplify $\left(\sqrt[3]{3}\right)^4$ and write the answer as a single radical.

**18.** Simplify (a) $\sqrt{x^4y^6}$, (b) $\sqrt[3]{x^6y^9}$.

**19.** Solve $\sqrt{3x+1}=x-1$ and identify any extraneous root.

**20.** Solve $\sqrt{x+7}=x+1$ and identify any extraneous root.

**21.** Solve $\sqrt{3x-2}=\sqrt{x+6}$.

**22.** Find the distance between $(2,1)$ and $(5,5)$, and the midpoint of the segment joining them.

## Advanced

**23.** Solve $\sqrt{2x+7}-\sqrt{x+3}=2$.

**24.** Solve $\sqrt{x+5}+\sqrt{x}=5$.

**25.** Solve $\sqrt[3]{3x+2}=2$.

**26.** Solve $\sqrt{x+1}=x-1$.

**27.** Rationalise the denominator: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{3}-1}$.

**28.** Simplify $\sqrt{11+6\sqrt2}$ by writing it as $\sqrt a+\sqrt b$ for suitable $a,b$.

**29.** Solve $x^{2/3}=4$ for all real $x$.

**30.** Use the distance formula to show that $A=(-1,2)$, $B=(3,5)$ and $C=(7,8)$ are collinear.

## Challenge

**31.** Solve $\sqrt{2x+3}+\sqrt{x-2}=4$ and show that one candidate obtained by squaring must be
discarded.

**32.** Solve $\sqrt{x^2-6x+9}=3-x$ and describe the full solution set.

**33.** Simplify $\sqrt{4+2\sqrt3}+\sqrt{4-2\sqrt3}$ without decimal approximation, and justify each
step.

**34.** Prove: if $n$ is a positive integer and $\sqrt n$ is rational, then $\sqrt n$ is an integer.

**35.** Explain why $\sqrt a\,\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}$ requires $a,b\ge0$. Give a numerical demonstration of
the failure when both are negative, and evaluate $\sqrt{-1}\,\sqrt{-1}$ in the complex numbers.

**36.** Solve $\sqrt{x}=x-6$.
