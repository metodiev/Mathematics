# Radicals and Rational Exponents — Solutions

> [Tutorial](README.md) · [Exercises](exercises.md) · 36 worked solutions

Each solution states the definition or sign condition being used, shows every step, and finishes with
a numerical or algebraic check. For radical equations the check is performed in the original
equation, which is the only place an extraneous root can be detected.

## Beginner

### Solution 1

(a) $9^2=81$ and $9\ge0$, so $\sqrt{81}=9$.

(b) The index $3$ is odd, so a negative radicand is allowed. Since $(-4)^3=-64$,
$\sqrt[3]{-64}=-4$.

(c) $3^4=81$ and $3\ge0$, so $\sqrt[4]{81}=3$. The equation $y^4=81$ has solutions $\pm3$; the
expression has the value $3$.

### Solution 2

Look for the largest perfect square dividing $98$: $98=49\cdot2$. Therefore

$$
\sqrt{98}=\sqrt{49}\sqrt2=7\sqrt2 .
$$

Check: $(7\sqrt2)^2=49\cdot2=98$ and $7\sqrt2>0$.

### Solution 3

$108=36\cdot3$, so $\sqrt{108}=\sqrt{36}\sqrt3=6\sqrt3$. Check: $(6\sqrt3)^2=36\cdot3=108$.

### Solution 4

$$
\sqrt{20x^2}=\sqrt{4x^2\cdot5}=\sqrt{4}\sqrt{x^2}\sqrt5=2|x|\sqrt5 .
$$

The absolute value is required because $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$; for $x=-3$ the original is
$\sqrt{180}=6\sqrt5$, and $2|-3|\sqrt5=6\sqrt5$ ✓, while $2x\sqrt5=-6\sqrt5$ would be wrong.

### Solution 5

$$
\sqrt{16x^2y^4}=\sqrt{16}\sqrt{x^2}\sqrt{y^4}=4|x|\,y^2 .
$$

$y^2$ needs no absolute value because it is already non-negative. Check at $x=-2$, $y=3$: the
radicand is $16\cdot4\cdot81=5184$ and $\sqrt{5184}=72=4\cdot2\cdot9$ ✓.

### Solution 6

(a) The index is $3$ and the exponent is $5$:
$\sqrt[3]{x^5}=x^{5/3}$.

(b) $\sqrt[5]{x^3}=x^{3/5}$.

Both identities hold where the radical is defined; for even indices one requires $x\ge0$.

### Solution 7

(a) $x^{3/4}=\sqrt[4]{x^3}$: the denominator $4$ is the index, the numerator $3$ the power.

(b) $x^{-2/3}=\dfrac{1}{x^{2/3}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{x^2}}$.

### Solution 8

$$
125^{2/3}=\left(\sqrt[3]{125}\right)^2=5^2=25 .
$$

The other order gives $\sqrt[3]{125^2}=\sqrt[3]{15625}=25$; the first is easier. Check: $25^{3/2}=125$ ✓.

### Solution 9

$$
81^{-3/4}=\frac{1}{81^{3/4}}=\frac{1}{\left(\sqrt[4]{81}\right)^3}=\frac{1}{3^3}=\frac{1}{27} .
$$

Check: $\left(\frac{1}{27}\right)^{-4/3}=27^{4/3}=(\sqrt[3]{27})^4=81$ ✓.

### Solution 10

Like radicals: same index $2$, same radicand $7$. By the distributive law,

$$
4\sqrt7+5\sqrt7=(4+5)\sqrt7=9\sqrt7 .
$$

Check numerically: $4(2.6458)+5(2.6458)=10.5830+13.2290=23.8120=9\sqrt7$ ✓.

### Solution 11

Simplify first: $\sqrt{12}=2\sqrt3$ and $\sqrt{27}=3\sqrt3$. Then

$$
\sqrt{12}+\sqrt{27}=2\sqrt3+3\sqrt3=5\sqrt3 .
$$

Check: $3.4641+5.1962=8.6603=5\sqrt3$ ✓.

### Solution 12

Multiply numerator and denominator by $\sqrt3$, which is $1$ in the form $\frac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}$:

$$
\frac{5}{\sqrt3}=\frac{5\sqrt3}{\sqrt3\cdot\sqrt3}=\frac{5\sqrt3}{3}.
$$

Check: $\frac{5}{1.7321}=2.8868$ and $\frac{5(1.7321)}{3}=2.8868$ ✓.

## Intermediate

### Solution 13

This is a difference of squares with $A=5$, $B=\sqrt3$:

$$
(5+\sqrt3)(5-\sqrt3)=5^2-(\sqrt3)^2=25-3=22 .
$$

Check by direct expansion: $25-5\sqrt3+5\sqrt3-3=22$ ✓.

### Solution 14

$$
(3\sqrt2-2)^2=(3\sqrt2)^2-2(3\sqrt2)(2)+2^2=18-12\sqrt2+4=22-12\sqrt2 .
$$

Check numerically: $3\sqrt2-2=2.2426$ and $2.2426^2=5.0294$; $22-12\sqrt2=22-16.9706=5.0294$ ✓.

### Solution 15

Multiply by the conjugate $\sqrt{11}+\sqrt7$:

$$
\frac{4}{\sqrt{11}-\sqrt7}
=\frac{4(\sqrt{11}+\sqrt7)}{(\sqrt{11})^2-(\sqrt7)^2}
=\frac{4(\sqrt{11}+\sqrt7)}{11-7}
=\sqrt{11}+\sqrt7 .
$$

Check: $\frac{4}{3.3166-2.6458}=\frac{4}{0.6708}=5.9628$ and $3.3166+2.6458=5.9624$ ✓ (rounding).

### Solution 16

Extract perfect cubes: $24=8\cdot3$ and $81=27\cdot3$, so

$$
\sqrt[3]{24}+\sqrt[3]{81}=2\sqrt[3]{3}+3\sqrt[3]{3}=5\sqrt[3]{3}.
$$

Check numerically: $2.8845+4.3267=7.2112$ and $5\sqrt[3]{3}=5(1.4422)=7.2112$ ✓.

### Solution 17

$$
\left(\sqrt[3]{3}\right)^4=\left(\sqrt[3]{3}\right)^3\cdot\sqrt[3]{3}=3\sqrt[3]{3}.
$$

In exponent notation $3^{4/3}=3^{1}\cdot3^{1/3}=3\sqrt[3]{3}$. Check: $(3\sqrt[3]3)^3=27\cdot3=81$ and
$(\sqrt[3]3)^{12}=3^4=81$ ✓.

### Solution 18

(a) Write $x^4y^6=(x^2)^2(y^3)^2$. Then $\sqrt{x^4y^6}=x^2|y^3|=x^2|y|^3$; the factor $x^2$ needs no
absolute value because it is non-negative.

(b) The index $3$ is odd, so no absolute value is needed: $\sqrt[3]{x^6y^9}=x^2y^3$.

Check at $x=-2$, $y=-3$: (a) gives $4\cdot27=108$ and $\sqrt{16\cdot729}=\sqrt{11664}=108$ ✓; (b)
gives $4\cdot(-27)=-108$ and $\sqrt[3]{64\cdot(-19683)}=\sqrt[3]{-1259712}=-108$ ✓.

### Solution 19

*Domain and sign condition.* The left side is a principal square root, hence non-negative, so the
right side satisfies $x-1\ge0$, i.e. $x\ge1$; the radicand $3x+1\ge0$ is then automatic.

*Square.* $3x+1=(x-1)^2=x^2-2x+1$.

*Solve.* $x^2-5x=0$, so $x(x-5)=0$ and $x=0$ or $x=5$.

*Apply the condition.* $x=0<1$ is rejected.

*Check.* $x=5$: $\sqrt{16}=4$ and $5-1=4$ ✓. For $x=0$: $\sqrt1=1$ but $0-1=-1$, so it is
extraneous. Solution set $\{5\}$.

### Solution 20

*Domain and sign condition.* The right side $x+1$ must be non-negative, so $x\ge-1$; the radicand
$x+7\ge0$ is then automatic.

*Square.* $x+7=(x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1$.

*Solve.* $x^2+x-6=0$, so $(x+3)(x-2)=0$ and $x=-3$ or $x=2$.

*Apply the condition.* $x=-3<-1$ is rejected.

*Check.* $x=2$: $\sqrt9=3$ and $2+1=3$ ✓. For $x=-3$ the left side is $2$ and the right side $-2$, so
it is indeed extraneous. Solution set $\{2\}$.

### Solution 21

Both sides are principal square roots, hence non-negative wherever they are defined, so squaring is
reversible on the common domain $x\ge\frac23$:

$$
3x-2=x+6 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2x=8 \;\Longrightarrow\; x=4 .
$$

*Check.* $\sqrt{12-2}=\sqrt{10}$ and $\sqrt{4+6}=\sqrt{10}$ ✓. Solution set $\{4\}$.

### Solution 22

*Distance.* With $(x_1,y_1)=(2,1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)=(5,5)$,

$$
d=\sqrt{(5-2)^2+(5-1)^2}=\sqrt{9+16}=\sqrt{25}=5 .
$$

*Midpoint.* $\left(\frac{2+5}{2},\frac{1+5}{2}\right)=\left(\frac72,3\right)$.

*Check.* The midpoint is at distance $\sqrt{(1.5)^2+2^2}=\sqrt{2.25+4}=2.5$ from $(2,1)$, half of
$5$ ✓.

## Advanced

### Solution 23

*Isolate.* $\sqrt{2x+7}=2+\sqrt{x+3}$. The right side is positive, so squaring preserves the solution
set at this step.

*Square once.*

$$
2x+7=4+4\sqrt{x+3}+(x+3),
$$

so $2x+7=x+7+4\sqrt{x+3}$, hence $x=4\sqrt{x+3}$.

*Sign condition and second square.* The right side $4\sqrt{x+3}$ is non-negative, so $x\ge0$. Squaring:

$$
x^2=16(x+3) \;\Longrightarrow\; x^2-16x-48=0 .
$$

*Solve.* The discriminant is $256+192=448=64\cdot7$, so

$$
x=\frac{16\pm8\sqrt7}{2}=8\pm4\sqrt7 .
$$

The value $8-4\sqrt7\approx-2.58$ is negative and is rejected; $8+4\sqrt7\approx18.58$ is admissible.

*Check.* For $x=8+4\sqrt7$ we have $x+3=11+4\sqrt7=(2+\sqrt7)^2$, so
$\sqrt{x+3}=2+\sqrt7$ and $4\sqrt{x+3}=8+4\sqrt7=x$ ✓. Also
$2x+7=23+8\sqrt7=(2+\sqrt{x+3})^2$ by construction. The solution set is $\{8+4\sqrt7\}$.

### Solution 24

*Isolate.* $\sqrt{x+5}=5-\sqrt x$. The right side must be non-negative, so $\sqrt x\le5$, i.e.
$0\le x\le25$.

*Square.*

$$
x+5=25-10\sqrt x+x \;\Longrightarrow\; 5=25-10\sqrt x \;\Longrightarrow\; 10\sqrt x=20
\;\Longrightarrow\;\sqrt x=2 .
$$

Hence $x=4$, which satisfies $0\le x\le25$.

*Check.* $\sqrt9+\sqrt4=3+2=5$ ✓. Solution set $\{4\}$.

### Solution 25

Cubing is reversible because $t\mapsto t^3$ is strictly increasing on $\mathbb{R}$; no sign condition
is needed.

$$
\sqrt[3]{3x+2}=2 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3x+2=2^3=8 \;\Longrightarrow\; 3x=6
\;\Longrightarrow\; x=2 .
$$

*Check.* $3(2)+2=8$ and $\sqrt[3]{8}=2$ ✓. Solution set $\{2\}$.

### Solution 26

*Sign condition.* The right side $x-1$ must be non-negative, so $x\ge1$.

*Square.* $x+1=(x-1)^2=x^2-2x+1$.

*Solve.* $x^2-3x=0$, so $x(x-3)=0$ and $x=0$ or $x=3$.

*Apply the condition.* $x=0<1$ is rejected.

*Check.* $x=3$: $\sqrt4=2$ and $3-1=2$ ✓. For $x=0$: $\sqrt1=1$ but $0-1=-1$, so it is extraneous.
Solution set $\{3\}$.

### Solution 27

Use $u^3-v^3=(u-v)(u^2+uv+v^2)$ with $u=\sqrt[3]{3}$ and $v=1$; the multiplier is
$u^2+u+1=\sqrt[3]{9}+\sqrt[3]{3}+1$:

$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt[3]{3}-1}
=\frac{\sqrt[3]{9}+\sqrt[3]{3}+1}{\left(\sqrt[3]{3}-1\right)\left(\sqrt[3]{9}+\sqrt[3]{3}+1\right)}
=\frac{\sqrt[3]{9}+\sqrt[3]{3}+1}{3-1}
=\frac{\sqrt[3]{9}+\sqrt[3]{3}+1}{2}.
$$

*Check numerically.* $\frac{1}{1.44225-1}=2.26118$ and $\frac{2.08008+1.44225+1}{2}=2.26117$ ✓.

### Solution 28

Write the radicand in the form $a+2\sqrt b$: $11+6\sqrt2=11+2\sqrt{18}$, so $a=11$ and $b=18$. Then

$$
c=\sqrt{a^2-4b}=\sqrt{121-72}=\sqrt{49}=7,
$$

and Proposition 8.9 gives

$$
\sqrt{11+6\sqrt2}=\sqrt{\frac{11+7}{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{11-7}{2}}=\sqrt9+\sqrt2=3+\sqrt2 .
$$

*Check.* $(3+\sqrt2)^2=9+6\sqrt2+2=11+6\sqrt2$ ✓, and $3+\sqrt2>0$, so it is the principal root.

### Solution 29

$$
x^{2/3}=\left(\sqrt[3]{x}\right)^2=4 \;\Longrightarrow\; \sqrt[3]{x}=\pm2
\;\Longrightarrow\; x=(\pm2)^3=\pm8 .
$$

Both signs are admissible because the exponent $\frac23$ has an odd denominator, so negative bases
are allowed.

*Check.* $8^{2/3}=(\sqrt[3]{8})^2=4$ ✓; $(-8)^{2/3}=(\sqrt[3]{-8})^2=(-2)^2=4$ ✓. Solution set
$\{-8,8\}$.

### Solution 30

Compute the three distances:

$$
AB=\sqrt{(3+1)^2+(5-2)^2}=\sqrt{16+9}=5,
$$
$$
BC=\sqrt{(7-3)^2+(8-5)^2}=\sqrt{16+9}=5,
$$
$$
AC=\sqrt{(7+1)^2+(8-2)^2}=\sqrt{64+36}=10 .
$$

Since $AB+BC=5+5=10=AC$, equality holds in the triangle inequality, which happens exactly when the
three points are collinear with $B$ between $A$ and $C$.

*Independent check by slopes.* The slope of $AB$ is $\frac{5-2}{3-(-1)}=\frac34$ and the slope of
$BC$ is $\frac{8-5}{7-3}=\frac34$. Equal slopes through the common point $B$ mean the points lie on
one line. ✓

## Challenge

### Solution 31

*Domain.* $x\ge2$.

*Isolate and record the sign condition.* $\sqrt{2x+3}=4-\sqrt{x-2}$. The right side must be
non-negative, so $\sqrt{x-2}\le4$, i.e. $x\le18$.

*Square once.*

$$
2x+3=16-8\sqrt{x-2}+(x-2)=x+14-8\sqrt{x-2},
$$

so $x-11=-8\sqrt{x-2}$, that is $11-x=8\sqrt{x-2}$. This requires $x\le11$.

*Square again.*

$$
(11-x)^2=64(x-2) \;\Longrightarrow\; 121-22x+x^2=64x-128
\;\Longrightarrow\; x^2-86x+249=0 .
$$

*Solve.* The discriminant is $86^2-4\cdot249=7396-996=6400=80^2$, so

$$
x=\frac{86\pm80}{2}=83 \quad\text{or}\quad x=3 .
$$

*Apply the conditions.* $x=83$ violates $x\le11$ (and also $x\le18$); it is extraneous. The candidate
$x=3$ satisfies all conditions.

*Check.* $x=3$: $\sqrt9+\sqrt1=3+1=4$ ✓. The solution set is $\{3\}$.

Why the extraneous root appears: squaring $11-x=8\sqrt{x-2}$ also solves $x-11=8\sqrt{x-2}$, and
$x=83$ satisfies that "sign-reversed" equation. The condition $11-x\ge0$ is exactly what excludes it.

### Solution 32

Use $\sqrt{x^2-6x+9}=\sqrt{(x-3)^2}=|x-3|$. The equation becomes

$$
|x-3|=3-x .
$$

The right side must be non-negative, so $3-x\ge0$, i.e. $x\le3$. For such $x$, $x-3\le0$, hence
$|x-3|=-(x-3)=3-x$, and the equation holds identically.

For $x>3$ we have $|x-3|=x-3$ while $3-x<0$, so the equation fails.

Therefore the solution set is $(-\infty,3]$, an interval rather than a finite set.

*Checks.* $x=3$: $0=0$ ✓. $x=0$: $\sqrt9=3=3-0$ ✓. $x=5$: $\sqrt{25-30+9}=\sqrt4=2$ but
$3-5=-2$ ✗.

### Solution 33

Denest each term.

*First term.* $4+2\sqrt3=(\sqrt3+1)^2$, since $3+2\sqrt3+1=4+2\sqrt3$. Both $\sqrt3+1>0$, so
$\sqrt{4+2\sqrt3}=\sqrt3+1$.

*Second term.* $4-2\sqrt3=(\sqrt3-1)^2$, since $3-2\sqrt3+1=4-2\sqrt3$. Both $\sqrt3-1>0$ (because
$\sqrt3>1$), so $\sqrt{4-2\sqrt3}=\sqrt3-1$.

*Sum.*

$$
\sqrt{4+2\sqrt3}+\sqrt{4-2\sqrt3}=(\sqrt3+1)+(\sqrt3-1)=2\sqrt3 .
$$

*Check numerically.* $\sqrt{7.4641}=2.7321$ and $\sqrt{0.5359}=0.7321$; their sum is $3.4641=2\sqrt3$
✓.

The justification of each denesting step is Proposition 8.2 together with the positivity of the
candidate: $\sqrt{t^2}=|t|$, and the chosen $t$ is positive.

### Solution 34

Suppose $\sqrt n=\frac pq$ with $p,q$ positive integers and $\gcd(p,q)=1$. Squaring,

$$
n=\frac{p^2}{q^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; p^2=nq^2 .
$$

Hence $q^2$ divides $p^2$. But $\gcd(p,q)=1$ implies $\gcd(p^2,q^2)=1$: any prime dividing both
$p^2$ and $q^2$ would divide both $p$ and $q$. The only positive divisor of $p^2$ that is coprime to
$p^2$ is $1$, so $q^2=1$ and $q=1$.

Therefore $\sqrt n=p$ is an integer. $\blacksquare$

*Remark.* The proof shows more: if $n$ is a positive integer and $\sqrt n$ is rational, then $n$ is a
perfect square. In particular $\sqrt2$ is irrational, since $2$ is not a perfect square.

### Solution 35

The identity $\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}$ is proved (Proposition 8.3) by observing that
$\sqrt a\sqrt b\ge0$ and that its square is $ab$. Both ingredients use $a,b\ge0$: for negative
radicands the principal square roots are not real numbers, so the argument does not apply.

*Demonstration with negatives.* Take $a=b=-1$. The left side $\sqrt{-1}\sqrt{-1}$ is not a real
number, while the right side $\sqrt{(-1)(-1)}=\sqrt1=1$ is real. Blindly applying the rule would
assert that a non-real quantity equals $1$.

*In the complex numbers.* With $i^2=-1$ and $\sqrt{-1}=i$,

$$
\sqrt{-1}\,\sqrt{-1}=i\cdot i=i^2=-1,
\qquad\text{while}\qquad
\sqrt{(-1)(-1)}=\sqrt1=1 .
$$

The two differ by a sign, which is exactly the information that the rule discards when the radicands
are negative: writing $\sqrt{-1}=i$ introduces a factor $i$ whose square is $-1$. The correct
statement in $\mathbb{C}$ is $\sqrt a\sqrt b=\pm\sqrt{ab}$, with the sign determined by the chosen
branches.

### Solution 36

*Domain and sign condition.* The left side $\sqrt x$ requires $x\ge0$ and is non-negative; the right
side $x-6$ must therefore satisfy $x\ge6$.

*Square.* $x=(x-6)^2=x^2-12x+36$, so $x^2-13x+36=0$.

*Solve.* $(x-4)(x-9)=0$, so $x=4$ or $x=9$.

*Apply the condition.* $x=4<6$ is rejected; equivalently $\sqrt4=2\ne4-6=-2$.

*Check.* $x=9$: $\sqrt9=3$ and $9-6=3$ ✓. The solution set is $\{9\}$.
