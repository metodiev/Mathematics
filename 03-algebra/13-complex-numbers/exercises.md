# Complex Numbers — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 40 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely: show every algebraic step, state which theorem or identity you use,
and finish with a check (substitute back, compare moduli, or verify a special value). Give exact
answers in standard form $a+bi$ unless a polar form is requested; angles are in radians. Full
solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md); do not open that file until you have attempted the
problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Write $(3+2i)+(1-5i)$ in standard form.

**2.** Multiply: $(2+i)(3-4i)$.

**3.** Simplify $i^{17}+i^{23}$.

**4.** Compute $(2i)^3$.

**5.** For $z = 5-12i$, state $\bar z$, $\lvert z\rvert$ and $z\bar z$.

**6.** Write $\dfrac{3+i}{1-i}$ in standard form.

**7.** Solve $x^2+9 = 0$ over $\mathbb{C}$.

**8.** Solve $x^2-4x+13 = 0$ over $\mathbb{C}$ and verify Vieta's formulas.

**9.** Write $1+i$ in polar form with the principal argument.

**10.** Write $-2$ in polar form with the principal argument.

**11.** Compute $\lvert(3+4i)(5-12i)\rvert$ without multiplying the numbers.

**12.** Compute $2\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{6}+i\sin\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\cdot 3\left(\cos\frac{\pi}{3}+i\sin\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$
and give the answer in Cartesian form.

## Intermediate

**13.** Write $\dfrac{1+i\sqrt3}{1-i\sqrt3}$ in standard form.

**14.** Compute $(1+i)^4+(1-i)^4$.

**15.** Solve $z^2-6z+25 = 0$.

**16.** Find both square roots of $5+12i$ by comparing real and imaginary parts.

**17.** Compute $(1-i)^{10}$ using De Moivre's theorem, then check by repeated squaring.

**18.** Find all cube roots of $27$ and sketch their position.

**19.** Find all fourth roots of $-4$ in standard form.

**20.** Solve $z^3 = 1$, list the roots, and hence factor $z^3-1$ into real factors.

**21.** Describe geometrically the sets (a) $\lvert z-2i\rvert = 3$ and (b) $\lvert z-1\rvert = \lvert z+1\rvert$.

**22.** Write $-\sqrt3+i$ in polar form and compute its sixth power.

**23.** Find the modulus and the principal argument of $(1+i)(\sqrt3-i)$, and confirm the argument
with the exact value of $\tan\frac{\pi}{12}$.

**24.** Solve $z^2-(3+2i)z+(1+3i) = 0$ over $\mathbb{C}$.

## Advanced

**25.** Solve $z^4+z^2+1 = 0$ and identify the four solutions as roots of unity.

**26.** Find every positive integer $n$ for which $(1+i)^n$ is real, and give the value in each case.

**27.** Prove the parallelogram law $\lvert z+w\rvert^2+\lvert z-w\rvert^2 = 2\lvert z\rvert^2+2\lvert w\rvert^2$
and explain why the name is appropriate.

**28.** Let $\lvert z\rvert = \lvert w\rvert = 1$ with $1+zw \neq 0$. Prove that
$\dfrac{z+w}{1+zw}$ is a real number.

**29.** Prove that for every $z \in \mathbb{C}$,
$\lvert \operatorname{Re}z\rvert+\lvert \operatorname{Im}z\rvert \le \sqrt2\,\lvert z\rvert$,
and determine exactly when equality holds.

**30.** Prove that a polynomial with real coefficients has nonreal roots in conjugate pairs, and
deduce that every real polynomial of odd degree has at least one real root.

**31.** Let $\omega = \cos\frac{2\pi}{n}+i\sin\frac{2\pi}{n}$ with $n \ge 2$. Prove
$\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(1-\omega^k\right) = n$.

**32.** Solve $z^2 = \bar z$ over $\mathbb{C}$.

**33.** Solve the system $\lvert z\rvert = 1$ and $z+\bar z = 1$ simultaneously.

**34.** An AC circuit has impedance $Z = 4+3i$ ohms and is driven by a voltage $V = 10$ volts.
Find $\lvert Z\rvert$, the phase angle of $Z$, the current $I = V/Z$, the magnitude $\lvert I\rvert$,
and the phase difference between current and voltage.

**35.** Find the complex multiplier that rotates by $120^\circ$ clockwise and triples the distance
from the origin. Apply it to $1+i\sqrt3$ and interpret the result.

**36.** Prove that $\mathbb{C}$ has no zero divisors: if $z_1z_2 = 0$ then $z_1 = 0$ or $z_2 = 0$.
Deduce that a quadratic equation over $\mathbb{C}$ has at most two roots.

## Challenge

**37.** Let $\omega = \cos\frac{2\pi}{7}+i\sin\frac{2\pi}{7}$. Prove that
$\cos\frac{2\pi}{7}+\cos\frac{4\pi}{7}+\cos\frac{6\pi}{7} = -\frac12$.

**38.** Using the fifth roots of unity, prove that $\cos 72^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt5-1}{4}$ and
$\cos 144^\circ = -\dfrac{\sqrt5+1}{4}$.

**39.** Let $z_1,z_2,z_3$ be distinct complex numbers and let $\omega = \cos\frac{2\pi}{3}+i\sin\frac{2\pi}{3}$.
(a) Prove that if $z_1+\omega z_2+\omega^2 z_3 = 0$ then the three points are the vertices of an
equilateral triangle. (b) Prove the identity
$$z_1^2+z_2^2+z_3^2 = z_1z_2+z_2z_3+z_3z_1$$
holds exactly when the triangle is equilateral.

**40.** The $n$-th roots of unity are the vertices of a regular $n$-gon inscribed in the unit circle.
Prove that its area is $\dfrac{n}{2}\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{n}$, and check the formula for $n=4$ and $n=6$.
