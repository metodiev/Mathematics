# Factoring — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 36 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely: state the method you are using and why it applies, write every
algebraic step, and check the result by expanding. Unless a problem says otherwise, factor
**completely over $\mathbb{R}$**. When a polynomial cannot be factored further, say so and justify it
(for instance by a discriminant argument). Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md); do not
open that file until you have attempted the problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Factor out the greatest common factor: $15x^3 - 25x^2$.

**2.** Factor out the greatest common factor: $6a^2b - 9ab^2 + 3ab$.

**3.** Factor $x^2 + 7x + 12$.

**4.** Factor $x^2 - 9x + 20$.

**5.** Factor $x^2 - x - 12$.

**6.** Factor $x^2 - 49$.

**7.** Factor $4x^2 - 25$.

**8.** Factor $x^2 + 10x + 25$.

**9.** Factor $x^2 - 12x + 36$.

**10.** Factor $x^3 - 8$.

**11.** Factor $x^3 + 27$.

**12.** Factor by grouping: $xy + 3x + 2y + 6$.

**14.** Factor $6x^2 - 7x - 5$.

## Advanced

**25.** Factor $x^3 + 2x^2 - 9x - 18$ completely.

**26.** Factor $x^4 + 64$ completely over $\mathbb{R}$. (Hint: write it as a difference of squares.)

**27.** Factor $\left(x+2\right)^2 - 9\left(x+2\right) + 20$ completely.

**28.** Factor $6x^2 - 5xy - 6y^2$.

**29.** Prove the following: if $a, b, c \in \mathbb{Z}$ with $a \neq 0$ and $ax^2 + bx + c$ factors as
$(px+q)(rx+s)$ with $p, q, r, s \in \mathbb{Z}$, then there are integers $u, v$ with $u + v = b$ and
$uv = ac$. Deduce that $4x^2 + 6x + 5$ has no factorisation with integer coefficients.

**30.** Factor $2x^5 - 32x$ completely over $\mathbb{R}$.

**31.** Factor $x^4 - 7x^2 + 12$ completely over $\mathbb{R}$ (the factors are not all rational).

**32.** Given that $p(1) = 0$ for $p(x) = x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$, factor $p$ completely and state its
roots.

## Challenge

**33.** Show that $x^4 + 1$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$, and give its complete factorisation over
$\mathbb{R}$. Your answer must explain why no factorisation over $\mathbb{Q}$ is possible.

**34.** Factor $x^8 - 1$ completely over $\mathbb{R}$. Then state how many real roots it has and
identify them.

**35.** Find every integer $k$ for which $x^2 + kx + 12$ factors as a product of two polynomials with
integer coefficients. Justify that your list is complete.

**36.** Prove that $n^4 + 4$ is composite for every integer $n \geq 2$. Then explain why the same
argument fails at $n = 1$.
