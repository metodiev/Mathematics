# Advanced Algebra — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 38 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely: show every step, name the theorem you use, and verify the answer — by
recombining a decomposition, substituting into the original system, or checking a small case of a
sum. Answers to polynomial equations should be given exactly, as roots of the stated polynomial with
their multiplicities. Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md); do not open that file until
you have attempted the problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Divide $x^3-5x^2+7x-2$ by $x-2$, stating the quotient and the remainder.

**2.** Find the remainder when $2x^3-x^2+4x-3$ is divided by $x+1$, using the remainder theorem.

**3.** Factor $x^2-7x+12$ and state its roots.

**4.** State the multiplicity of each root of $P(x) = (x-1)^3(x+2)^2(x-5)$, and the degree of $P$.

**5.** Write down the *shape* of the partial fraction decomposition of
$\dfrac{x+1}{(x-1)(x+4)}$; do not solve for the coefficients.

**6.** Decompose $\dfrac{5x+1}{(x-1)(x+2)}$ into partial fractions.

**7.** Find the 10th term of the arithmetic sequence $3, 7, 11, \dots$.

**8.** Compute $1+2+\cdots+100$.

**9.** Find the 8th term of the geometric sequence $2, 6, 18, \dots$.

**10.** Compute $1+\dfrac12+\dfrac14+\dfrac18+\cdots$.

**11.** Compute $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{4}\left(k^2-1\right)$.

**12.** Solve the system $x+y = 5$, $xy = 6$ by finding a quadratic with roots $x$ and $y$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Solve $x^3+2x^2-5x-6 = 0$ using the rational root theorem.

**14.** Find all rational roots of $3x^3-5x^2-11x-3 = 0$.

**15.** Apply Descartes' rule of signs to $P(x) = x^4-3x^3+2x^2+x-1$: how many positive and how many
negative real roots can it have?

**16.** Decompose $\dfrac{x+5}{(x-1)^2}$ into partial fractions.

**17.** Decompose $\dfrac{3x^2+2x+1}{x(x^2+1)}$ into partial fractions.

**18.** Decompose $\dfrac{x^3+1}{x^2-1}$, dividing first if necessary.

**19.** Solve the system $x^2+y^2 = 25$, $x+y = 7$.

**20.** Solve the system $x-y = 2$, $x^2-y^2 = 16$.

**21.** Find the sum of all multiples of $7$ between $1$ and $200$.

**22.** For the geometric series $3+\dfrac32+\dfrac34+\cdots$, find (a) the sum to infinity and
(b) the partial sum $S_6$.

**23.** Prove that $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k(k+1)} = \frac{n}{n+1}$ by telescoping, and
evaluate the sum for $n = 99$.

**24.** Solve by elimination
$$\begin{cases}x+y+z = 6,\\ 2x-y+z = 3,\\ x+2y-z = 2.\end{cases}$$

## Advanced

**25.** Given that $x = 2$ is a double root of $x^4-6x^3+9x^2+4x-12$, find all roots with their
multiplicities.

**26.** A polynomial with real coefficients has $2$ as a root of multiplicity $2$, $-1$ as a root of
multiplicity $3$, and $1+i$ as a root. State the smallest possible degree and write the polynomial in
factored form with an unknown leading coefficient.

**27.** Use Descartes' rule of signs to determine the number of positive and negative real roots of
$P(x) = x^3+x+1$, and deduce how many real and how many nonreal roots it has.

**28.** Decompose $\dfrac{3x^2+x}{(x+1)(x^2+1)}$ into partial fractions and verify the result.

**29.** Solve the symmetric system $x^2+y^2 = 10$, $xy = 3$ in full.

**30.** The sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic sequence is $S_n = 3n^2+5n$. Find the first
term and the common difference.

**31.** A geometric series has first term $5$ and sum to infinity $20$. Find the common ratio and the
partial sum $S_4$.

**32.** Prove that $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k+1)} = \frac{n}{2n+1}$, and state
the limit as $n\to\infty$.

**33.** Solve and classify
$$\begin{cases}x+2y-z = 3,\\ 2x+4y-2z = 7,\\ x-y+z = 0.\end{cases}$$

**34.** Determine the value of $k$ for which
$$\begin{cases}x+y+z = 2,\\ 2x+2y+2z = k,\\ x-y = 0\end{cases}$$
has infinitely many solutions, and describe the solution set in that case.

## Challenge

**35.** Solve the symmetric system $x^3+y^3 = 9$, $x+y = 3$.

**36.** Find every real number $a$ for which the polynomial $x^3-3x^2+a$ has a repeated root, and
give the factorisation in each case.

**37.** For $P(x) = x^5-x-1$: prove that $P$ has no rational root, apply Descartes' rule to bound the
number of positive and negative real roots, and explain why $P$ is nevertheless not solvable by
radicals.

**38.** The roots of $x^3-6x^2+11x-6$ are $1, 2, 3$. Without expanding the reversed polynomial, find
the monic cubic whose roots are the reciprocals $\frac11, \frac12, \frac13$, and verify it with
Vieta's formulas.
