# Solid Geometry — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 36 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely: write the formula you use before substituting, keep units on every line,
and state whether you are computing volume (cubic units) or surface area (square units). Figures are not
drawn to scale. Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md); do not open that file until you have
attempted the problems.

## Beginner

**1.** A rectangular box measures $5\ \mathrm{cm}\times4\ \mathrm{cm}\times3\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find its
volume, its total surface area, and the length of its space diagonal.

**2.** A cube has total surface area $150\ \mathrm{cm^2}$. Find its edge, its volume and its space
diagonal.

**3.** A cube has volume $343\ \mathrm{cm^3}$. Find its edge and its total surface area.

**4.** A right prism has as base a right triangle with legs $3\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $4\ \mathrm{cm}$, and its
height is $10\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find the base area, the volume and the total surface area.

**5.** A right circular cylinder has radius $5\ \mathrm{cm}$ and height $7\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find its volume,
its lateral area and its total surface area.

**6.** A right circular cylinder has diameter $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ and height $12\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find its
volume and its total surface area.

**7.** A right circular cone has base radius $6\ \mathrm{cm}$ and height $8\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find its slant
height, its volume, its lateral area and its total surface area.

**8.** A sphere has radius $7\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find its surface area and the volume of the ball it bounds.

**9.** A ball has volume $288\pi\ \mathrm{cm^3}$. Find its radius and its surface area.

**10.** A right prism has a regular hexagonal base of side $2\ \mathrm{cm}$ and height $5\ \mathrm{cm}$.
Find the base area, the volume and the total surface area.

**11.** A convex polyhedron has $8$ vertices and $6$ faces. Use Euler's formula to find the number of
edges, and name the solid if all its faces are congruent squares.

**12.** A convex polyhedron has $6$ vertices and $12$ edges. Use Euler's formula to find the number of
faces, and name the solid if all its faces are equilateral triangles.

**13.** A square pyramid has a square base and a single apex. Count its vertices, edges and faces, and
verify Euler's formula.

## Intermediate

**14.** A regular square pyramid has base side $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ and slant height $13\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find
its height, its volume and its total surface area.

**15.** A right circular cone has slant height $13\ \mathrm{cm}$ and base radius $5\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find its
height, its volume and its total surface area.

**16.** A sphere of radius $3\ \mathrm{cm}$ is inscribed in a cube of edge $6\ \mathrm{cm}$ (the sphere
touches all six faces). What fraction of the cube's volume does the sphere occupy?

**17.** A cube is inscribed in a sphere of radius $3\sqrt3\ \mathrm{cm}$ (all eight vertices lie on the
sphere). Find the cube's edge, its volume, the sphere's volume, and the ratio of the two volumes.

**18.** A conical frustum has height $3\ \mathrm{cm}$ and base radii $4\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $2\ \mathrm{cm}$.
Find its volume and its lateral area.

**19.** The lateral surface of a right cone unrolls into a semicircle of radius $10\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find the
cone's base radius, height and volume.

**20.** A cylindrical water tank has radius $2\ \mathrm{m}$ and depth $3\ \mathrm{m}$. Find its volume in
cubic metres and its capacity in litres.

**21.** A convex polyhedron has $6$ vertices, and all its $8$ faces are triangles. Find the number of edges
in two ways (from the faces, and from Euler's formula), and name the solid.

**22.** A prism has $12$ vertices and $8$ faces. Find the number of edges, the number of sides of its base,
and the name of the prism.

**23.** A net of a cube consists of six unit squares joined edge to edge. Explain why no net of a cube can
contain five squares in a single straight row.

**24.** A circular sector of radius $12\ \mathrm{cm}$ and angle $120^\circ$ is rolled into a cone. Find the
cone's base radius, height and volume.

**25.** A solid consists of a cylinder of radius $3\ \mathrm{cm}$ and height $10\ \mathrm{cm}$ with a cone
of the same radius and height $4\ \mathrm{cm}$ on top. Find the volume and the total external surface area,
including the flat bottom.

## Advanced

**26.** Prove that a triangular prism can be partitioned into three tetrahedra of equal volume, and deduce
that a pyramid with base area $B$ and height $h$ has volume $\tfrac13Bh$.

**27.** Using Cavalieri's principle, prove that an oblique cylinder has the same volume as a right cylinder
with the same base radius and the same height.

**28.** A closed box has a square base of side $x$ and height $h$, and volume $V$. Show that its surface
area is at least $6V^{2/3}$, with equality only for the cube. (You may use AM–GM.)

**29.** Prove that there is no Platonic solid with hexagonal faces, and no Platonic solid whose faces have
$p\ge7$ sides. In each case say exactly which inequality fails.

**30.** A regular tetrahedron has edge $a$. Show that its height is $a\sqrt{2/3}$ and that its volume is
$\dfrac{a^3}{6\sqrt2}$.

**31.** Show that a regular octahedron of edge $a$ is the union of two square pyramids, and deduce that its
volume is $\dfrac{\sqrt2}{3}a^3$.

**32.** Let a plane cut a cube. Prove that the section is a polygon with at most six sides, and that it
cannot be a regular pentagon. (It can be a regular hexagon; you need not prove that.)

## Challenge

**33.** Derive the volume of a ball of radius $R$ from Cavalieri's principle: compare the hemisphere with
the cylinder of radius $R$ and height $R$ minus the cone of radius $R$ and height $R$ whose apex is at the
centre of the cylinder's base. Write out both cross-sectional areas at height $y$ and justify the
comparison.

**34.** A square frame is made from a slab with a square hole cut through it, so that the solid has a
tunnel. After splitting the top and bottom faces into four trapezoids each, it has $16$ vertices, $32$
edges and $16$ faces. (a) Compute $V-E+F$. (b) Compute $V-E+F$ for two regular tetrahedra glued at a single
vertex. (c) Explain, in each case, which hypothesis of Euler's theorem fails.

**35.** A hemisphere of radius $R$ and a cone of base radius $R$ and height $R$ stand on the same plane.
(a) Which has the greater volume, and by what factor? (b) Find the height $h$ of a cone of base radius $R$
whose volume equals that of the hemisphere. (c) Explain the answers using Cavalieri's comparison from
Section 7.5.

**36.** A right circular cylinder is inscribed in a sphere of radius $R$, so that its two rim circles lie on
the sphere. Find the radius and height of the cylinder of maximum volume, and that maximum volume. Verify
that the answer is less than the sphere's volume.
