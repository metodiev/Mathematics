# Triangles — Exercises

> Companion to [the tutorial](README.md). Attempt every problem before opening
> [the solutions](solutions.md).

Thirty-six problems, numbered continuously and grouped by difficulty. Starred problems ($\star$) are longer.
Nothing here needs an idea beyond the tutorial; the theorem numbers refer to it.

## Beginner

**1.** Classify each triangle both by its sides and by its angles: (a) sides $7, 7, 7$; (b) sides $6, 8, 10$;
(c) sides $5, 5, 8$; (d) sides $9, 11, 13$.

**2.** Two angles of a triangle are given. Find the third angle and classify the triangle by its angles:
(a) $37^\circ$ and $58^\circ$; (b) $90^\circ$ and $45^\circ$; (c) $110^\circ$ and $25^\circ$;
(d) $60^\circ$ and $60^\circ$.

**3.** In an isosceles triangle, find the two missing angles: (a) the vertex angle is $40^\circ$; (b) each
base angle is $65^\circ$; (c) each base angle is $80^\circ$.

**4.** Decide which of these triples can be the side lengths of a triangle: (a) $3, 4, 7$; (b) $5, 6, 10$;
(c) $2, 2, 5$; (d) $7, 9, 13$; (e) $1, 2, 3$.

**5.** Find the missing side of each right triangle: (a) legs $5$ and $12$, hypotenuse?; (b) legs $7$ and
$24$, hypotenuse?; (c) hypotenuse $26$ and one leg $10$, other leg?; (d) legs $1$ and $1$, hypotenuse?

**6.** Use the special triangles: (a) a $45^\circ$–$45^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle has leg $9$, find the
hypotenuse; (b) a $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle has shortest side $4$, find the other two sides;
(c) a $45^\circ$–$45^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle has hypotenuse $12$, find the legs; (d) a
$30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle has hypotenuse $18$, find the legs.

**7.** Find the area: (a) a triangle with base $14$ and height $9$; (b) a triangle with base $6.4$ and
height $2.5$; (c) a right triangle with legs $9$ and $12$.

**8.** The angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 4$. Find the three angles and classify the
triangle.

**9.** In $\triangle ABC$, $A = 52^\circ$ and $B = 71^\circ$. (a) Find the interior angle at $C$. (b) The side
$BC$ is extended beyond $C$ to $D$; find the exterior angle $\angle ACD$.

**10.** State which congruence criterion applies — SAS, ASA, AAS, SSS or RHS — or write "none":
(a) $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$, $\angle B = \angle E$; (b) $\angle A = \angle D$, $\angle B = \angle E$,
$BC = EF$; (c) $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$, $CA = FD$; (d) $\angle B = \angle E = 90^\circ$, $AC = DF$,
$AB = DE$; (e) $AB = DE$, $AC = DF$, $\angle B = \angle E$.

**11.** An equilateral triangle has side $10$. Find its perimeter, its altitude and its area.

**12.** Name the centre described: (a) the point equidistant from the three vertices; (b) the point
equidistant from the three sides; (c) the intersection of the three medians; (d) the intersection of the
three altitudes.

## Intermediate

**13.** Use Heron's formula to find the area: (a) sides $7, 8, 9$; (b) sides $5, 5, 6$.

**14.** For the triangle with sides $6, 8, 10$: (a) compute the area in two different ways; (b) find the
semiperimeter and the inradius.

**15.** In $\triangle ABC$, the point $D$ lies on $AB$ with $AD = 3$ and $DB = 5$, and the point $E$ lies on
$AC$ with $DE \parallel BC$. (a) If $AE = 4$, find $EC$. (b) Find the ratio $DE/BC$. (c) If $DE = 6$, find
$BC$.

**16.** Two triangles are similar with similarity ratio $3 : 4$. The smaller has area $45$. Find the area of
the larger triangle and the ratio of the two perimeters.

**17.** In a triangle the median $AM$ has length $18$. (a) How far is the centroid $G$ from $A$, and how far
from $M$? (b) If instead $AG = 10$, how long is the median?

**18.** Find the area from two sides and the included angle: (a) $a = 8$, $b = 11$, $C = 30^\circ$;
(b) $a = 6$, $b = 10$, $C = 150^\circ$.

**19.** A triangle has two sides of lengths $10$ and $14$ and area $56$. Find every possible length of the
third side.

**20.** In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = AC$ and $D$ is the midpoint of $BC$. Prove that $AD \perp BC$.

**21.** In the quadrilateral $ABCD$, $AB = CD$ and $BC = AD$. Prove that $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, that is,
$AB \parallel CD$ and $BC \parallel AD$.

**22.** A ladder of length $13$ m rests against a vertical wall with its foot $5$ m from the wall. The foot
is then pulled out until it is $12$ m from the wall. How far does the top of the ladder slide down?

## Advanced

**23.** Prove that the sum of the three medians of a triangle is less than the perimeter.

**24.** Prove that the sum of the three medians is greater than $\tfrac34$ of the perimeter.

**25.** Prove that if two medians of a triangle are equal, then the triangle is isosceles.

**26.** Let $M$ be the midpoint of the side $BC$ of $\triangle ABC$. Prove that if $MA = MB = MC$, then
$\angle A = 90^\circ$.

**27.** *(Angle bisector theorem.)* In $\triangle ABC$ the bisector of $\angle A$ meets $BC$ at $D$. Prove
that $BD/DC = AB/AC$. *Hint:* draw through $C$ the line parallel to $AD$, and let it meet the line $AB$ at
$E$.

**28.** *(Apollonius.)* In $\triangle ABC$ the point $M$ is the midpoint of $BC$. Prove that
$AB^2 + AC^2 = 2\,AM^2 + 2\,BM^2$.

**29.** In a right triangle, the altitude from the right angle to the hypotenuse divides the hypotenuse into
segments of lengths $x$ and $y$. Prove that $h^2 = xy$, where $h$ is that altitude.

**30.** Prove that in a triangle with sides $a$, $b$, $c$, if $a^2 + b^2 > c^2$, then the angle opposite
$c$ is acute.

## Challenge

**31.** Let $P$ be a point strictly inside $\triangle ABC$. Prove that $PB + PC < AB + AC$.

**32.** Let $P$ be a point inside $\triangle ABC$ and let $s$ be the semiperimeter. Prove that
$PA + PB + PC > s$.

**33.** Prove that among all triangles with a fixed perimeter, the equilateral triangle has the largest area.
*You may use $\tfrac{x+y+z}{3} \ge \sqrt[3]{xyz}$ for positive $x, y, z$, with equality only when
$x = y = z$.*

**34.** *(Viviani.)* Let $P$ be a point inside an equilateral triangle of side $a$. Prove that the sum of the
distances from $P$ to the three sides equals the altitude of the triangle, and conclude that the sum does not
depend on where $P$ is.

**35.** In $\triangle ABC$, $AB = AC$, and $P$ is any point of the segment $BC$. Prove that
$AB^2 - AP^2 = BP \cdot PC$.

**36.** Prove that a triangle is equilateral if and only if its three medians are equal.
