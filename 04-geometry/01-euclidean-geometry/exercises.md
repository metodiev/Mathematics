# Exercises: Euclidean Geometry

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 40 problems · Beginner → Challenge · Full solutions in [solutions.md](solutions.md)

Write the **reason** for every step, not only the step. A geometry answer with correct numbers and no justification is not a solution. Problems marked *Prove* require a complete argument from the axioms and theorems of the tutorial (cited by number); nothing may be justified by a diagram. Problems 25–40 deliberately test the logical distinctions of the chapter — converse versus theorem, neutral versus Euclidean, axiom versus theorem.

## Beginner (1–14)

**1.** For each angle, name its type (acute, right, obtuse or straight), its complement if one exists, and its supplement: (a) $37°$; (b) $90°$; (c) $124°$; (d) $15.5°$.

**2.** The ray $\overrightarrow{OB}$ is interior to $\angle AOC$, with $m\angle AOB = 42°$ and $m\angle BOC = 31°$. (a) Find $m\angle AOC$. (b) How large is the angle between $\overrightarrow{OB}$ and the bisector of $\angle AOC$?

**3.** $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$ form a linear pair, with $m\angle 1 = (6x-4)°$ and $m\angle 2 = (3x+13)°$. Find $x$ and both measures, and check the answer.

**4.** Two lines meet at a point, and one of the four angles measures $68°$. Find the other three.

**5.** The points $A$, $B$, $C$ are collinear with $B$ between $A$ and $C$, and $D$ is a point not on that line. (a) Name two opposite rays with endpoint $B$. (b) Is the ray $\overrightarrow{BA}$ the same as the ray $\overrightarrow{BC}$? (c) How many distinct lines contain at least two of $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$? (d) Is $\angle ABC$ an angle with an interior in the sense of Definition 5.8?

**6.** Two angles of a triangle measure $48°$ and $67°$. Find the third, and say which theorem you used and whether it needs the parallel postulate.

**7.** In $\triangle ABC$ the exterior angle at $A$ measures $125°$ and one remote interior angle measures $62°$. Find the other remote interior angle and the interior angle at $A$.

**8.** In $\triangle ABC$ we have $AB = AC$ and $m\angle B = 55°$. Find $m\angle C$ and $m\angle A$.

**9.** Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Label the angles $1,2,3,4$ at the upper intersection (upper-left, upper-right, lower-right, lower-left) and $5,6,7,8$ at the lower intersection in the same order. One of the eight angles measures $115°$; suppose it is angle $1$. Find all eight measures, state which angle is alternate interior to angle $3$, and state which is corresponding to angle $2$.

**10.** In each case decide whether the two triangles must be congruent, and if so by which criterion; if not, say why not. (a) $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$, $m\angle B = m\angle E$. (b) $m\angle A = m\angle D$, $m\angle B = m\angle E$, $AB = DE$. (c) $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$, $CA = FD$. (d) $m\angle A = m\angle D$, $m\angle B = m\angle E$, $BC = EF$. (e) $AB = DE$, $BC = EF$, $m\angle A = m\angle D$.

**11.** State the converse of the theorem "vertical angles are equal", and say whether it is true. Justify your answer with a specific configuration.

**12.** Which of Euclid's five postulates is used in each of the following? (a) joining two points by a segment; (b) extending $\overline{AB}$ past $B$; (c) drawing the circle with centre $A$ through $B$; (d) asserting that every right angle has the same measure; (e) concluding that two lines meet on a stated side.

**13.** Two complementary angles are in the ratio $2:3$. Find both.

**14.** One of two supplementary angles is $30°$ more than twice the other. Find both.

## Intermediate (15–24)

**15.** Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, and a pair of same-side interior angles measure $(3x+20)°$ and $(2x+10)°$. Find $x$ and both measures, and check.

**16.** The angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2:3:4$. Find them and classify the triangle.

**17.** In $\triangle ABC$ the exterior angle at $A$ measures $(4x-5)°$, and the two remote interior angles measure $(2x+10)°$ and $(x+20)°$. Find $x$, the exterior angle and all three interior angles.

**18.** *Prove:* if two angles are both supplementary to the same angle, then they are equal. Name every axiom or common notion used.

**19.** *Prove:* the interior angles of a convex quadrilateral sum to $360°$. State clearly where convexity is used.

**20.** *Prove:* if two lines are cut by a transversal so that a pair of same-side interior angles is supplementary, then the two lines are parallel. Is the converse of your statement also true, and does it need the parallel postulate?

**21.** $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$ and $PM \perp \overline{AB}$. *Prove:* $PA = PB$.

**22.** Two lines meet. The angles $\angle 1$ and $\angle 3$ are vertical angles, with $m\angle 1 = (7x-30)°$ and $m\angle 3 = (4x+45)°$. Find $x$ and all four angles.

**23.** Describe the straightedge-and-compass construction of the bisector of an angle, and *prove* that the constructed ray really bisects the angle.

**24.** In $\triangle ABC$, $m\angle A = 50°$. The bisectors of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ meet at $I$. Find $m\angle BIC$, and identify every theorem used.

## Advanced (25–33)

**25.** *Prove* that a triangle cannot have two right angles, in two ways: (a) from the angle-sum theorem; (b) from the exterior angle inequality alone. Which axioms does each argument use?

**26.** *Prove:* if two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then the bisectors of a pair of alternate interior angles are parallel lines.

**27.** *Prove* the perpendicular bisector theorem in both directions: for distinct points $A$, $B$ and a point $P$, $PA = PB$ if and only if $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $\overline{AB}$.

**28.** *Prove:* if two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, then the bisectors of a pair of same-side interior angles are perpendicular. Where in the proof is the parallel postulate used?

**29.** Let $ABCD$ be a convex quadrilateral with $AB \parallel CD$ and $BC \parallel AD$. *Prove* that the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ bisect each other.

**30.** *Prove* (Euclid I.18): if $AC > AB$ in $\triangle ABC$, then $m\angle B > m\angle C$.

**31.** *Prove:* if a convex quadrilateral $ABCD$ has one pair of opposite sides both parallel and equal — say $AB \parallel CD$ and $AB = CD$ — then the other pair of opposite sides is parallel too.

**32.** *Prove* (Euclid I.19, the converse of Exercise 30): if $m\angle B > m\angle C$ in $\triangle ABC$, then $AC > AB$.

**33.** A **rectangle** is a quadrilateral with four right angles. *Prove* that the diagonals of a rectangle are equal.

## Challenge (34–40)

**34.** Given three segments of lengths $a$, $b$, $c$ satisfying the triangle inequalities ($a + b > c$, $b + c > a$, $c + a > b$), describe a straightedge-and-compass construction of a triangle with those side lengths, prove that the construction produces such a triangle, and explain exactly where each inequality is needed.

**35.** *Show that SSA is not a congruence criterion.* Take $\angle A = 30°$, $AB = 10$, $BC = 6$. Explain geometrically why there are two non-congruent triangles with these data, and find both possible values of $AC$. (You may use that in a $30$–$60$–$90$ triangle the side opposite $30°$ is half the hypotenuse, which follows from Euclid I.1 and the angle-sum theorem.)

**36.** *Prove* that the perpendicular bisectors of the three sides of a triangle are concurrent (all pass through one point).

**37.** On a sphere, take the three points $N = (0,0,1)$, $P = (1,0,0)$, $Q = (0,1,0)$ and join them by arcs of great circles. Show that the resulting "triangle" has three right angles, so its angle sum is $270°$, and explain which Euclidean axioms fail on the sphere.

**38.** Euclid's Construction I.1 (equilateral triangle on a given segment) assumes that the two circles meet. Show that this cannot be proved from the incidence, order and segment-measure axioms alone, by exhibiting a model of those axioms in which the two circles have no common point. What kind of additional axiom repairs the gap?

**39.** A student offers this "proof" that alternate interior angles are equal for any two lines cut by a transversal: *"Let $\ell$ and $m$ be cut by $t$ at $E$ and $F$. Draw through $E$ the line $n$ that makes with $t$ an alternate interior angle equal to the angle at $F$; then $n \parallel m$ by the alternate interior criterion. But there is only one line through $E$, namely $\ell$. Hence $\ell = n$, and the alternate interior angles are equal."* Identify the error precisely, and explain which statement in the argument is equivalent to the parallel postulate.

**40.** *Prove* that there is **at most one** point $Q$ on a line $\ell$ such that $PQ \perp \ell$ for a given point $P$ not on $\ell$. (Existence of such a point is a separate question, not asked here.)
