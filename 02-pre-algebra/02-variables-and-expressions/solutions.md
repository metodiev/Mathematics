# Variables and Algebraic Expressions — Solutions

> [Tutorial](README.md) · [Exercises](exercises.md)

Each solution names the law being used. In an exam, the name is not always required, but being able to name the law is the best test of whether you understand why the step is legal.

## Beginner

**1.** "Seven more than $n$" adds $7$ to $n$:
$$n+7.$$
Order does not matter for addition, so $7+n$ is the same expression.

**2.** "The sum of $x$ and $2$" must be formed first, so it is grouped:
$$5(x+2).$$
Without parentheses, $5x+2$ would mean five times $x$, then add $2$ — a different quantity.

**3.** Written as a sum, $7x^2-4x+9 = 7x^2+(-4x)+9$. The terms are
$$7x^2,\quad -4x,\quad 9.$$
The coefficient of $x$ is $-4$ (the sign belongs to the term). The constant term is $9$. The coefficient of $x^2$ is $7$.

**4.** The terms have the same variable part $x$, so the distributive property gives
$$5x+3x=(5+3)x=8x.$$

**5.** Combine the like terms $9y$ and $-4y$; the constant $2$ has no partner:
$$9y-4y+2=(9-4)y+2=5y+2.$$

**6.** Reorder and group like terms (commutative and associative laws):
$$6a+2b-4a+5b=(6a-4a)+(2b+5b)=(6-4)a+(2+5)b=2a+7b.$$
$2a$ and $7b$ are unlike, so the process stops.

**7.** Distribute $3$ over both terms:
$$3(x+5)=3x+3\cdot5=3x+15.$$

**8.** Distribute $-2$; note $(-2)(-3)=+6$:
$$-2(4x-3)=-8x+6.$$

**9.** Substitute:
$$4(3)-7=12-7=5.$$

**10.** Substitute with parentheses, then apply the order of operations:
$$2(-3)^2+(-3)=2\cdot9-3=18-3=15.$$
The square is applied to $-3$, giving $+9$; the trailing $+x$ becomes $-3$.

**11.** Substitute both values:
$$5\bigl(2+(-6)\bigr)=5(-4)=-20.$$

## Intermediate

**12.** Distribute, then combine constants:
$$8+3(x-2)=8+3x-6=3x+2.$$
The constants $8$ and $-6$ are like terms.

**13.** Distribute first, then combine the $x$-terms:
$$4(2x+3)-5x=8x+12-5x=(8-5)x+12=3x+12.$$

**14.** Distribute $-2$ over both terms, then combine constants:
$$7-2(3x-4)=7-6x+8=15-6x.$$
The sign of $+8$ is the step to watch: $-2\cdot(-4)=+8$.

**15.** The minus sign before the parenthesis changes both interior signs:
$$5x+3-(2x-7)=5x+3-2x+7=(5-2)x+(3+7)=3x+10.$$

**16.** Distribute each product:
$$2(3a+4b)=6a+8b,\qquad 3(2a-b)=6a-3b.$$
Then
$$6a+8b+6a-3b=(6+6)a+(8-3)b=12a+5b.$$

**17.** First combine the numerator, then divide:
$$\dfrac{\dfrac32+\dfrac52}{2}=\dfrac{\dfrac82}{2}=\dfrac{4}{2}=2.$$
Equivalently, the average of $\frac32$ and $\frac52$ is $\frac12\left(\frac32+\frac52\right)=2$.

**18.** The cost is the per-notebook charge times the number of notebooks, plus the fixed shipping:
$$C=2.75n+4.$$
At $n=6$:
$$C=2.75(6)+4=16.50+4=20.50,$$
so the total is \$20.50. The coefficient $2.75$ has units of dollars per notebook; the constant $4$ has units of dollars.

**19.** Perimeter is twice the length plus twice the width:
$$P=2(x+5)+2x=2x+10+2x=4x+10.$$
Equivalently $P=2\bigl((x+5)+x\bigr)=2(2x+5)=4x+10$. A check at $x=1$: the rectangle is $6\times1$, perimeter $14$; the formula gives $4+10=14$.

**20.** The greatest common factor of $12$ and $18$ is $6$:
$$12x+18=6(2x)+6(3)=6(2x+3).$$
Check by distributing: $6(2x+3)=12x+18$.

**21.** The greatest common factor of $15$ and $25$ is $5$:
$$15a-25=5(3a)-5(5)=5(3a-5).$$

## Advanced

**22.** Terms can be combined only when their variable parts are identical, because combining uses the distributive property with a common factor:
$$cx+dx=(c+d)x.$$
In $5x+2y$ the variable parts are $x$ and $y$; there is no single variable expression that both terms contain, so no distribution is possible. The proposed answer $7xy$ would require $5x+2y=(5+2)xy$, which is false.

Numerical checks: at $x=y=1$, both $5x+2y=7$ and $7xy=7$, a coincidence of that one point. At $x=2$, $y=1$:
$$5(2)+2(1)=12,\qquad 7(2)(1)=14,$$
so the expressions differ. At $x=1$, $y=2$ they give $9$ and $14$. One counterexample is enough to disprove equivalence.

**23.** Distribute both products, taking care with the second minus sign:
$$3(2x-1)=6x-3,$$
$$-2(3x-4)=-6x+8.$$
Adding,
$$6x-3-6x+8=(6-6)x+(-3+8)=0x+5=5.$$
The $x$-terms cancel because their coefficients sum to $6-6=0$. The expression is therefore *constant*: whatever $x$ is, the value is $5$. This does not mean $x=0$; it means $x$ has no influence on the value.

**24.** Substitute with parentheses:
$$2(-2)^2-3(-2)(3)+(3)^2=2(4)+18+9=8+18+9=35.$$
Each sign deserves a check: $(-2)^2=4$; $-3(-2)(3)=+18$; $3^2=9$.

**25.** Subtract by distributing the minus over the second expression:
$$A-B=(0.10n+25)-(0.08n+40)=0.10n+25-0.08n-40=(0.10-0.08)n+(25-40).$$
Hence
$$A-B=0.02n-15.$$
The coefficient $0.02$ is the difference in per-minute cost (plan $A$ costs two cents more per minute); the constant $-15$ is the difference in fixed fees.

Plan $A$ is cheaper when $A-B<0$, that is
$$0.02n-15<0\iff 0.02n<15\iff n<750.$$
So plan $A$ is cheaper for fewer than $750$ minutes, the plans cost the same at $750$ minutes, and plan $B$ is cheaper above $750$ minutes.

**26.** Treat $(x+3)$ as a single quantity and distribute the second factor over it:
$$(x+3)(x+2)=x(x+2)+3(x+2).$$
Now distribute each part:
$$=x^2+2x+3x+6=x^2+5x+6.$$

The area model: draw a rectangle with height $x+3$ and width $x+2$, and cut it by one vertical and one horizontal line into four rectangles of areas
$$x\cdot x=x^2,\qquad x\cdot2=2x,\qquad 3\cdot x=3x,\qquad 3\cdot2=6.$$
The total area is $x^2+2x+3x+6=x^2+5x+6$. The two $x$-rectangles combine into $5x$ because they are like terms.

**27.** Substitute each value:
$$C=-40:\quad F=\dfrac95(-40)+32=-72+32=-40.$$
$$C=100:\quad F=\dfrac95(100)+32=180+32=212.$$
$$C=37:\quad F=\dfrac95(37)+32=\dfrac{333}{5}+32=66.6+32=98.6.$$
The first result, $-40$, is the temperature at which the two scales agree; the second is the boiling point of water; the third is normal body temperature.

**28.** Take an $a\times(b+c)$ rectangle made of unit squares, where $a,b,c$ are positive whole numbers.

*Count by rows.* There are $a$ rows, and each row contains $b+c$ unit squares, so the total is $a(b+c)$.

*Count by pieces.* Cut the rectangle with a vertical line into an $a\times b$ rectangle and an $a\times c$ rectangle. The first contains $ab$ unit squares and the second $ac$, so the total is $ab+ac$.

Both counts describe the same rectangle, so the totals are equal:
$$a(b+c)=ab+ac.$$
This is a proof for positive whole numbers. The identity is then extended to all integers and rationals by the usual sign and fraction rules, and taken as an axiom (or proved from a construction) for real numbers.

## Challenge

**29.** (a) Distribute:
$$2(x+3)=2x+2\cdot3=2x+6.$$
The two expressions are identical after simplification, so they are equivalent for every $x$.

(b) Evaluate at $x=0$: $2(0+3)=6$, while $2(0)+3=3$. Since $6\neq3$, the expressions are not equivalent. (Any other value, such as $x=1$ giving $8$ versus $5$, also works.)

(c) Suppose $2(x+3)=2x+3$ for some $x$. Expanding the left side gives
$$2x+6=2x+3.$$
Subtract $2x$ from both sides:
$$6=3.$$
This is a false statement about numbers, independent of $x$. The assumption that such an $x$ exists has led to a falsehood, so no such $x$ exists. The equation has no solution; the two expressions differ by the constant $3$.

**30.** The first square uses $4$ sticks. Each additional square shares one side with the previous square and adds $3$ new sticks. So a row of $n$ squares uses
$$4+3(n-1)=4+3n-3=3n+1$$
sticks. The formula also gives $3(1)+1=4$ for $n=1$, as required.

For $301$ sticks:
$$3n+1=301\implies 3n=300\implies n=100.$$
A row of $100$ squares uses $301$ sticks.

For $40$ squares:
$$3(40)+1=121,$$
so $121$ sticks. The check $n=2\Rightarrow7$ matches the direct count $4+3$.

**31.** Let the two consecutive odd integers be
$$2n+1\quad\text{and}\quad 2n+3,$$
where $n$ is an integer. (Any odd integer is $2n+1$; the next odd integer is two more.) Their sum is
$$(2n+1)+(2n+3)=4n+4=4(n+1).$$
Since $n+1$ is an integer, the sum is $4$ times an integer, hence divisible by $4$.

Examples: $3+5=8=4\cdot2$; $7+9=16=4\cdot4$; $11+13=24=4\cdot6$. The same proof can be written with the pair $2n-1$ and $2n+1$, whose sum is $4n$.

**32.** $x^2$ means $x\cdot x$, while $2x$ means $x+x$. These are different operations: at $x=3$, $x^2=9$ but $2x=6$; at $x=-1$, $x^2=1$ but $2x=-2$. Since they disagree at some values, they are not equivalent expressions. (They do agree at $x=2$: $4=4$; and at $x=0$: $0=0$.)

To find all values where they agree, rewrite the equality and bring everything to one side:
$$x^2=2x\iff x^2-2x=0\iff x(x-2)=0.$$
The last step factors out the common factor $x$ using the distributive property. A product of two numbers is zero only if one of the factors is zero, so
$$x=0\quad\text{or}\quad x=2.$$
Both check: $0^2=2(0)$ and $2^2=2(2)$. The expressions are equal at exactly these two values and unequal everywhere else.
