# Conic Sections — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 34 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely and check every numerical answer by substituting it back. Where a
problem asks for a description, state the type, the centre or vertex, the axes, the foci, the
eccentricity and anything else the question names. Do not open [solutions.md](solutions.md) until you
have attempted the problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Write the standard equation of the circle with centre $(3,-2)$ and radius $5$.

**2.** Find the centre and the radius of the circle $x^2+y^2-8x+6y-11=0$.

**3.** For the parabola $y^2 = 16x$, find the vertex, the focus, the directrix and the length of the
latus rectum.

**4.** For the parabola $x^2 = -8y$, find the focus and the directrix.

**5.** Write the equation of the parabola with vertex at the origin and focus $(0,3)$.

**6.** For the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{36}+\dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$, find $a$, $b$, $c$, the foci and the
eccentricity.

**7.** For the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{16} = 1$, find $a$, $b$, $c$, the foci and the
asymptotes.

**8.** Identify the curve $4x^2+4y^2-12x+8y+1 = 0$ and give its centre and radius.

**9.** Identify the curve $y^2-4y-8x+4 = 0$ and give its vertex, focus and directrix.

**10.** An ellipse has foci $(\pm 3,0)$ and vertices $(\pm 5,0)$. Write its equation.

**11.** A hyperbola has vertices $(0,\pm 2)$ and foci $(0,\pm 3)$. Write its equation.

**12.** Find the eccentricity of the ellipse $16x^2+25y^2 = 400$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Identify the curve $9x^2+4y^2+36x-8y+4 = 0$ and give its centre, semi-axes, foci, vertices
and eccentricity.

**14.** Identify the curve $9x^2-16y^2-18x+32y-151 = 0$ and give its centre, vertices, foci,
asymptotes and eccentricity.

**15.** A parabola has focus $(2,3)$ and directrix $y = -1$. Find its vertex, its equation and the
length of its latus rectum.

**16.** An ellipse has foci $(1,2)$ and $(7,2)$ and major axis of length $10$. Find its equation.

**17.** A hyperbola has foci $(0,\pm 5)$ and vertices $(0,\pm 3)$. Find its equation, its asymptotes
and its eccentricity.

**18.** Classify each equation by computing its discriminant, and say what the sign means:
(a) $5x^2+4xy+2y^2-3x+7 = 0$; (b) $4x^2+12xy+9y^2-x = 0$; (c) $x^2-6xy+2y^2+5 = 0$;
(d) $2x^2+2y^2-4x+6y = 0$.

**19.** For the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{49}+\dfrac{y^2}{24} = 1$, find the distance between the foci and
the length of the latus rectum.

**20.** Find the equation of the circle through $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$ and $(0,6)$, and give its centre and
radius.

**21.** The parabola $y = ax^2+bx+c$ passes through $(0,1)$, $(1,0)$ and $(2,3)$. Find $a$, $b$, $c$,
the vertex and the focus, and verify the focus numerically by testing the point $(0,1)$.

**22.** A hyperbola has foci $(\pm 6,0)$ and asymptotes $y = \pm x$. Find its equation and its
eccentricity.

## Advanced

**23.** Let $P = (x,y)$ be a point of the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ with
$c^2 = a^2-b^2$ and foci $F_{\pm} = (\pm c,0)$. Prove that $|PF_{+}| = a-ex$ and $|PF_{-}| = a+ex$,
where $e = c/a$, and deduce that $|PF_+|+|PF_-| = 2a$.

**24.** Let $P = (x,y)$ be a point of the right branch $\left(x \ge a\right)$ of
$\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$, with $c^2 = a^2+b^2$ and foci $F_{\pm} = (\pm c,0)$. Prove
that $|PF_+| = ex-a$ and $|PF_-| = ex+a$, and deduce that $|PF_-|-|PF_+| = 2a$.

**25.** Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} A & B/2\\ B/2 & C\end{pmatrix}$ be the matrix of the quadratic part
of $Ax^2+Bxy+Cy^2+Dx+Ey+F = 0$. Prove that a rotation of the axes replaces $M$ by $R^{\mathsf T}MR$
for some rotation matrix $R$, and deduce that $B^2-4AC$ is unchanged.

**26.** For the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ with $x>a$, let $P = (x,y)$ be on the
upper half of the right branch. Prove that the distance from $P$ to the line $y = \dfrac{b}{a}x$ tends
to $0$ as $x\to\infty$.

**27.** Rotate the axes to remove the $xy$ term from $5x^2+4\sqrt3\,xy+9y^2 = 14$, identify the conic,
and give its semi-axes and eccentricity.

**28.** Prove that the chord of the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ through the focus
$(c,0)$ perpendicular to the major axis has length $\dfrac{2b^2}{a}$, and show that the corresponding
chord of the hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}-\dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ has the same length.

**29.** Starting from the focus–directrix law $|PF| = e\,d(P,\ell)$ with the focus at the origin and
the directrix the line $x = d_0$, derive the polar equation $r = \dfrac{e\,d_0}{1+e\cos\theta}$. Then
show, by converting to Cartesian coordinates, that for $0<e<1$ the curve is an ellipse with
$a = \dfrac{e\,d_0}{1-e^2}$ and $c = ea$.

**30.** Two LORAN stations stand at $A = (-200,0)$ and $B = (200,0)$, distances in kilometres. A
receiver measures that the signal from $A$ arrives $0.0008$ s later than the signal from $B$, and radio
travels at $3\times10^{5}$ km/s. A second measurement shows that the receiver is $100$ km due north of
the midpoint of $AB$. Find the receiver's position, and verify that it satisfies the measured time
difference.

## Challenge

**31.** Find the locus of points $P$ whose distance to the origin is twice their distance to the line
$x = 3$. Identify the conic, write it in standard form, and verify that its eccentricity equals the
ratio $2$ used in the definition.

**32.** Let $E$ be the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ and let $(x_0,y_0)$ be a point of
$E$. Prove that the line $\dfrac{xx_0}{a^2}+\dfrac{yy_0}{b^2} = 1$ meets $E$ in the single point
$(x_0,y_0)$, and explain why this makes it the tangent at that point.

**33.** (a) Prove that all parabolas are similar: for any $p \ne 0$, the curve $y^2 = 4px$ is carried
onto $y^2 = 4x$ by a single scaling. (b) Prove that a conic defined by $|PF| = e\,d(P,\ell)$ with
$e = 1$ is a parabola, by placing $F$ at the origin and $\ell$ at $x = d_0$ and deriving its equation.

**34.** A hyperbolic cooling tower has a vertical cross-section that is a hyperbola. Its waist has
radius $25$ m at a height of $40$ m above the ground, and its radius is $30$ m at a height of $70$ m.
Model the cross-section with the waist at the origin of coordinates, find $b$, and predict the radius
at a height of $100$ m. State one reason the model must break down far above the tower.
