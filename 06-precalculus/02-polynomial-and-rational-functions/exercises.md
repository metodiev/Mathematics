# Polynomial and Rational Functions — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 38 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely. When you divide, write the division identity $P=DQ+R$ and check it; when
you solve an inequality, draw the sign chart and justify every endpoint; when you assert an asymptote,
give the limit that defines it. Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md); do not open that file
until you have attempted the problems.

## Beginner

**1.** For each polynomial, state the degree, the leading coefficient, the constant term and the end
behaviour.

(a) $3x^4-2x+7$  (b) $-x^5+4x^3-x$  (c) $2x^2-8x+1$  (d) $-4x^3+x^2$

**2.** Divide $x^3-2x^2+4x-5$ by $x-1$ using long division, and write the division identity.

**3.** Use synthetic division to divide $x^3-7x+6$ by $x-2$, and factor the polynomial completely.

**4.** Use the remainder theorem to compute $P(3)$ for $P(x)=2x^3-4x^2+x-5$, then confirm the answer
with synthetic division.

**5.** Decide whether $x-2$ is a factor of each polynomial.

(a) $x^3-3x^2+4$  (b) $x^3+2x-8$  (c) $x^4-2x^3+3x^2-8x+4$

**6.** For $P(x)=x^3(x-2)^2(x+1)$: state the degree, the leading coefficient, every zero with its
multiplicity, and whether the graph crosses or touches at each zero.

**7.** Describe the end behaviour and the behaviour at each zero of $f(x)=-2(x-1)(x+3)^2(x-4)$.

**8.** Find the domain of each rational function.

(a) $\dfrac{x+1}{x^2-9}$  (b) $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2+1}$  (c) $\dfrac{1}{x^2-2x}$  (d) $\dfrac{x-3}{x^2+4}$

**9.** Find all vertical asymptotes.

(a) $f(x)=\dfrac{2x+1}{x-3}$  (b) $g(x)=\dfrac{x}{x^2-4}$  (c) $h(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{(x-1)^2}$

**10.** Find the horizontal asymptote, or explain why there is none.

(a) $\dfrac{3x^2-1}{x^2+2}$  (b) $\dfrac{2x+1}{x^3-1}$  (c) $\dfrac{x^3}{x^2+1}$  (d) $\dfrac{5x}{2x-7}$

**11.** Locate the hole in $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2-5x+6}$, give the coordinates of the missing point, and
state the vertical asymptote.

**12.** Evaluate each limit informally, justifying the answer by the behaviour of the leading terms or of
the denominator.

(a) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{4x^2-3}{2x^2+x}$  (b) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to2^+}\frac{1}{x-2}$
(c) $\displaystyle\lim_{x\to1^-}\frac{x}{x-1}$

**13.** Solve $(x+2)(x-1)(x-3)>0$ using a sign chart.

## Intermediate

**14.** Divide $3x^4-2x^2+x-5$ by $x^2-2$ using long division, and verify the division identity by
expansion.

**15.** Divide $2x^3+3x^2-11x-6$ by $x+3$ using synthetic division, and factor the cubic completely.

**16.** Divide $2x^3-3x^2+4x-5$ by $2x-1$, using synthetic division with the monic divisor $x-\tfrac12$
and then rescaling the quotient.

**17.** Find all rational roots of $2x^3+x^2-7x-6$, and factor the polynomial completely.

**18.** Apply Descartes' rule of signs to determine the possible numbers of positive and negative real
roots.

(a) $P(x)=x^5-3x^4+2x^2-x+7$  (b) $Q(x)=x^4+3x^2+2$

**19.** The real polynomial $P$ has $3-2i$ as a root. Write the real quadratic factor that this forces,
and justify why $3+2i$ is a root.

**20.** Construct the monic cubic with real coefficients whose roots are $2$ and $1+i$, and state all
three roots.

**21.** Analyse $f(x)=\dfrac{x-1}{x^2-4}$: domain, intercepts, vertical and horizontal asymptotes, and the
sign of $f$ on each interval.

**22.** Find the slant asymptote of $f(x)=\dfrac{2x^2-3x+1}{x+1}$, and verify the limit definition
$f(x)-(mx+k)\to0$.

**23.** Solve $\dfrac{x-3}{x+1}\le 2$.

**24.** Solve $x^3-4x \le 0$.

**25.** A factory's cost function is $C(x)=0.1x^2+8x+500$ dollars for $x$ items. Find the average cost
$A(x)=C(x)/x$, identify its slant asymptote, and compute $A(100)$.

**26.** A tank contains $100$ litres of brine holding $20$ kg of dissolved salt. Adding $x$ litres of pure
water gives concentration $c(x)=\dfrac{20}{100+x}$ kg/L. State the domain, the horizontal asymptote and
the value $c(100)$; then find the volume of water needed for the concentration to fall to
$0.05$ kg/L.

## Advanced

**27.** Prove the remainder theorem and the factor theorem, starting from the division algorithm.

**28.** Prove: if $P$ has real coefficients and $z$ is a root of $P$, then $\bar z$ is a root of $P$ with
the same multiplicity.

**29.** Prove that every real polynomial of odd degree has at least one real root.

**30.** Prove by induction that a nonzero polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ real roots, counted with
multiplicity.

**31.** Prove the rational root theorem.

**32.** Prove that if $f=P/Q$ with $\deg P<\deg Q$, then $\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}f(x)=0$, and that if the
degrees are equal with leading coefficients $a,b$, the limit is $a/b$.

**33.** Let $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2+3x+2}{x+1}$. Simplify, state the domain of $f$, identify the hole, and
explain why $f$ has no vertical asymptote even though its denominator vanishes.

**34.** Let $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-1}{x^2+1}$. Prove that $-1 \le f(x)<1$ for every real $x$, that $y=1$ is a
horizontal asymptote, and find where the graph attains $-1$ and where it crosses the $x$-axis. Is $0$ in
the range?

## Challenge

**35.** (a) Prove that $x^4+4x^3+6x^2+4x+1=(x+1)^4$. (b) Deduce that $-1$ is a root of multiplicity $4$
of that polynomial, and describe the behaviour of its graph at $x=-1$.

**36.** Let $P$ be a real polynomial of degree $n$ with $n$ real roots counted with multiplicity, say
$r_1,\dots,r_n$. Prove that $P(x)=a_n(x-r_1)\cdots(x-r_n)$.

**37.** Construct a rational function with a hole at $x=2$, exactly one vertical asymptote $x=-1$, the
horizontal asymptote $y=3$, and no $x$-intercepts. Prove that your function has all four properties.

**38.** (a) Prove that a rational function cannot have both a horizontal asymptote $y=L$ and a slant
asymptote $y=mx+k$ with $m \ne 0$. (b) Prove that a slant asymptote, when it exists, is unique.
