# Functions and Graphs — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 38 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely. For a domain question, name every restriction and intersect them; for a
range question, either solve $y=f(x)$ for $x$ or justify the answer from a known family. Proofs must
cite the definitions they use. Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md); do not open that file
until you have attempted the problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Decide whether each relation is a function. For those that are, give the domain and the range;
for those that are not, say which input is repeated.

(a) $\{(1,2),\,(2,3),\,(3,4),\,(1,5)\}$

(b) $\{(1,2),\,(2,2),\,(3,5)\}$

(c) the table

| $x$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | $4$ | $0$ | $1$ | $4$ |

(d) the equation $y^2=x$, with $x$ as input.

**2.** For $f(x)=2x^2-3x+1$, compute $f(-2)$, $f(0)$, $f(3)$ and $f(a+1)$, simplifying the last answer.

**3.** Find the domain of each function.

(a) $f(x)=\sqrt{4-3x}$  (b) $g(x)=\dfrac{1}{x^2-x-6}$  (c) $h(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{\sqrt{x-5}}$
(d) $k(x)=\sqrt{x^2-9}$

**4.** Find the range of each function.

(a) $f(x)=x^2-8x+11$  (b) $g(x)=3-\sqrt{x+1}$  (c) $h(x)=\dfrac{2}{x-1}$

**5.** Classify each function as even, odd, or neither.

(a) $f(x)=x^4-2x^2$  (b) $g(x)=x^5-x$  (c) $h(x)=x^2+x+1$  (d) $k(x)=\dfrac{x}{x^2+4}$

**6.** For $f(x)=x^2+6x+2$, complete the square, state the vertex, the range, and the intervals on which
$f$ is increasing and decreasing.

**7.** For $f(x)=x^2-3x$, compute the average rate of change on $[1,4]$ and on $[2,2+h]$. Simplify the
second answer and state what it tends to as $h \to 0$.

**8.** Describe the transformations that take the graph of the stated base function to each graph.

(a) $y=(x-2)^2+5$ from $y=x^2$  (b) $y=-\sqrt{x+1}$ from $y=\sqrt{x}$
(c) $y=2\lvert x\rvert-3$ from $y=\lvert x\rvert$  (d) $y=\dfrac{1}{x-3}$ from $y=\dfrac1x$

**9.** For $y=\sqrt{x+2}-3$, state the domain and range, find both intercepts, and describe the graph as
a transformation of $y=\sqrt{x}$.

**10.** Let $f(x)=x^2+1$ and $g(x)=2x-3$. Find $(f\circ g)(x)$, $(g\circ f)(x)$, $(f\circ g)(1)$ and
$(g\circ f)(1)$.

**11.** Write each function as a composition $f\circ g$ of two simpler functions.

(a) $h(x)=(3x-5)^4$  (b) $h(x)=\sqrt{x^2+1}$  (c) $h(x)=\dfrac{1}{(x+2)^3}$

**12.** Find the inverse of $f(x)=5x-2$, and verify both inverse identities by direct computation.

**13.** The table defines a function $g$.

| $x$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $g(x)$ | $3$ | $5$ | $5$ | $7$ |

Explain why $g$ has no inverse, and state which injectivity condition fails.

## Intermediate

**14.** Let $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ and $g(x)=\dfrac{1}{x-4}$. Find $(f\circ g)(x)$ and $(g\circ f)(x)$, with the
domain of each.

**15.** For $f(x)=\dfrac{4x+1}{x-2}$: find $f^{-1}$, state the domain and range of both $f$ and $f^{-1}$,
and identify the vertical and horizontal asymptote of each graph.

**16.** Let $f(x)=x^2-4x+3$ with domain $[2,\infty)$. Show that $f$ is injective there, find its range,
and find $f^{-1}$.

**17.** Show that $f(x)=\dfrac{x^3}{x^2-4}$ is an odd function, stating its domain.

**18.** For $f(x)=\dfrac1x$, compute $\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$ and simplify it to a single fraction.

**19.** Prove that $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{x+1}$ is strictly increasing on $(-1,\infty)$, and give a pair
$x_1<x_2$ in $\mathbb{R}\setminus\{-1\}$ with $f(x_1)>f(x_2)$, showing that $f$ is not increasing on its
whole domain.

**20.** For $y=-3\lvert 2x+8\rvert+1$: identify the parameters $a,b,h,k$, give the image of the vertex
under the mapping rule, and find the $y$-intercept, the $x$-intercepts and the range.

**21.** A ball thrown upward has height $h(t)=-5t^2+20t$ metres after $t$ seconds. Compute the average
velocity on $[0,2]$, on $[1,3]$ and on $[2,4]$, and interpret the sign of each answer.

**22.** Let $f(x)=\sqrt{x+3}$ and $g(x)=\sqrt{2-x}$. Find $(f\circ g)(x)$ and its domain.

**23.** A function is defined piecewise by
$$f(x)=\begin{cases} x+2, & x<0,\\ x^2, & 0 \le x \le 2,\\ 2x-1, & x>2.\end{cases}$$
Compute $f(-3)$, $f(0)$, $f(1)$, $f(2)$, $f(3)$, and determine the domain and range.

**24.** Two data sets are given.

| $x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $P(x)$ | $6$ | $11$ | $16$ | $21$ |
| $Q(x)$ | $6$ | $18$ | $54$ | $162$ |

Identify the family of each, give its formula, and predict both values at $x=5$.

**25.** Show that $f(x)=x^3+1$ and $g(x)=\sqrt[3]{x-1}$ are inverse functions by computing both
compositions. State the domain and range of each.

**26.** Find the inverse of $f(x)=\dfrac{x-3}{x+2}$, together with its domain and range, and verify that
$f\bigl(f^{-1}(0)\bigr)=0$.

## Advanced

**27.** Let $g:A\to B$ and $f:B\to C$ be injective. Prove from the definition that $f\circ g$ is
injective.

**28.** (a) Prove that if $g:B\to A$ satisfies $g(f(a))=a$ for every $a \in A$, then $f:A\to B$ is
injective. (b) Prove that if $f$ has an inverse, then $f$ is bijective.

**29.** Prove that the inverse of a bijection is unique: if $g$ and $h$ are both inverses of
$f:A\to B$, then $g=h$.

**30.** Let $g:A\to B$ and $f:B\to C$ be bijections. Prove that $(f\circ g)^{-1}=g^{-1}\circ f^{-1}$ by
verifying the two inverse identities.

**31.** (a) Prove that if $f\circ g$ is injective, then $g$ is injective. (b) Give an example of
functions $f$ and $g$, with $f$ not injective, such that $f\circ g$ is injective. Explain why your
example does not contradict (a).

**32.** (a) Prove that a strictly monotone function on an interval is injective. (b) Give an example of an
injective function that is not monotone on its domain, and explain where the interval hypothesis of (a)
fails.

**33.** For $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ with $a \ne 0$, prove that
$$\frac{f(u+h)-f(u)}{h}=2au+ah+b \quad (h \ne 0),$$
and deduce the instantaneous rate of change at $x=u$ as $h \to 0$.

**34.** A square of side $x$ is cut from each corner of a $12\ \text{cm}\times12\ \text{cm}$ sheet and the
flaps are folded into an open box, so $V(x)=x(12-2x)^2$ with $0<x<6$. Show that
$$128-V(x)=-4(x-2)^2(x-8),$$
and deduce that the maximum volume is $128\ \text{cm}^3$, attained at $x=2$.

## Challenge

**35.** (a) Show that $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{1-x}$ is a bijection from $(0,1)$ onto $(0,\infty)$ by finding an
explicit inverse and checking both identities. (b) Explain why the same formula is not a function from
$\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$.

**36.** (a) Prove that every function defined on a domain symmetric about $0$ can be written uniquely as
the sum of an even function and an odd function, and give the two parts explicitly. (b) Apply the
decomposition to $f(x)=2^x$.

**37.** Let $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ be a bijection that is strictly increasing and odd. Prove that
$f^{-1}$ is odd.

**38.** (a) Prove that a function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is even if and only if its graph is
symmetric about the $y$-axis. (b) Prove that a function cannot be both even and odd unless it is the
zero function. (c) Prove that no even function $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is injective.
