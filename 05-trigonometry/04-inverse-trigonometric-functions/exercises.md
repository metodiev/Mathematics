# Inverse Trigonometric Functions — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 36 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Give exact values in radians unless a degree sign is written. When a problem asks for a
simplification, state the domain of the simplified expression. When a problem asks for a proof, state
the hypotheses and justify every step. Decimal approximations are checks, never substitutes for exact
answers.

Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md). Do not open that file until you have attempted the
problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Evaluate $\arcsin\dfrac12$.

**2.** Evaluate $\arccos\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\right)$.

**3.** Evaluate $\arctan(-1)$.

**4.** Evaluate $\arcsin\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)$.

**5.** Evaluate $\arccos0$.

**6.** Evaluate $\arctan\sqrt3$.

**7.** State the domain and the range of $\arccos$, and describe the shape of its graph.

**8.** Evaluate $\sin\left(\arccos\dfrac35\right)$.

**9.** Evaluate $\cos\left(\arcsin\left(-\dfrac{5}{13}\right)\right)$.

**10.** Evaluate $\tan\left(\arccos\dfrac12\right)$.

**11.** Evaluate $\arccos\left(\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$.

**12.** Evaluate $\arcsin\left(\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{6}\right)$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Evaluate $\arcsin\left(\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\right)$.

**14.** Evaluate $\arctan\left(\tan\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\right)$.

**15.** Evaluate $\arccos\left(\cos\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{5}\right)\right)$.

**16.** Evaluate $\arcsin\left(\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\right)$.

**17.** Prove that $\arcsin x+\arccos x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for every $x\in[-1,1]$.

**18.** Prove that $\arctan x+\arctan\dfrac1x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x>0$, and that the sum is
$-\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ for $x<0$.

**19.** Simplify $\sin(2\arcsin x)$, stating the domain.

**20.** Simplify $\cos(2\arctan x)$, stating the domain.

**21.** Show that $\arctan\dfrac12+\arctan\dfrac13=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

**22.** Show that $\arctan2+\arctan3=\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.

**23.** Simplify $\tan(\arccos x)$, stating the domain.

**24.** Evaluate $\cos\left(\arctan\dfrac34+\arctan\dfrac{5}{12}\right)$ exactly.

## Advanced

**25.** Solve $\arcsin x=\arccos x$ for $x\in[-1,1]$.

**26.** Solve $\arctan(2x)+\arctan(3x)=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ for real $x$, and explain why any candidate that
fails the hypotheses of the arctangent addition formula must be rejected.

**27.** Prove that for every $x>-1$,
$$\arctan x+\arctan\frac{1-x}{1+x}=\frac{\pi}{4}.$$

**28.** Prove that $\arccos(-x)=\pi-\arccos x$ for $x\in[-1,1]$ and that
$\arctan(-x)=-\arctan x$ for $x\in\mathbb{R}$.

**29.** Differentiate (a) $f(x)=\arcsin(2x)$ and (b) $g(x)=\arctan(x^2)$, stating the domains on which
each formula is valid.

**30.** Evaluate (a) $\displaystyle\int_0^1\frac{dx}{1+x^2}$ and
(b) $\displaystyle\int_0^{1/2}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}$.

**31.** Write $3\sin\theta+4\cos\theta$ in the form $R\sin(\theta+\varphi)$ with $R>0$ and
$-\pi<\varphi\le\pi$, and state the maximum value.

**32.** A right triangle has legs $7$ and $24$. Express its two acute angles using inverse
trigonometric functions, evaluate them in degrees to two decimal places, and verify that they sum to
$90^\circ$.

## Challenge

**33.** Prove that
$$\arctan\frac12+\arctan\frac15+\arctan\frac18=\frac{\pi}{4}.$$

**34.** Prove Machin's formula
$$4\arctan\frac15-\arctan\frac{1}{239}=\frac{\pi}{4}.$$
You may use the double-angle formula for tangent, $\tan2\alpha=\dfrac{2\tan\alpha}{1-\tan^2\alpha}$,
and the arctangent addition formula of the tutorial.

**35.** Prove that for $\lvert x\rvert<1$,
$$\arcsin x=\arctan\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}.$$

**36.** Prove that for every $x\in[-1,1]$,
$$\arccos x=2\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{1-x}{2}}.$$
