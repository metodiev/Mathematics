# The Unit Circle and Radian Measure — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · [Solutions](solutions.md)

Work without a calculator unless the problem asks for a decimal answer. Angle conversions and exact values should be done by hand until they are automatic. Problems 33–36 are proofs, derivations or open-ended modelling tasks; a complete answer includes the reasoning.

## Beginner

**1.** Convert $60^\circ$, $135^\circ$, $210^\circ$ and $330^\circ$ to radians, exactly.

**2.** Convert $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, $\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$ to degrees.

**3.** Find the arc length cut off on a circle of radius $4$ by a central angle of $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ radians.

**4.** Find the area of a sector of radius $5$ with central angle $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$ radians.

**5.** Give the exact values of $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}$, $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4}$, $\tan\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\cos\pi$ and $\tan\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.

**6.** State the quadrant containing the terminal side of each angle: $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$, $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

**7.** State the sign (positive or negative) of $\sin 200^\circ$, $\cos 100^\circ$, $\tan 250^\circ$ and $\cos 340^\circ$.

**8.** Find the reference angle of $150^\circ$, $240^\circ$, $315^\circ$ and $120^\circ$.

**9.** Evaluate exactly $\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$, $\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ and $\tan\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

**10.** Evaluate exactly $\sin\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$, $\cos\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ and $\tan\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.

**11.** Evaluate exactly $\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$, $\cos\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$ and $\tan\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$.

**12.** State the amplitude and the period of $y = 4\sin\theta$ and of $y = -2\cos\theta + 3$.

**13.** Convert $1$ radian to degrees (two decimal places) and $1^\circ$ to radians (exact and as a decimal to four significant figures).

**14.** A wheel of radius $0.35\ \mathrm{m}$ turns through an angle of $5$ radians as it rolls without slipping. How far does it travel?

## Intermediate

**15.** A sector has radius $9\ \mathrm{cm}$ and area $27\ \mathrm{cm}^2$. Find its central angle in radians and its arc length.

**16.** A sector has arc length $12\ \mathrm{cm}$ and radius $8\ \mathrm{cm}$. Find the central angle in radians and in degrees, and the area of the sector.

**17.** Evaluate exactly $\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3} + \cos\dfrac{5\pi}{6} + \tan\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$.

**18.** For $\theta = \dfrac{5\pi}{4}$, find the reference angle and the exact values of $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.

**19.** Solve $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$ for $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$.

**20.** Solve $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ for $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$.

**21.** Solve $\tan\theta = -1$ for $\theta \in [0, 2\pi)$.

**22.** For $y = 2\sin(3\theta)$, state the amplitude and the period, give the zeros in $[0, 2\pi)$, and find the first positive $\theta$ at which the maximum occurs.

**23.** For $y = -3\cos\left(\dfrac{\theta}{2}\right) + 2$, state the amplitude, the period, the midline and the range, and find where the maximum and minimum occur.

**24.** Write a sinusoid with amplitude $5$, period $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and midline $y = -1$ whose maximum occurs at $\theta = 0$.

**25.** A Ferris wheel has radius $12\ \mathrm{m}$ and its centre is $13\ \mathrm{m}$ above the ground. It turns once every $40\ \mathrm{s}$, and a seat starts at the lowest point. Write the height $h(t)$ of the seat after $t$ seconds, and evaluate it at $t = 10$, $20$ and $30$ seconds.

## Advanced

**26.** Prove that for $0 < \theta < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$,
$$\sin\theta < \theta < \tan\theta,$$
using areas on the unit circle, and deduce that $\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\theta} \to 1$ as $\theta \to 0$.

**27.** Using reflections of the unit circle, prove that $\sin(\pi - \theta) = \sin\theta$ and $\cos(\pi - \theta) = -\cos\theta$ for every $\theta$. State clearly which reflection is used and what it does to the coordinates.

**28.** (a) Prove that $\tan(\theta + \pi) = \tan\theta$ wherever both sides are defined. (b) Show that if $\tan(\theta + p) = \tan\theta$ for all $\theta$ where both are defined, then $p$ is an integer multiple of $\pi$; conclude that the least positive period of $\tan$ is $\pi$.

**29.** The average monthly temperature in a city is modelled by
$$T(t) = 15 - 10\cos\left(\frac{\pi(t-1)}{6}\right),$$
where $t$ is the month number, $t = 1$ for January. Find the maximum and minimum temperatures, the period, and the months during which $T(t) \ge 20\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$.

**30.** The depth of water in a harbour is modelled by
$$D(t) = 4.2 + 2\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right),$$
where $t$ is in hours after midnight and $D$ in metres. (a) Find all $t \in [0, 12)$ at which $D(t) = 3\ \mathrm{m}$, to two decimal places. (b) Find the total length of time in a $12$-hour cycle during which $D(t) \ge 5.5\ \mathrm{m}$.

**31.** Prove that a chord subtending a central angle $\theta$ in a circle of radius $r$ has length $2r\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}$, and use the result to find the chord for $r = 10$ and $\theta = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

**32.** Use the small-angle approximation to estimate $\sin 3^\circ$, compare with the true value, compute the relative error, and compare that error with the prediction $\theta^2/6$.

## Challenge

**33.** Prove that the least positive period of $\sin$ is $2\pi$: that is, show that if $\sin(\theta + p) = \sin\theta$ for every real $\theta$, then $p$ is an integer multiple of $2\pi$. Then use the symmetry of the circle to explain why $\cos\theta = \sin\left(\theta + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ for every $\theta$.

**34.** A goat is tethered to one corner of a square shed $10\ \mathrm{m}$ by $10\ \mathrm{m}$, by a rope $25\ \mathrm{m}$ long. The shed's walls block the rope, which wraps around the corners. Find the total area the goat can graze, exactly and to one decimal place. Draw a plan of the reachable region and justify each sector you count.

**35.** A circular segment is the region between a chord and the arc it cuts off. Prove that for a circle of radius $r$ and central angle $\theta$ (with $0 < \theta < \pi$) the segment has area
$$\tfrac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta),$$
and evaluate it for $r = 10\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

**36.** A harbour's tide is recorded as high water $8.0\ \mathrm{m}$ at 04:00 and low water $2.0\ \mathrm{m}$ at 10:00, repeating with period $12$ hours.
(a) Fit a sinusoidal model $D(t) = D + A\cos(B(t - t_0))$ to these data, explaining how each parameter is obtained from the recorded values.
(b) Use the model to predict the depth at $t = 0$, $t = 7$ and $t = 13$ hours.
(c) Find the times of day at which the depth is at least $6.0\ \mathrm{m}$, and the total duration of that window in each cycle.
(d) Discuss two ways in which the model is an idealisation, and state what the actual period of the dominant tidal component is.
