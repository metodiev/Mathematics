# Trigonometric Functions — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · [Solutions](solutions.md)

Work without a calculator unless the problem explicitly asks for a decimal answer. Problems that require a calculator say so; everything else has an exact answer. Problems 33–36 are proofs, derivations or open-ended modelling tasks — a complete answer includes the reasoning, not only a final number.

## Beginner

**1.** A right triangle has legs of length $6$ and $8$. Find the hypotenuse, then write $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$ for the acute angle $\theta$ opposite the side of length $6$.

**2.** In a right triangle the side opposite the acute angle $\theta$ has length $9$ and the hypotenuse has length $41$. Find the third side and then $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.

**3.** Compute exactly: $\sin 30^\circ + \cos 60^\circ$.

**4.** Compute exactly: $\tan 45^\circ + \sin 30^\circ$.

**5.** Compute exactly: $\sin 60^\circ \cdot \cos 30^\circ$.

**6.** Compute exactly: $\sin 45^\circ \cdot \cos 45^\circ + \sin^2 30^\circ$.

**7.** Compute exactly: $\dfrac{\tan 60^\circ}{\tan 30^\circ}$.

**8.** A $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle has hypotenuse $14$. Find the lengths of both legs.

**9.** An isosceles right triangle has hypotenuse $8$. Find the length of each leg.

**10.** In a right triangle, $\sin\theta = \dfrac{5}{13}$ with $\theta$ acute. Find $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.

**11.** Give the exact values of $\sec 45^\circ$, $\csc 30^\circ$ and $\cot 60^\circ$.

**12.** A road rises $4\ \mathrm{m}$ over a horizontal run of $100\ \mathrm{m}$. Find $\tan\theta$ for the angle of the road to the horizontal, and then $\theta$ in degrees to two decimal places.

**13.** A guy wire $6\ \mathrm{m}$ long runs from the top of a vertical pole to a point on the ground, making an angle of $55^\circ$ with the ground. How high on the pole is the wire attached, and how far is the anchor point from the foot of the pole? Give both answers to two decimal places.

**14.** A tree casts a shadow $18\ \mathrm{m}$ long when the sun's elevation is $50^\circ$. Find the height of the tree to two decimal places.

## Intermediate

**15.** In a right triangle, $\cos\theta = \dfrac{15}{17}$ with $\theta$ acute. Find $\sin\theta$, $\tan\theta$, $\csc\theta$ and $\cot\theta$ exactly.

**16.** Simplify the expression $\tan\theta \cdot \cos\theta$ for an acute angle $\theta$, justifying each step.

**17.** In a right triangle, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{7}{24}$ with $\theta$ acute. Find $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and $\sec\theta$ exactly.

**18.** From a point on level ground $60\ \mathrm{m}$ from the foot of a vertical cliff, the angle of elevation of the top of the cliff is $32^\circ$. Find the height of the cliff to two decimal places.

**19.** A kite string $45\ \mathrm{m}$ long makes an angle of $38^\circ$ with the horizontal. Find the height of the kite above the ground and its horizontal distance from the person flying it, both to two decimal places (the person's hand is at ground level).

**20.** A ship sails $20\ \mathrm{km}$ on a bearing of $040^\circ$. How far north and how far east of its starting point is it? Give exact expressions and decimal values to two places.

**21.** From a point $A$ on level ground the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is $45^\circ$. From a point $B$, $20\ \mathrm{m}$ farther from the tower along the same straight line, the angle of elevation is $30^\circ$. Find the height of the tower exactly, and approximately to two decimal places.

**22.** Using a right triangle, prove that $\sin(90^\circ - \theta) = \cos\theta$ for an acute angle $\theta$. Hence express $\sin 25^\circ$ as a cosine.

**23.** A wheelchair ramp must rise $0.60\ \mathrm{m}$ with a slope of $1{:}12$ (one unit of rise per twelve units of horizontal run). Find the horizontal run, the length of the ramp along its slope, and the angle it makes with the horizontal in degrees to two decimal places.

**24.** A force of $300\ \mathrm{N}$ acts at $35^\circ$ above the horizontal. Find its horizontal and vertical components to two decimal places, and check that their squares add to $300^2$ up to rounding.

**25.** An aircraft flies $150\ \mathrm{km}$ on a bearing of $030^\circ$, then $100\ \mathrm{km}$ on a bearing of $120^\circ$. Find the total displacement north and the total displacement east, exactly and to two decimal places.

## Advanced

**26.** Prove that $1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$ for an acute angle $\theta$ in two different ways: (a) algebraically from $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$; (b) directly from the side lengths of the right triangle.

**27.** Prove that for every acute angle $\theta$, $\sin\theta + \cos\theta > 1$. Give two proofs, one using the triangle inequality and one using algebra, and explain why equality can never occur.

**28.** A vertical flagpole stands on the flat roof of a building $20\ \mathrm{m}$ tall. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of the pole is $60^\circ$ and the angle of elevation of the top of the building is $45^\circ$. Find the height of the pole exactly and approximately.

**29.** Two observers stand on a straight horizontal road, $100\ \mathrm{m}$ apart, with a tower between them and in the same vertical plane. From one observer the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is $30^\circ$; from the other it is $45^\circ$. Find the height of the tower exactly and approximately.

**30.** Prove that for an acute angle $\theta$,
$$\tan\theta + \cot\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta\cos\theta},$$
and deduce that $\tan\theta + \cot\theta \ge 2$, stating where equality occurs.

**31.** Prove that for an acute angle $\theta$,
$$\frac{1 + \sin\theta}{\cos\theta} = \frac{\cos\theta}{1 - \sin\theta},$$
and evaluate both sides at $\theta = 60^\circ$ to check the identity.

**32.** An observer whose eyes are $1.8\ \mathrm{m}$ above level ground stands $30\ \mathrm{m}$ from a vertical wall. The angle of elevation of the bottom of a window is $25^\circ$ and the angle of elevation of the top of the same window is $40^\circ$. Find the height of the window to two decimal places. Explain why the observer's eye height does not enter the calculation.

## Challenge

**33.** Prove that if $0^\circ < \alpha < \beta < 90^\circ$ then $\sin\alpha < \sin\beta$. Then explain why this strict monotonicity is exactly what is needed for $\arcsin$ to be a well-defined function on $(0,1)$.

**34.** Derive the exact value $\tan 15^\circ = 2 - \sqrt{3}$ by pure geometry, starting from a $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle with hypotenuse $2$ and extending one leg. Then find $\sin 15^\circ$ and $\cos 15^\circ$ in exact form.

**35.** Let $\theta$ be acute and suppose $\tan\theta = \dfrac{p}{q}$ with $p, q$ positive integers. Prove that
$$\sin\theta = \frac{p}{\sqrt{p^2+q^2}}, \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{q}{\sqrt{p^2+q^2}}.$$
Deduce that $\sin^2\theta$ and $\cos^2\theta$ are always rational, and that $\sin\theta$ is rational exactly when $\sqrt{p^2+q^2}$ is an integer — that is, exactly when $p, q$ and the hypotenuse form a Pythagorean triple. Verify the formula on the $20$–$21$–$29$ right triangle.

**36.** You must find the height of a tower on the far bank of a river, without crossing the river.
(a) Describe a measurement procedure that uses only a tape measure and an instrument for measuring angles of elevation, and derive a formula for the height.
(b) Apply it: at point $A$ the angle of elevation is $35^\circ$; at point $B$, $40\ \mathrm{m}$ farther from the tower along the same straight line, the angle is $22^\circ$. Compute the height to two decimal places.
(c) Identify the two largest sources of error in the method and say how each affects the answer.
