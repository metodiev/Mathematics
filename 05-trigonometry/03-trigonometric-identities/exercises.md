# Trigonometric Identities — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 38 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Work each problem completely. When a problem asks you to simplify, state the values of the variable
for which the simplified expression is defined. When a problem asks you to prove an identity, start
from one side and transform it into the other, state the domain, and never assume what is to be
proved. Give exact values wherever they exist, and a decimal approximation to three or four decimal
places as a check.

Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md). Do not open that file until you have attempted the
problems.

## Beginner

**1.** Given $\sin\theta=\dfrac{7}{25}$ and $\theta$ acute, find $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$.

**2.** Given $\cos\theta=-\dfrac{8}{17}$ and $\theta$ in the third quadrant, find $\sin\theta$ and
$\tan\theta$.

**3.** Simplify $\sec\theta\cos\theta$, stating the values for which the expression is defined.

**4.** Simplify $\dfrac{1-\sin^2\theta}{\sin^2\theta}$.

**5.** Simplify $\dfrac{\tan\theta}{\sec\theta}$.

**6.** Simplify $\sin\theta\cot\theta$.

**7.** Given $\csc\theta=\dfrac53$ and $\theta$ acute, find $\cot\theta$.

**8.** Find the exact value of $\sin15^\circ$.

**9.** Find the exact value of $\cos75^\circ$.

**10.** Find the exact value of $\tan105^\circ$.

**11.** Given $\sin\alpha=\dfrac{5}{13}$ and $\cos\beta=\dfrac45$, with $\alpha$ and $\beta$ both
acute, find $\sin(\alpha+\beta)$.

**12.** Given $\cos\theta=\dfrac35$ and $\theta$ acute, find $\sin2\theta$ and $\cos2\theta$.

## Intermediate

**13.** Prove that $(\sin x+\cos x)^2=1+2\sin x\cos x$ for all real $x$.

**14.** Prove that $(1-\cos^2x)(1+\cot^2x)=1$ for all $x$ at which both sides are defined.

**15.** Prove that $\tan x+\cot x=\sec x\csc x$ for all $x$ at which both sides are defined.

**16.** Prove that $(\sec x-\tan x)(\sec x+\tan x)=1$ for all $x$ at which both sides are defined.

**17.** Simplify $\dfrac{\sin^3x+\sin x\cos^2x}{\cos x}$.

**18.** Write $\sin4x\cos2x$ as a sum of two sines.

**19.** Write $\cos5\theta\cos3\theta$ as a sum of two cosines.

**20.** Express $\sin7x+\sin3x$ as a product.

**21.** Express $\cos4x-\cos2x$ as a product.

**22.** Find the exact value of $\cos22.5^\circ$.

**23.** Given $\tan\theta=\dfrac34$ and $\theta$ in the third quadrant, find $\sin2\theta$.

**24.** Given $\sin\theta=\dfrac{8}{17}$ and $\theta$ in the second quadrant, find $\tan\dfrac{\theta}{2}$.

## Advanced

**25.** Prove that $\dfrac{1+\cos2x}{\sin2x}=\cot x$ for all $x$ at which both sides are defined.

**26.** Prove that $\sin3x=3\sin x-4\sin^3x$ for all real $x$.

**27.** Prove that $\tan\left(x+\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)=\dfrac{1+\tan x}{1-\tan x}$ for all $x$ at which
both sides are defined.

**28.** Prove that $\dfrac{1-\cos2x}{1+\cos2x}=\tan^2x$ for all $x$ at which both sides are defined.

**29.** Prove that $\cos^4x-\sin^4x=\cos2x$ for all real $x$.

**30.** Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be the angles of a triangle. Prove that
$$\cos^2A+\cos^2B+\cos^2C+2\cos A\cos B\cos C=1.$$

**31.** Let $A$, $B$, $C$ be the angles of a triangle, none of them a right angle. Prove that
$$\tan A+\tan B+\tan C=\tan A\tan B\tan C.$$

**32.** Suppose that $A+B=\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and that $\tan A$ and $\tan B$ are both defined. Prove that
$$(1+\tan A)(1+\tan B)=2.$$

**33.** Prove that
$$\sin x\,\sin(60^\circ-x)\,\sin(60^\circ+x)=\tfrac14\sin3x$$
for all real $x$.

## Challenge

**34.** Prove that
$$\frac{1+\sin x}{1-\sin x}=(\sec x+\tan x)^2$$
for all $x$ at which both sides are defined. State the domain.

**35.** Suppose that $\sin x+\cos x=k$.

(a) Express $\sin^3x+\cos^3x$ in terms of $k$.

(b) Determine the set of values that $k$ can take as $x$ ranges over the real numbers, and explain
why the expression in part (a) is automatically real for those values.

**36.** Prove that $\cos36^\circ\cos72^\circ=\dfrac14$ without using a calculator.

**37.** Prove that
$$\tan\theta\,\tan(60^\circ-\theta)\,\tan(60^\circ+\theta)=\tan3\theta$$
for all $\theta$ at which both sides are defined.

**38.** Prove that
$$\cos20^\circ\cos40^\circ\cos80^\circ=\frac18.$$
