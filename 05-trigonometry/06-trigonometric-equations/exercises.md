# Trigonometric Equations — Exercises

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 38 problems · Levels: Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced, Challenge

Unless a restricted interval is stated, give the **general solution** with an integer parameter
$n\in\mathbb{Z}$ and list every family. When an interval is given, give the complete solution set in
that interval and state how many solutions it contains. Exact values in radians are expected; decimal
approximations, where requested, are checks.

Full solutions are in [solutions.md](solutions.md). Do not open that file until you have attempted the
problems.

## Challenge

**33.** Solve $\sin3\theta=\sin5\theta$.

**34.** Solve $2\sin^2\theta+3\sin\theta\cos\theta+\cos^2\theta=0$, giving exact general solutions.
Explain why dividing by $\cos^2\theta$ is legitimate.

**35.** Solve $\cos\theta+\cos2\theta+\cos3\theta=0$.

**36.** The depth of water at a harbour is modelled by
$$d(t)=5+3\sin\frac{\pi t}{6}\ \text{metres},$$
where $t$ is the time in hours after midnight.

(a) Find the maximum and minimum depths and all times in the first $24$ hours at which they occur.

(b) Find all times in the first $24$ hours at which the depth is $6.5$ metres.

**37.** Over a day, the temperature is modelled by a sinusoid with minimum $8^\circ$C at 04:00 and
maximum $20^\circ$C at 16:00.

(a) Find the amplitude, mean level, period and angular frequency.

(b) Write the model in the form $T(t)=D+A\cos(B(t-C))$, where $t$ is the hour after midnight.

(c) Solve $T(t)=17$ for $t\in[0,24)$ and interpret the answers.

(d) State the times of maximum and minimum temperature according to the model.

**38.** Each of the following proposed solutions contains an error. Identify the error, explain the
principle it violates, and give the correct general solution.

(a) To solve $\sin\theta\cos\theta=\sin\theta$, a student divides both sides by $\sin\theta$ and
concludes $\cos\theta=1$, so $\theta=2n\pi$.

(b) To solve $\tan\theta=\sqrt3$ on $[0,2\pi)$, a student writes $\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and stops.

(c) To solve $\sin2\theta=\dfrac12$, a student writes $2\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{6}+2n\pi$, hence
$\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{12}+n\pi$, and stops.
