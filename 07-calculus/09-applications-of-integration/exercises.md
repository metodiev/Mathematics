# Applications of Integration — Exercises

> [Applications of Integration](README.md) · 36 problems · Beginner → Challenge · Solutions: [solutions.md](solutions.md)

Work through the problems in order. Each cites the section of the tutorial it draws on. The set is
designed so that every formula of §§7–8 is used at least twice, and so that the **set-up** — the variable,
the element, and the limits — is exercised as much as the integration. Write the set-up before you
integrate, and differentiate every antiderivative you produce.

Notation: all lengths are in metres unless a problem states otherwise, $\rho g=9800\ \mathrm{N/m^3}$ for
water, and logarithms are natural. Answers to areas and volumes are exact unless an approximation is
requested.

## Beginner

**1.** *(§7.2)* Find the area of the region bounded by $y=x^2$ and $y=2x$.

**2.** *(§7.2)* Find the area of the region bounded by $y=e^x$, $y=1$, $x=0$ and $x=\ln 3$.

**3.** *(§7.4)* The region under $y=2x$ from $x=0$ to $x=3$ is revolved about the $x$-axis. Find the
volume, and check it against the volume of the cone it generates.

**4.** *(§7.5)* The region bounded by $y=x$, $y=0$ and $x=2$ is revolved about the $y$-axis. Find the
volume by shells, and check by washers.

**5.** *(§7.8)* Find the average value of $f(x)=x^2$ on $[0,3]$, and find all $c\in[0,3]$ with
$f(c)$ equal to that average.

**6.** *(§7.6)* Find the arc length of $y=\dfrac12\left(e^x+e^{-x}\right)$ from $x=0$ to $x=1$.

**7.** *(§7.9)* A spring has constant $k=50\ \mathrm{N/m}$. Find the work needed to stretch it from its
natural length to $0.2$ m beyond it.

**8.** *(§7.12)* A vertical rectangular plate is $2$ m wide and $3$ m high, with its top edge at the
surface of the water. Find the hydrostatic force on one side.

**9.** *(§7.10)* Use the centroid formulas to find the centroid of the rectangle
$0\le x\le a$, $0\le y\le b$.

**10.** *(§7.13)* A random variable has density $f(x)=3x^2$ on $[0,1]$ and $f(x)=0$ elsewhere. Verify
that $f$ is a density, and compute $\Pr\left(X\le\frac12\right)$ and $\mathbb{E}[X]$.

**11.** *(§7.14)* Demand is $p=50-2q$. At the equilibrium quantity $q^*=10$ (so $p^*=30$), find the
consumer surplus.

**12.** *(§7.7)* The graph of $y=R$ for $0\le x\le h$ is revolved about the $x$-axis. Use the
surface-area formula to find the area of the resulting cylinder.

## Intermediate

**13.** *(§7.2)* Find the geometric area of the region between $y=x^3$ and $y=x$ on $[-1,1]$. Explain why
a single integral of $x-x^3$ does not give the answer.

**14.** *(§7.3)* The base of a solid is the disk $x^2+y^2\le4$. Cross-sections perpendicular to the
$x$-axis are squares with one side in the base. Find the volume.

**15.** *(§7.4)* The region between $y=\sqrt x$ and $y=x$ on $[0,1]$ is revolved about the $x$-axis. Find
the volume.

**16.** *(§7.5)* The region bounded by $y=x^2$, $y=0$ and $x=1$ is revolved about the $y$-axis. Find the
volume by shells.

**17.** *(§7.6)* Find the arc length of $y=\ln(\sec x)$ from $x=0$ to $x=\pi/4$.

**18.** *(§7.7)* Find the area of the surface generated by revolving $y=\sqrt{x}$, $0\le x\le4$, about
the $x$-axis.

**19.** *(§7.9)* A rectangular tank with a $2$ m by $3$ m base and height $4$ m is full of water and is
emptied over its top. Find the work done.

**20.** *(§7.10)* Find the centroid of the region bounded by $y=\sqrt{x}$, $y=0$ and $x=4$.

**21.** *(§7.12)* A vertical rectangular plate is $4$ m wide and $3$ m high, with its top edge $2$ m below
the surface. Find the hydrostatic force on one side.

**22.** *(§7.14)* Demand is $D(q)=30-q^2$ and supply is $S(q)=6+\frac12q^2$. Find the equilibrium, the
consumer surplus and the producer surplus.

## Advanced

**23.** *(§7.8)* Compute the root-mean-square value of (a) $f(x)=x$ on $[0,1]$ and (b) $f(x)=\sin x$ on
$[0,\pi]$.

**24.** *(§7.11)* A uniform lamina of density $1$ occupies the region under $y=x^2$ from $x=0$ to $x=1$.
Find $I_y$ and $I_x$.

**25.** *(§7.11)* A cone is generated by revolving $y=\frac rhx$, $0\le x\le h$, about the $x$-axis,
and has uniform density $\rho$. Show that its moment of inertia about the axis is
$I=\frac{3}{10}Mr^2$, where $M$ is its mass.

**26.** *(§7.12)* A vertical triangular plate has its base of length $4$ m along the water surface and
its apex $3$ m below the surface. Find the hydrostatic force, and check the answer with the centroid
shortcut.

**27.** *(§7.13)* A random variable has density $f(x)=\frac34(1-x^2)$ on $[-1,1]$ and $0$ elsewhere.
Verify that $f$ is a density, and compute $\mathbb{E}[X]$, $\operatorname{Var}(X)$ and
$\Pr\left(\lvert X\rvert\le\frac12\right)$.

**28.** *(§7.3, §7.4)* A spherical cap is obtained by revolving $y=\sqrt{R^2-x^2}$, $a\le x\le R$, about
the $x$-axis, where $0\le a\le R$. Show that its volume is
$V=\frac{\pi}{3}(R-a)^2(2R+a)$.

**29.** *(§7.9)* An inverted conical tank has radius $2$ m at the top and height $4$ m, with its vertex
at the bottom. It is full of water and is emptied over the top. Find the work done.

**30.** *(§7.10)* Find the centroid of the region bounded by $y=\sin x$ and $y=0$ for $0\le x\le\pi$.

**31.** *(§7.5)* The region bounded by $y=\sin x$ and $y=0$ for $0\le x\le\pi$ is revolved about the
$y$-axis. Find the volume by shells.

## Challenge

**32.** *(§8.6)* Prove Pappus's first theorem for revolution about a horizontal external axis $y=k$:
if a region of area $A$ lies entirely above $y=k$ and has centroid at distance $d=\bar y-k$ from the
axis, then the volume of the solid of revolution is $V=2\pi dA$. State the hypotheses you use.

**33.** *(§7.4, §7.10)* A torus is generated by revolving the disk $(x-R)^2+y^2\le r^2$, where $R>r>0$,
about the $y$-axis. Show, first by washers and then by Pappus, that its volume is $2\pi^2Rr^2$.

**34.** *(§7.10)* A semicircular lamina occupies $x^2+y^2\le R^2$, $y\ge0$, and has area density
$\rho(y)=ky$ for a constant $k>0$. Find its mass and its centroid, and explain why $\bar y>\frac{4R}{3\pi}$
(the centroid of the uniform semicircle).

**35.** *(§7.14)* The Lorenz curve of an income distribution is $L(x)=x^n$ with $n\ge1$, where $L(x)$ is
the fraction of total income received by the poorest fraction $x$ of the population. The Gini coefficient
is $G=1-2\int_0^1L(x)\,dx$. Show that $G=\frac{n-1}{n+1}$, and compute $G$ for $n=2$ and $n=3$.

**36.** *(§7.7, §8.5)* Derive the surface-area formula for revolution about the $y$-axis,
$S=2\pi\int_a^b x\sqrt{1+f'(x)^2}\,dx$, from the lateral area of a frustum. Explain carefully why the
radius of the typical band is $x$ rather than $f(x)$, and why the limit of the frustum areas is the
integral.
