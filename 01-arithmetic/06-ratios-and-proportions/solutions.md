# Solutions: Ratios, Rates and Proportions

> [Tutorial](README.md) · [Exercises](exercises.md) · 36 worked solutions

Each solution states the strategy, shows every significant step, and checks the result. Where a problem has more than one natural method, the alternative is noted.

## Beginner (1–12)

**1.** Divide both terms by their greatest common divisor, $5$:

$$
15:25 = \frac{15}{5}:\frac{25}{5} = 3:5.
$$

Check: $15/25=0.6$ and $3/5=0.6$.

**2.** There are $18+24=42$ students.

(a) Boys to girls: $18:24$. Divide both terms by $6$:

$$
18:24=3:4.
$$

(b) Boys to all students: $18:42$. Divide both terms by $6$:

$$
18:42=3:7.
$$

(c) Girls are $24$ of the $42$ students, so the fraction is

$$
\frac{24}{42}=\frac{4}{7}.
$$

Check: $\frac{3}{7}+\frac{4}{7}=1$, so the boys' and girls' fractions of the whole account for the entire class. Note that (a) is part-to-part, while (b) and (c) are part-to-whole.

**3.** Multiply both terms by $100$ to clear the decimals:

$$
3.6:0.48 = 360:48.
$$

The greatest common divisor of $360$ and $48$ is $24$, so

$$
360:48 = 15:2.
$$

Check: $3.6/0.48 = 360/48 = 15/2 = 7.5$.

**4.** Convert to the same unit. Since $2$ h $=120$ min,

$$
50\text{ min}:2\text{ h} = 50:120.
$$

Divide both terms by $10$:

$$
50:120 = 5:12.
$$

Check: $50/120=5/12$.

**5.** Average speed is distance divided by time:

$$
\frac{275\text{ km}}{5\text{ h}} = 55\text{ km/h}.
$$

Check: $55 \cdot 5=275$ km.

**6.** Divide the total cost by the mass:

$$
\frac{15.60}{6}=2.60.
$$

The unit price is $\$2.60$ per kilogram. Check: $6 \cdot 2.60=15.60$.

**7.** Cross-multiply:

$$
20x = 5 \cdot 12 = 60, \qquad x=3.
$$

Alternatively, the scale factor from the left ratio to the right ratio is $\frac{20}{5}=4$. The right numerator must therefore be $4$ times the left numerator: $12=4x$, so $x=3$.

Check: $3/5=0.6$ and $12/20=0.6$.

**8.** Write the proportion as fractions with corresponding quantities in the same positions:

$$
\frac{4}{7}=\frac{20}{x}.
$$

Cross-multiply:

$$
4x = 7 \cdot 20 = 140, \qquad x=35.
$$

Check: $20/35 = 4/7$.

**9.** Let $s$ be the cups of sugar. Flour and sugar correspond, so

$$
\frac{5}{2}=\frac{15}{s}.
$$

Cross-multiply:

$$
5s = 2 \cdot 15 = 30, \qquad s=6.
$$

So $6$ cups of sugar are needed. Alternatively, $15$ is $3$ times $5$, so the sugar is $3$ times $2$, namely $6$.

**10.** Direct variation means $y=kx$ with $k=4.5$, so

$$
y=4.5x.
$$

When $x=8$,

$$
y=4.5 \cdot 8=36.
$$

When $y=27$,

$$
27=4.5x, \qquad x=\frac{27}{4.5}=6.
$$

Check: $y/x=36/8=4.5$ and $27/6=4.5$.

**11.** Actual length $=$ drawing length $\times$ scale factor:

$$
3.5\text{ cm} \times 200000 = 700000\text{ cm}.
$$

Convert: $700000\text{ cm}=7000\text{ m}=7\text{ km}$.

Check: $7$ km $=700000$ cm, and $700000/200000=3.5$ cm.

**12.** Multiply by the conversion factors, arranging them so that kilometres and hours cancel:

$$
72\ \frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \times \frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}} \times \frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}}
= \frac{72000}{3600}\ \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} = 20\ \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}.
$$

Check: $20$ m/s over $3600$ s is $72000$ m $=72$ km.

## Intermediate (13–22)

**13.** The parts sum to $3+4+5=12$. One part is

$$
\frac{720}{12}=60.
$$

The shares are

$$
3 \cdot 60=180, \qquad 4 \cdot 60=240, \qquad 5 \cdot 60=300.
$$

Check: $180+240+300=720$, and $180:240:300=3:4:5$ after dividing by $60$.

**14.** The ratio $5:8$ has $8-5=3$ more parts in the larger number. These $3$ parts correspond to the difference $27$, so one part is

$$
\frac{27}{3}=9.
$$

The numbers are

$$
5 \cdot 9=45, \qquad 8 \cdot 9=72.
$$

Check: $72-45=27$ and $45:72=5:8$.

**15.** "Increase in the ratio $7:5$" means multiply by $\frac{7}{5}$:

$$
60 \cdot \frac{7}{5}=84.
$$

The increase is $84-60=24$. Check: $84/60=1.4=7/5$.

**16.** The mixture has $3+5=8$ parts and a total of $24$ L, so one part is $24/8=3$ L. It contains

$$
3 \cdot 3=9\text{ L juice}, \qquad 5 \cdot 3=15\text{ L water}.
$$

Let $x$ be the litres of water added. The juice amount stays at $9$ L, while the water becomes $15+x$. The required ratio is $3:7$:

$$
\frac{9}{15+x}=\frac{3}{7}.
$$

Cross-multiply:

$$
63=3(15+x)=45+3x, \qquad 3x=18, \qquad x=6.
$$

So $6$ L of water must be added. Check: the new amounts are $9$ L juice and $21$ L water, and $9:21=3:7$.

**17.** First find the unit rate:

$$
\frac{150\text{ km}}{2.5\text{ h}}=60\text{ km/h}.
$$

At the same speed, the distance in $4.2$ h is

$$
60 \cdot 4.2=252\text{ km}.
$$

Check: $252/4.2=60$, the same speed.

**18.** For inverse variation, $xy=k$. Using the given pair,

$$
k=12 \cdot 7=84.
$$

Thus $y=84/x$. When $x=21$,

$$
y=\frac{84}{21}=4.
$$

Check: $21 \cdot 4=84=12 \cdot 7$.

**19.** Compute the unit prices:

$$
\frac{11.40}{3}=3.80\ \text{\$/kg}, \qquad \frac{18.50}{5}=3.70\ \text{\$/kg}.
$$

The $5$ kg offer is cheaper per kilogram by

$$
3.80-3.70=0.10\ \text{\$/kg}.
$$

Check: $5 \cdot 3.70=18.50$ and $3 \cdot 3.80=11.40$.

**20.** Convert the room dimensions to centimetres and divide by the scale factor $80$:

$$
\frac{480\text{ cm}}{80}=6\text{ cm}, \qquad \frac{360\text{ cm}}{80}=4.5\text{ cm}.
$$

The drawing is $6$ cm by $4.5$ cm. Check: $6 \cdot 80=480$ cm $=4.8$ m and $4.5 \cdot 80=360$ cm $=3.6$ m.

**21.** The shortest side grows from $9$ cm to $15$ cm, so the similarity ratio is

$$
k=\frac{15}{9}=\frac{5}{3}.
$$

The other sides are

$$
12 \cdot \frac{5}{3}=20\text{ cm}, \qquad 15 \cdot \frac{5}{3}=25\text{ cm}.
$$

The new perimeter is

$$
15+20+25=60\text{ cm}.
$$

Check: the original perimeter is $9+12+15=36$ cm, and $36 \cdot \frac{5}{3}=60$ cm. The side ratios are all $\frac{5}{3}$.

**22.** Use conversion factors so that grams cancel and cubic centimetres cancel:

$$
0.92\ \frac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} \times \frac{1\text{ kg}}{1000\text{ g}} \times \frac{10^6\text{ cm}^3}{1\text{ m}^3}
= \frac{0.92 \cdot 10^6}{1000}\ \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}=920\ \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}.
$$

Check: $920$ kg/m$^3$ means $920$ kg in $10^6$ cm$^3$, or $0.92$ g/cm$^3$.

## Advanced (23–31)

**23.** *Strategy:* add $1$ to both sides of the proportion, then combine each side into a single fraction.

Assume $\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}$ with $b,d\neq 0$. Add $1$ to both sides:

$$
\frac{a}{b}+1=\frac{c}{d}+1.
$$

Write each $1$ with the same denominator as the fraction beside it:

$$
\frac{a}{b}+\frac{b}{b}=\frac{c}{d}+\frac{d}{d}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\frac{a+b}{b}=\frac{c+d}{d}.
$$

For the numerical illustration, take $a=3$, $b=4$, $c=9$, $d=12$. The hypothesis holds because $\frac{3}{4}=\frac{9}{12}=0.75$. The identity gives

$$
\frac{3+4}{4}=\frac{7}{4}=1.75, \qquad \frac{9+12}{12}=\frac{21}{12}=1.75,
$$

so both sides agree. The proof uses only the hypothesis and the fact that $b,d\neq 0$; no numerical property of the particular values is used.

**24.** The rate is

$$
\frac{45\text{ km}}{4\text{ L}}=11.25\text{ km/L}, \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \frac{4\text{ L}}{45\text{ km}}=\frac{4}{45}\text{ L/km}.
$$

(a) For $180$ km,

$$
180\text{ km} \times \frac{4\text{ L}}{45\text{ km}} = \frac{720}{45}=16\text{ L}.
$$

(b) At $\$1.65$ per litre,

$$
16 \cdot 1.65 = 26.40,
$$

so the fuel costs $\$26.40$.

(c) On $10$ L,

$$
10\text{ L} \times 11.25\text{ km/L}=112.5\text{ km}.
$$

Check: $180$ km is $4$ times $45$ km, so it needs $4$ times $4$ L, namely $16$ L. Also $10$ L is $2.5$ times $4$ L, so it covers $2.5$ times $45$ km, namely $112.5$ km.

**25.** Measure the work in worker-days. The whole wall requires

$$
12 \cdot 15=180\text{ worker-days}.
$$

In the first $5$ days, the $12$ workers complete

$$
12 \cdot 5=60\text{ worker-days}.
$$

The remaining work is

$$
180-60=120\text{ worker-days}.
$$

After $3$ workers leave, $9$ workers remain. The additional time is

$$
\frac{120}{9}=\frac{40}{3}=13\frac{1}{3}\text{ days}.
$$

So $13\frac{1}{3}$ more days are needed, and the wall takes $18\frac{1}{3}$ days in total. Check: $60+9 \cdot \frac{40}{3}=60+120=180$ worker-days.

**26.** Convert each map length to an actual length using the scale factor $50000$:

$$
4\text{ cm} \times 50000 = 200000\text{ cm}=2\text{ km},
$$

$$
2.5\text{ cm} \times 50000 = 125000\text{ cm}=1.25\text{ km}.
$$

The actual area is therefore

$$
2\text{ km} \times 1.25\text{ km}=2.5\text{ km}^2.
$$

Check with the area scale: the map area is $4 \cdot 2.5=10$ cm$^2$, and

$$
10 \text{ cm}^2 \times (50000)^2 = 2.5 \times 10^{10}\text{ cm}^2 = 2.5 \times 10^6\text{ m}^2=2.5\text{ km}^2.
$$

**27.** Let $x$ be the litres of $10\%$ solution and $y$ the litres of $50\%$ solution. The total volume and the salt content give

$$
x+y=40,
$$

$$
0.10x+0.50y=0.20 \cdot 40=8.
$$

From the first equation, $y=40-x$. Substitute:

$$
0.10x+0.50(40-x)=8.
$$

Expand and simplify:

$$
0.10x+20-0.50x=8, \qquad -0.40x=-12, \qquad x=30.
$$

Then $y=40-30=10$. So $30$ L of the $10\%$ solution and $10$ L of the $50\%$ solution are needed. Check: the salt is $0.10 \cdot 30+0.50 \cdot 10=3+5=8$ L-equivalent, which is $20\%$ of $40$ L. The ratio of the two solutions is $3:1$.

**28.** (a) Let the rectangle have sides $a$ and $b$, so its area is $A=ab$. After scaling by $k>0$, the sides are $ka$ and $kb$, and the new area is

$$
A'=(ka)(kb)=k^2ab=k^2A.
$$

(b) Any polygon can be divided into triangles. Scaling by $k$ sends each triangle to a similar triangle whose base and height are both multiplied by $k$. The area of a triangle is $\frac{1}{2}bh$, so each triangle's area is multiplied by $k^2$. The polygon's area is the sum of the triangle areas, so it is also multiplied by $k^2$. For a curved figure, approximate it by polygons and take a limit; every approximating polygon's area is multiplied by the same factor $k^2$, so the limit is too. This is the standard argument; the full analytic treatment belongs to geometry.

**29.** The longer side grows from $15$ cm to $24$ cm, so the similarity ratio is

$$
k=\frac{24}{15}=\frac{8}{5}=1.6.
$$

The shorter side becomes

$$
10 \cdot 1.6=16\text{ cm}.
$$

The area is multiplied by

$$
k^2=\left(\frac{8}{5}\right)^2=\frac{64}{25}=2.56.
$$

So the area increases by a factor of $2.56$, which is a $156\%$ increase. Check: the original area is $10 \cdot 15=150$ cm$^2$; the new area is $16 \cdot 24=384$ cm$^2$; and $384/150=2.56$.

**30.** (a)

$$
1\ \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \times \frac{1000\text{ g}}{1\text{ kg}} \times \frac{1\text{ m}^3}{10^6\text{ cm}^3}
= \frac{1000}{10^6}\ \frac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}=0.001\ \frac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}.
$$

(b) Convert miles to kilometres:

$$
60\ \frac{\text{mi}}{\text{h}} \times \frac{1.609344\text{ km}}{1\text{ mi}}=96.56064\ \frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}.
$$

Then convert kilometres per hour to metres per second:

$$
96.56064\ \frac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} \times \frac{1000\text{ m}}{1\text{ km}} \times \frac{1\text{ h}}{3600\text{ s}}
= \frac{96560.64}{3600}\ \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}=26.8224\ \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}}.
$$

So $60$ mph is approximately $96.56$ km/h or $26.82$ m/s. Check: $60$ mph is a little less than $100$ km/h, and $26.82$ m/s times $3.6$ is $96.56$ km/h.

**31.** **Direct case.** Direct proportionality means $A=kB$ for some constant $k\neq 0$. If $B$ becomes $1.10B$, then

$$
A_{\text{new}}=k(1.10B)=1.10(kB)=1.10A.
$$

So $A$ increases by $10\%$. The percentage increase is independent of $k$ and of the starting value, provided the starting value is nonzero.

**Inverse case.** Inverse proportionality means $A=k/B$ with $k\neq 0$. If $B$ becomes $1.10B$, then

$$
A_{\text{new}}=\frac{k}{1.10B}=\frac{1}{1.10}\cdot\frac{k}{B}=\frac{A}{1.1}.
$$

The change is

$$
\frac{A}{1.1}-A=A\left(\frac{1}{1.1}-1\right)=A\left(\frac{1-1.1}{1.1}\right)=-\frac{0.1}{1.1}A=-\frac{1}{11}A.
$$

Thus $A$ decreases by $\frac{1}{11}\approx 9.09\%$, not by $10\%$. The asymmetry arises because a $10\%$ increase in $B$ must be undone by dividing by $1.1$, not by subtracting $10\%$ of the new value.

## Challenge (32–36)

**32.** Let the common value be

$$
k=\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}=\frac{e}{f}.
$$

Then, by the definition of a fraction,

$$
a=kb, \qquad c=kd, \qquad e=kf.
$$

Add the three equations:

$$
a+c+e=kb+kd+kf=k(b+d+f).
$$

Since $b+d+f\neq 0$, divide both sides by $b+d+f$:

$$
\frac{a+c+e}{b+d+f}=k.
$$

But $k$ is the common value of the three original fractions, so

$$
\frac{a+c+e}{b+d+f}=\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}=\frac{e}{f}.
$$

This proves the claim. The hypothesis $b+d+f\neq 0$ is essential: without it the displayed fraction is undefined. The result is a weighted mediant; it is not the arithmetic mean of the three fractions in general.

**33.** Let the common initial height be $1$ unit. The first candle burns at the rate $\frac{1}{4}$ per hour, so after $t$ hours its height is

$$
h_1(t)=1-\frac{t}{4}.
$$

The second burns at $\frac{1}{6}$ per hour, so

$$
h_2(t)=1-\frac{t}{6}.
$$

For $0<t\le 4$, both candles are still burning and $h_2(t)>h_1(t)$, because the second candle burns more slowly. The condition that one is twice the other must therefore be

$$
h_2(t)=2h_1(t).
$$

Substitute:

$$
1-\frac{t}{6}=2\left(1-\frac{t}{4}\right).
$$

Expand the right side:

$$
1-\frac{t}{6}=2-\frac{t}{2}.
$$

Subtract $1$ from both sides and add $\frac{t}{2}$:

$$
\frac{t}{2}-\frac{t}{6}=1.
$$

Since $\frac{t}{2}-\frac{t}{6}=\frac{3t}{6}-\frac{t}{6}=\frac{2t}{6}=\frac{t}{3}$,

$$
\frac{t}{3}=1, \qquad t=3.
$$

After $3$ hours,

$$
h_1(3)=1-\frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{4}, \qquad h_2(3)=1-\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2},
$$

so the second candle is exactly twice as tall as the first. The time is within the burning time of both candles, so the answer is valid. The first candle can never be twice the second, because it is always shorter while both burn.

**34.** For similar figures, perimeters scale by the linear factor $k$. An increase of $20\%$ means

$$
k=1.20=\frac{6}{5}.
$$

Areas scale by $k^2$:

$$
k^2=\left(\frac{6}{5}\right)^2=\frac{36}{25}=1.44.
$$

The new area is $1.44$ times the old area, so it has increased by

$$
1.44-1=0.44,
$$

that is, by $44\%$. Check: if the garden were a square of side $10$ m, its perimeter would be $40$ m and its area $100$ m$^2$. After a $20\%$ perimeter increase the side is $12$ m, the perimeter is $48$ m, and the area is $144$ m$^2$, a $44\%$ increase.

**35.** Initially the $20$ L mixture has $3+1=4$ parts, so one part is $20/4=5$ L. Thus there are

$$
15\text{ L milk}, \qquad 5\text{ L water}.
$$

Because the mixture is uniform, removing $x$ litres removes milk and water in the same $3:1$ ratio:

$$
\text{milk removed}=\frac{3x}{4}, \qquad \text{water removed}=\frac{x}{4}.
$$

After removal, the amounts are

$$
15-\frac{3x}{4}\text{ L milk}, \qquad 5-\frac{x}{4}\text{ L water}.
$$

Adding $x$ litres of water changes only the water:

$$
5-\frac{x}{4}+x=5+\frac{3x}{4}.
$$

The new ratio is $3:2$, so

$$
\frac{15-\frac{3x}{4}}{5+\frac{3x}{4}}=\frac{3}{2}.
$$

Cross-multiply:

$$
2\left(15-\frac{3x}{4}\right)=3\left(5+\frac{3x}{4}\right).
$$

Expand:

$$
30-\frac{3x}{2}=15+\frac{9x}{4}.
$$

Subtract $15$ from both sides and add $\frac{3x}{2}$:

$$
15=\frac{9x}{4}+\frac{3x}{2}=\frac{9x}{4}+\frac{6x}{4}=\frac{15x}{4}.
$$

Therefore

$$
x=4.
$$

So $4$ L of the mixture were removed and replaced with $4$ L of water. Check: removing $4$ L removes $3$ L milk and $1$ L water, leaving $12$ L milk and $4$ L water; adding $4$ L water gives $12$ L milk and $8$ L water, and $12:8=3:2$.

**36.** The statement "three cats catch three mice in three minutes" gives a *rate per cat*. The three cats together catch $3$ mice in $3$ minutes, which is $1$ mouse per minute for the group, or

$$
\frac{1}{3}\text{ mouse per minute per cat}.
$$

In $100$ minutes, one cat catches

$$
100 \cdot \frac{1}{3}=\frac{100}{3}\text{ mice}.
$$

To catch $100$ mice, the number of cats required is

$$
\frac{100}{100/3}=3.
$$

So $3$ cats are needed. The tempting answer $100$ confuses the number of mice with the number of cats; the rate is proportional to the number of cats, and each cat simply works for a longer time. Check: $3$ cats at $\frac{1}{3}$ mouse per minute each have a combined rate of $1$ mouse per minute, so in $100$ minutes they catch $100$ mice.
