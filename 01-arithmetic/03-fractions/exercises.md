# Fractions — Exercises

> Companion to [the tutorial](README.md). Attempt every problem before opening
> [the solutions](solutions.md).

Thirty-six problems, numbered continuously and grouped by difficulty. Problems marked with a star
($\star$) are proofs or extended investigations.

## Beginner

**1.** Write a fraction in lowest terms for each situation.
(a) A chocolate bar is divided into $12$ equal squares and $9$ of them have been eaten. What fraction
has been eaten?
(b) A strip is divided into $8$ equal parts and $5$ are shaded. What fraction is shaded?

**2.** Rewrite each fraction with denominator $24$: (a) $\frac{3}{4}$; (b) $\frac{5}{6}$;
(c) $\frac{7}{8}$.

**3.** Simplify to lowest terms: (a) $\frac{12}{18}$; (b) $\frac{25}{40}$; (c) $\frac{36}{60}$;
(d) $\frac{17}{51}$.

**4.** Compare, using a common denominator: (a) $\frac{3}{5}$ and $\frac{5}{8}$; (b) $\frac{7}{12}$
and $\frac{4}{7}$. State which is larger in each case.

**5.** Convert: (a) $2\frac{3}{8}$ to an improper fraction; (b) $\frac{19}{6}$ to a mixed number.

**6.** Compute: (a) $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{8}$; (b) $\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{6}$;
(c) $\frac{7}{9} + \frac{2}{9}$.

**7.** Compute: (a) $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}$; (b) $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{6}$;
(c) $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{3}$.

**8.** Compute: (a) $\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{5}$; (b) $\frac{4}{7}\cdot\frac{7}{8}$;
(c) $\frac{5}{6}\cdot 12$.

**9.** Compute: (a) $\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{2}$; (b) $\frac{2}{5} \div \frac{3}{7}$;
(c) $6 \div \frac{2}{3}$.

**10.** Which is larger, $\frac{5}{7}$ or $\frac{5}{9}$? Explain without computing a common
denominator.

**11.** A class has $24$ students and $9$ of them wear glasses. What fraction of the class wears
glasses? Give the answer in lowest terms.

**12.** A recipe needs $\frac{3}{4}$ cup of milk for the batter and $\frac{2}{3}$ cup for the glaze.
How much milk is needed in total? Give the answer as a mixed number.

**13.** Five identical pizzas are shared equally among four people. How much pizza does each person
receive? Give the answer as a mixed number.

## Intermediate

**14.** Simplify $\frac{105}{147}$ by computing the greatest common divisor with the Euclidean
algorithm.

**15.** Use the Euclidean algorithm to compute $\gcd(1071, 462)$, then simplify $\frac{462}{1071}$.

**16.** Compute $3\frac{1}{4} + 2\frac{5}{6}$. Give the answer as a mixed number.

**17.** Compute $5\frac{1}{3} - 2\frac{3}{4}$. Give the answer as a mixed number.

**18.** Compute $\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{9}{10}\cdot\frac{5}{6}$.

**19.** Compute $\frac{3}{8} \div \frac{9}{16}$.

**20.** Compute

$$
\left(\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{3}\right) \div \left(\dfrac{1}{2} - \dfrac{1}{3}\right).
$$

**21.** Compute $1 - \left(\frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{4}\right)$.

**22.** Arrange $\frac{3}{4}$, $\frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{7}{12}$, $\frac{5}{6}$ in increasing order.

**23.** A recipe that serves $6$ people uses $1\frac{1}{2}$ cups of sugar. How much sugar is needed
for $10$ people?

**24.** In a class of $32$ students, $\frac{3}{4}$ walk to school. How many students walk, and how
many do not?

**25.** A pipe fills $\frac{1}{6}$ of a tank every minute. How long does it take to fill
$\frac{2}{3}$ of the tank?

## Advanced

**26.** Using Definition 5.3 (equality of fractions as a cross-product), prove that
$\frac{a}{b} = \frac{ak}{bk}$ for every integer $k \neq 0$.

**27.** Let $\frac{a}{b} < \frac{c}{d}$ with $b > 0$ and $d > 0$. Prove that

$$
\dfrac{a}{b} < \dfrac{a+c}{b+d} < \dfrac{c}{d}.
$$

**28.** Using the characterization "$\frac{a}{b}$ is the unique number $x$ with $bx = a$", prove that

$$
\dfrac{a}{b} + \dfrac{c}{b} = \dfrac{a+c}{b}
$$

for $b \neq 0$. Explain where uniqueness is used.

**29.** $\star$ Prove that $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b}\cdot\frac{d}{c}$ by the
"multiply by the reciprocal" argument. State precisely why the hypothesis $c \neq 0$ is needed, and
say what goes wrong if $c = 0$.

**30.** (a) Prove that $\frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1} = \frac{1}{n(n+1)}$ for every integer $n \ge 1$.
(b) Use part (a) to compute

$$
\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{12} + \dfrac{1}{20} + \dfrac{1}{30}.
$$

**31.** $\star$ Prove that between any two distinct fractions there are infinitely many distinct
fractions. (Construct them explicitly and show they are all different.)

**32.** Express $\frac{5}{6}$ as a sum of distinct unit fractions in two different ways. Verify both
identities.

**33.** A farmer plants $\frac{3}{8}$ of a field with wheat and $\frac{1}{3}$ with barley, leaving the
rest for oats. (a) What fraction of the field is planted with oats? (b) If the field is $48$ hectares,
how many hectares are used for oats?

## Challenge

**34.** Prove that if $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{c}{d}$, with $b > 0$, $d > 0$, $\gcd(|a|, b) = 1$ and
$\gcd(|c|, d) = 1$, then $a = c$ and $b = d$. (This is the uniqueness of lowest terms.) You may use
Euclid's lemma: if $\gcd(m,n) = 1$ and $m \mid nk$, then $m \mid k$.

**35.** Prove that there is no rational number $x$ with $x^2 = 2$. Structure the argument as follows:
suppose $x = \frac{a}{b}$ in lowest terms with $b > 0$, derive $a^2 = 2b^2$, show that $a$ and $b$ are
both even, and obtain a contradiction with lowest terms.

**36.** $\star$ (a) Apply the greedy algorithm for Egyptian fractions to $\frac{5}{7}$: repeatedly
subtract the largest unit fraction not exceeding the current remainder. Show the resulting
decomposition.

(b) Find every fraction with denominator $60$ that lies strictly between $\frac{1}{3}$ and
$\frac{2}{5}$, and give each in lowest terms.
