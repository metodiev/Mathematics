# Exercises: Exponents and Roots

> [Tutorial](README.md) · 36 problems · Beginner → Challenge · Full solutions in [solutions.md](solutions.md)

Show every step. For proof problems, a numerical example is not a proof; state the hypothesis, the assumption for contradiction (if used), and the conclusion.

## Beginner (1–12)

**1.** Evaluate: (a) $3^4$, (b) $7^0$, (c) $2^{-4}$, (d) $(-5)^3$.

**2.** Evaluate: (a) $(-4)^2$, (b) $-4^2$, (c) $\left(\dfrac35\right)^2$, (d) $\left(-\dfrac23\right)^{-2}$.

**3.** Simplify, stating any restriction on the variables: (a) $x^5x^7$, (b) $\dfrac{y^9}{y^4}$, (c) $\left(z^3\right)^4$.

**4.** Simplify: (a) $\left(3x^2\right)^2$, (b) $\left(a^3b\right)^2$, (c) $\left(2x^3y^{-2}\right)^2$.

**5.** Write each expression with positive exponents only: (a) $a^{-3}$, (b) $\dfrac{1}{x^{-5}}$, (c) $(2x)^{-2}$.

**6.** Evaluate: (a) $\sqrt{64}$, (b) $-\sqrt{64}$, (c) $\sqrt0$, (d) $\sqrt[3]{-64}$, (e) $\sqrt{\dfrac19}$.

**7.** For each of $64$, $81$, $100$, and $125$, state whether it is a perfect square, a perfect cube, both, or neither.

**8.** Simplify: (a) $\sqrt{36}\cdot\sqrt4$, (b) $\sqrt{36\cdot4}$, (c) $\sqrt{\dfrac{9}{16}}$.

**9.** Simplify: (a) $\sqrt{75}$, (b) $\sqrt{18}$, (c) $\sqrt[3]{24}$.

**10.** Write in scientific notation: (a) $72{,}500$, (b) $0.000058$. Write in decimal form: (c) $8.4\times10^2$.

**11.** Compute and give the answer in scientific notation: (a) $(2\times10^3)(4\times10^5)$, (b) $\dfrac{6\times10^7}{2\times10^4}$.

**12.** Between which two consecutive integers does each number lie? (a) $\sqrt{30}$, (b) $\sqrt{200}$.

## Intermediate (13–22)

**13.** Simplify $\left(x^3y^2\right)^{-2}\left(xy^3\right)^4$.

**14.** Simplify $\dfrac{a^{1/2}a^{1/3}}{a^{1/6}}$.

**15.** Evaluate: (a) $81^{3/4}$, (b) $125^{-2/3}$, (c) $\left(\dfrac{9}{16}\right)^{3/2}$.

**16.** Simplify $\sqrt{72x^4y^7}$, assuming $x\ge0$ and $y\ge0$.

**17.** Rationalize the denominator: (a) $\dfrac{5}{\sqrt5}$, (b) $\dfrac{8}{3\sqrt2}$, (c) $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3-1}$.

**18.** Simplify $\sqrt{\dfrac27}$ by rationalizing the denominator.

**19.** Compute and give the answer in scientific notation: (a) $\left(4.5\times10^{-3}\right)\left(2\times10^6\right)$, (b) $\dfrac{7.2\times10^5}{2.4\times10^{-2}}$.

**20.** Order from smallest to largest: $1.2\times10^6$, $8\times10^5$, $9\times10^5$, $1.05\times10^6$.

**21.** Solve for $x$: (a) $2^x=2^7$, (b) $\left(3^2\right)^x=3^8$, (c) $5^x=\dfrac{1}{25}$.

**22.** Simplify $\left(8x^3\right)^{-2/3}$.

## Advanced (23–31)

**23.** Prove the quotient law: for $a\neq0$ and $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$ with $m>n$,

$$
\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}.
$$

Then explain what the law gives when $m=n$ and when $m<n$.

**24.** Prove both identities for $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$ and $m,n\in\mathbb{N}$:

$$
(ab)^n=a^n b^n, \qquad \left(a^m\right)^n=a^{mn}.
$$

**25.** Prove that $\sqrt2$ is irrational.

**26.** Prove that $\sqrt3$ is irrational, adapting the argument for $\sqrt2$.

**27.** Simplify $\left(64x^{-6}y^9\right)^{-1/3}$.

**28.** Simplify $\sqrt[3]{16x^5y^8}$.

**29.** Estimate $\sqrt2$ to three decimal places using Newton's method with $x_0=1.5$. Show at least three iterates and state the final approximation.

**30.** A light-year is about $9.46\times10^{12}$ km. A star is $4.2$ light-years away. (a) Express the distance in kilometres in scientific notation. (b) At a speed of $1.0\times10^5$ km/h, how many years would the journey take? Use $1$ year $\approx8766$ hours.

**31.** Solve for real $x$: (a) $\sqrt{x+7}=5$, (b) $x=\sqrt{x+6}$. Check for extraneous solutions.

## Challenge (32–36)

**32.** Prove that $\sqrt2+\sqrt3$ is irrational. (Hint: suppose it is rational, square it, and use the irrationality of $\sqrt6$.)

**33.** Solve $2^{2x}-5\cdot2^x+4=0$ for real $x$.

**34.** Prove that $\sqrt[3]{2}$ is irrational.

**35.** Which is larger, $\sqrt7+\sqrt{10}$ or $\sqrt3+\sqrt{14}$? Prove your answer without using decimal approximations.

**36.** Prove: if $n\ge2$ is an integer and $2^n-1$ is prime, then $n$ is prime.
