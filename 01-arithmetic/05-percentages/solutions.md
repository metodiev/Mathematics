## Beginner

### **1.**

A percentage is a fraction with denominator $100$:

$$
45\%=\frac{45}{100}=\frac{9}{20}.
$$

As a decimal, divide by $100$ by moving the decimal point two places left:

$$
45\%=0.45.
$$

### **2.**

Convert the fraction to a decimal by division:

$$
\frac{5}{8}=0.625.
$$

To convert to a percentage, multiply by $100\%$:

$$
0.625\cdot100\%=62.5\%.
$$

Equivalently, $\tfrac{5}{8}=\tfrac{625}{1000}=62.5\%$.

### **3.**

$$
7\%=\frac{7}{100}=0.07.
$$

The fraction $\tfrac{7}{100}$ is already in lowest terms because $7$ is prime and does not divide
$100$.

### **4.**

The discount is $25\%$ of $\$84$:

$$
0.25\cdot84=21.
$$

The sale price is the original price minus the discount:

$$
84-21=63.
$$

Equivalently, the sale price is $75\%$ of the original:
$0.75\cdot84=63$. The discount is $\$21$ and the sale price is $\$63$.

### **5.**

The tip is $18\%$ of $\$52$:

$$
0.18\cdot52=9.36.
$$

The total is the bill plus the tip:

$$
52+9.36=61.36.
$$

The tip is $\$9.36$ and the total paid is $\$61.36$.

### **6.**

Divide the part by the whole and multiply by $100\%$:

$$
\frac{20}{80}\cdot100\%=0.25\cdot100\%=25\%.
$$

So $25\%$ of respondents prefer tea.

### **7.**

Here the part is $18$ and the rate is $30\%=0.30$. The whole is unknown:

$$
W=\frac{P}{r}=\frac{18}{0.30}=60.
$$

The class has $60$ students. As a check, $30\%$ of $60$ is $0.30\cdot60=18$.

### **8.**

An increase of $20\%$ multiplies the fare by $1.20$:

$$
90(1+0.20)=90\cdot1.20=108.
$$

The new fare is $\$108$.

### **9.**

A decrease of $15\%$ multiplies the price by $1-0.15=0.85$:

$$
180\cdot0.85=153.
$$

The sale price is $\$153$.

### **10.**

For $0.006$, multiply by $100\%$:

$$
0.006\cdot100\%=0.6\%.
$$

For $150\%$, divide by $100$:

$$
150\%=1.5.
$$

So $0.006=0.6\%$, and $150\%=1.5$.

### **11.**

$$
\frac{42}{50}\cdot100\%=0.84\cdot100\%=84\%.
$$

The student answered $84\%$ of the questions correctly.

### **12.**

The rise is $4\%$ of $\$18.50$:

$$
0.04\cdot18.50=0.74.
$$

The new wage is

$$
18.50+0.74=19.24.
$$

Equivalently, $18.50(1.04)=19.24$. The new hourly wage is $\$19.24$.

## Intermediate

### **13.**

Multiply by $1+0.08=1.08$:

$$
45\cdot1.08=48.60.
$$

The new price is $\$48.60$.

### **14.**

Let $W$ be the original price. After a $30\%$ discount, the price paid is $70\%$ of $W$:

$$
0.70W=84.
$$

Hence

$$
W=\frac{84}{0.70}=120.
$$

The original price was $\$120$. As a check, $30\%$ of $\$120$ is $\$36$, and $120-36=84$.

### **15.**

The first year multiplies by $1.04$; the second multiplies by $1.03$. The combined factor is

$$
1.04\cdot1.03=1.0712.
$$

The net growth is $1.0712-1=0.0712=7.12\%$. It is not $4\%+3\%=7\%$, because the second year's
$3\%$ applies to the already increased population.

### **16.**

An $8\%$ rise multiplies by $1.08$, and an $8\%$ fall multiplies by $0.92$. The net factor is

$$
1.08\cdot0.92=0.9936,
$$

a net change of $0.9936-1=-0.0064=-0.64\%$. The salary ends $0.64\%$ below where it started.

### **17.**

After a $25\%$ discount the price factor is $0.75$; after a further $12\%$ discount it is $0.88$.
The combined factor is

$$
0.75\cdot0.88=0.66.
$$

The single equivalent discount is

$$
1-0.66=0.34=34\%.
$$

The discounts do not add to $37\%$ because the second discount applies to the already reduced price.

### **18.**

Marked price after a $30\%$ markup on $\$80$:

$$
80(1+0.30)=80\cdot1.30=104.
$$

Sale price after a $20\%$ discount on $\$104$:

$$
104(1-0.20)=104\cdot0.80=83.20.
$$

Profit:

$$
83.20-80=3.20.
$$

As a percentage of cost:

$$
\frac{3.20}{80}=0.04=4\%.
$$

The selling price is $\$83.20$, and the profit is $4\%$ of cost.

### **19.**

Sales tax:

$$
0.075\cdot180=13.50.
$$

Total:

$$
180+13.50=193.50.
$$

Equivalently, $180(1.075)=193.50$. The total cost is $\$193.50$.

### **20.**

The change in percentage points is the arithmetic difference:

$$
5.4-4.5=0.9\ \text{pp}.
$$

The relative change uses the original rate as the base:

$$
\frac{5.4-4.5}{4.5}\cdot100\%=\frac{0.9}{4.5}\cdot100\%=0.2\cdot100\%=20\%.
$$

The rate rose by $0.9$ percentage points, which is a $20\%$ relative increase.

### **21.**

Simple interest:

$$
I=P_{0}rt=2000\cdot0.035\cdot4=280.
$$

Final amount:

$$
A=P_{0}(1+rt)=2000(1+0.035\cdot4)=2000\cdot1.14=2280.
$$

The interest is $\$280$, and the final amount is $\$2{,}280$.

### **22.**

Compound interest for $4$ years:

$$
A=2000(1.035)^{4}.
$$

Compute the power:

$$
1.035^{2}=1.071225,
\qquad
1.035^{4}=(1.071225)^{2}=1.147523000625.
$$

Thus

$$
A=2000\cdot1.147523000625=2295.04600125.
$$

Rounded to the nearest cent, the final amount is $\$2{,}295.05$.

### **23.**

Let the original price be $W$. After a $25\%$ increase the price is $1.25W$. A decrease of rate $d$
must return to $W$:

$$
1.25W(1-d)=W.
$$

Divide by $W>0$:

$$
1.25(1-d)=1,\qquad 1-d=\frac{1}{1.25}=0.8.
$$

Hence $d=0.2=20\%$. The decrease must be $20\%$, not $25\%$, because it is computed on the larger
increased price.

### **24.**

The price including $20\%$ VAT is $1.20$ times the pre-tax price:

$$
1.20W=186.
$$

Hence

$$
W=\frac{186}{1.20}=155.
$$

The price before tax is $\$155$. The tax is $\$31$, which is $20\%$ of $\$155$.

## Advanced

### **25.**

After an increase of $p\%$, the multiplier is $1+\dfrac{p}{100}$. After the decrease of $p\%$, the
second multiplier is $1-\dfrac{p}{100}$. The net factor is

$$
\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)\left(1-\frac{p}{100}\right)
=1-\frac{p^{2}}{10000}.
$$

The net rate is $-\dfrac{p^{2}}{10000}$, so the net percentage change is

$$
-\frac{p^{2}}{10000}\cdot100\%=-\frac{p^{2}}{100}\%.
$$

For $p=15$,

$$
-\frac{15^{2}}{100}=-\frac{225}{100}=-2.25\%.
$$

The quantity ends $2.25\%$ below its starting value.

### **26.**

The combined multiplier is

$$
(1+0.10)(1+0.20)=1.10\cdot1.20=1.32.
$$

If the original value is $W$, then

$$
1.32W=132,
\qquad
W=\frac{132}{1.32}=100.
$$

The original value was $100$.

### **27.**

The absolute error is

$$
|12.4-12.5|=0.1.
$$

The percent error uses the true value as the base:

$$
\frac{0.1}{12.5}\cdot100\%=0.008\cdot100\%=0.8\%.
$$

The percent error is $0.8\%$.

### **28.**

The amount of salt in the original solution is $15\%$ of $200$ mL. With density $1$ g/mL, the
solution has mass $200$ g, so the salt mass is

$$
0.15\cdot200=30\ \text{g}.
$$

Adding $50$ mL of water adds $50$ g of mass and no salt, so the new total mass is

$$
200+50=250\ \text{g}.
$$

The new mass percentage is

$$
\frac{30}{250}=0.12=12\%.
$$

The concentration is $12\%$. This assumes the volumes add and the density of the solution is
$1$ g/mL; in a real solution, volume contraction and density changes would need to be measured.

### **29.**

We prove Bernoulli's inequality $(1+r)^{t}\ge1+rt$ for integers $t\ge0$ by induction, then note
that the inequality is strict for $r>0$ and $t>1$.

For $t=0$, both sides equal $1$. Suppose the inequality holds for some $t\ge0$:

$$
(1+r)^{t}\ge1+rt.
$$

Since $r>0$, we have $1+r>0$, so multiplying by $1+r$ preserves the inequality:

$$
(1+r)^{t+1}\ge(1+rt)(1+r)=1+(t+1)r+tr^{2}.
$$

Because $tr^{2}\ge0$,

$$
1+(t+1)r+tr^{2}\ge1+(t+1)r.
$$

Thus $(1+r)^{t+1}\ge1+(t+1)r$, completing the induction. If $r>0$ and $t\ge1$, then $tr^{2}>0$, so
the inequality is strict for $t+1\ge2$. In particular, for every integer $t>1$,
$(1+r)^{t}>1+rt$.

For interest, $P_{0}(1+r)^{t}$ is the compound amount and $P_{0}(1+rt)$ is the simple amount. The
inequality says that with a positive rate, compound interest exceeds simple interest after more than
one year, because interest earns interest.

### **30.**

The first group has $40$ students and a pass rate of $75\%$, so the number of passing students is
$0.75\cdot40=30$. The second group has $10$ students and a pass rate of $90\%$, so the number of
passing students is $0.90\cdot10=9$. The total number of passing students is

$$
30+9=39,
$$

and the total number of students is $40+10=50$. The overall pass rate is

$$
\frac{39}{50}=0.78=78\%.
$$

The unweighted average would be $82.5\%$, but that ignores the different group sizes.

### **31.**

The nominal annual rate is $5\%=0.05$, and quarterly compounding means $n=4$ periods per year, each
with rate $0.05/4=0.0125$. The effective annual rate is

$$
\left(1+\frac{0.05}{4}\right)^{4}-1=(1.0125)^{4}-1.
$$

Compute the power:

$$
(1.0125)^{4}=1.050945336914\ldots
$$

so

$$
r_{\text{eff}}=0.050945336914\ldots=5.0945336914\ldots\%.
$$

Correct to four decimal places, the effective annual rate is $5.0945\%$.

## Challenge

### **32.**

$A$ is $20\%$ of $B$:

$$
A=0.20B.
$$

$B$ is $30\%$ of $C$:

$$
B=0.30C.
$$

Substitute the second equation into the first:

$$
A=0.20(0.30C)=0.06C.
$$

Thus $A$ is $6\%$ of $C$.

In general, $p\%$ of $q\%$ of a whole $W$ is

$$
\frac{p}{100}\left(\frac{q}{100}W\right)=\frac{pq}{10000}W.
$$

As a percentage of $W$, this is

$$
\frac{pq}{10000}\cdot100\%=\frac{pq}{100}\%.
$$

For $p=20$ and $q=30$, $\dfrac{20\cdot30}{100}=6$, confirming the result.

### **33.**

Let the original value be $W$. After a decrease of $p\%$, the value is

$$
W\left(1-\frac{p}{100}\right).
$$

An increase of $q\%$ multiplies this by $1+\dfrac{q}{100}$. To restore $W$, we need

$$
W\left(1-\frac{p}{100}\right)\left(1+\frac{q}{100}\right)=W.
$$

Divide by $W>0$:

$$
\left(1-\frac{p}{100}\right)\left(1+\frac{q}{100}\right)=1.
$$

Solve for $q$:

$$
1+\frac{q}{100}=\frac{1}{1-p/100}=\frac{100}{100-p},
$$

so

$$
\frac{q}{100}=\frac{100}{100-p}-1=\frac{p}{100-p},
\qquad
q=\frac{100p}{100-p}.
$$

For $p=20$,

$$
q=\frac{100\cdot20}{80}=25\%.
$$

For $p=50$,

$$
q=\frac{100\cdot50}{50}=100\%.
$$

A $50\%$ decrease must be undone by a $100\%$ increase, because the remaining half must be doubled.

### **34.**

The rule of $72$ estimates the doubling time as

$$
t\approx\frac{72}{8}=9\ \text{years}.
$$

The exact doubling time solves $(1.08)^{t}=2$:

$$
t=\frac{\ln 2}{\ln 1.08}.
$$

Using $\ln 2\approx0.693147$ and $\ln 1.08\approx0.076961$,

$$
t\approx\frac{0.693147}{0.076961}\approx9.0065\ \text{years}.
$$

The rule of $72$ gives $9$ years, which is about $0.0065$ years (roughly $2.4$ days) short of the
exact value.

The rule is approximate because, for small $r$, $\ln(1+r)\approx r$, so

$$
t=\frac{\ln2}{\ln(1+r)}\approx\frac{0.693147}{r}.
$$

Multiplying numerator and denominator by $100$ gives approximately $69.3/r$ when $r$ is written as
a percentage. The number $72$ is used instead of $69.3$ because it is divisible by many common
rates ($1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12$) and because it compensates for the second-order term in
$\ln(1+r)$ over the range of rates where the rule is used. It is a mental approximation, not an
identity.

### **35.**

First, $A$ is $20\%$ more than $B$:

$$
A=1.20B.
$$

Then

$$
\frac{B}{A}=\frac{1}{1.20}=0.8333\ldots,
$$

so $B$ is $16\tfrac{2}{3}\%$ less than $A$.

Second, $C$ is $20\%$ less than $D$:

$$
C=0.80D.
$$

Then

$$
\frac{D}{C}=\frac{1}{0.80}=1.25,
$$

so $D$ is $25\%$ more than $C$.

In general, if $A=\left(1+\dfrac{p}{100}\right)B$, then

$$
\frac{B}{A}=\frac{1}{1+p/100}=\frac{100}{100+p},
$$

so $B$ is

$$
\left(1-\frac{100}{100+p}\right)\cdot100\%=\frac{100p}{100+p}\%
$$

less than $A$. If $C=\left(1-\dfrac{p}{100}\right)D$, then

$$
\frac{D}{C}=\frac{1}{1-p/100}=\frac{100}{100-p},
$$

so $D$ is

$$
\left(\frac{100}{100-p}-1\right)\cdot100\%=\frac{100p}{100-p}\%
$$

more than $C$. The answers differ because the base changes: the first percentage compares $B$ with
$A$, while the second compares $D$ with $C$. A $25\%$ change in one direction is not undone by a
$25\%$ change in the other.

### **36.**

Store A applies $25\%$ off, leaving $75\%$ of the original price, then $10\%$ off that reduced
price, leaving $90\%$ of $75\%$:

$$
0.75\cdot0.90=0.675.
$$

So Store A's final price is $67.5\%$ of the original, a $32.5\%$ discount.

Store B's final price is $65\%$ of the original, a $35\%$ discount. Therefore Store B is cheaper.

The difference in final price is

$$
0.675-0.65=0.025,
$$

which is $2.5\%$ of the original price, or $2.5$ percentage points of discount. Store A's discounts
do not add to $35\%$ because the second $10\%$ applies to the already discounted price. In
multiplier form,

$$
(1-0.25)(1-0.10)=1-0.25-0.10+0.25\cdot0.10=0.675,
$$

so the equivalent single discount is $32.5\%$, not $35\%$.
